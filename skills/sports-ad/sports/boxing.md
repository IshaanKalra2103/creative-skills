# Boxing

What a boxing ad has to get right, and what image and video models get wrong. Shot names
refer to `references/shots.md`; transitions to `references/transitions.md`.

## Movements

Each movement: the phases in order, how long they take at real speed (30 fps), the body cues
that make it read as real, the frame where the product shows best, and how to shoot it.
Frame counts are approximate (approx.) unless a source or measured figure is named beside them.

### Stance and guard

- Orthodox stance: left foot forward, right foot back, feet a little wider than the shoulders,
  at an angle of about 45 degrees to the opponent. The rear heel lifted, the weight on the balls
  of the feet, the knees soft, the hips and shoulders turned about 45 degrees. The hands high:
  the lead hand at about the cheekbone or chin level, the rear hand against the chin or cheek,
  the elbows in and down to protect the ribs, the chin tucked behind the lead shoulder, the eyes
  looking over the lead glove. Southpaw is the mirror.
- The stance bounces lightly: a small rhythmic bounce every 8-12 frames.
- Tells: feet in line like a fencer's, the hands at the waist, the chin up, both feet flat,
  the shoulders square to the camera, the guard with the palms flat toward the face.

### Jab

| phase | frames at 30 fps | body |
|---|---|---|
| start | 1-2 | A small shoulder shrug and a push from the rear foot; the lead hand leaves the guard. |
| extension | 2-3 | The lead arm extends straight, the fist turns palm down at the end; the lead shoulder rises to the chin. |
| contact | 1 | Knuckles of the first two fingers land, the shoulder covers the chin, the rear hand stays at the chin. |
| retraction | 3-4 | The arm returns along the same line to the guard, faster than it went out. |

- A jab takes about 0.1-0.15 s to land (3-4 frames, approx.) and the full out-and-back takes 6-9 frames (approx.).
  The lead foot slides forward a few centimetres with the punch.
- Hero frame: the full extension, the glove's front facing camera in a low three-quarter
  view, or the rear hand at the chin.
- Tells: a slow windup pulled back before the punch ("telegraphed"), the rear hand dropped,
  the fist rotating before it leaves the guard, the body leaning forward with the head over
  the lead foot.

### Cross (straight right)

| phase | frames at 30 fps | body |
|---|---|---|
| load | 2 | Weight to the rear leg, the rear heel pushes the floor. |
| rotation | 2-3 | The rear foot pivots, the hip and shoulder rotate toward the opponent, the arm extends straight. |
| contact | 1 | The fist lands with the palm down, the chin tucked behind the shoulder, the lead hand protects the face. |
| recovery | 4-5 | The hips unwind, the arm returns, the weight returns to centre. |

- The power travels from the floor through the leg, the hip and the shoulder: the rear heel
  turns, the knee points inward, the torso rotates. About 8-10 frames in total.
- Hero frame: the contact with the hip rotated fully, the rear heel turned up, in profile.
- Tells: an arm punch only, with the torso still; both feet planted; the rear heel flat; the
  punch thrown from outside the guard; the chin lifted.

### Lead hook

- The lead elbow raised to shoulder height and bent at about 90 degrees, the fist travels in a
  horizontal arc, the lead foot pivots on its ball and the hips and shoulders rotate; the thumb
  faces down or in, the rear hand stays at the chin. 3-5 frames for the arc, 7-10 in total with
  recovery.
- Hero frame: the end of the arc, the fist beside the chin line with the body twisted.
- Tells: a swing with a straight arm like a haymaker, the elbow below the fist, the rear hand
  dropped, no pivot on the lead foot.

### Uppercut

- The knees dip, the shoulder drops on the punching side, then the legs drive up, the hip
  rotates and the fist travels up in a short vertical arc, the palm facing the face; the elbow
  at the side and bent, then extended to about 90-120 degrees. 3-5 frames for the rise.
- Tells: the fist swung from the waist with the arm straight, the knees straight, the palm down.

### Slip and roll (defence)

- Slip: as the punch comes, the head moves a hand width to the outside of the punch (to the
  side of the jab or cross), with a small knee bend and a torso rotation; the eyes stay on the
  opponent. 3-5 frames out, 3-5 frames back.
- Roll (bob and weave): the knees bend and the torso dips in a U-shaped arc under a hook, the
  head traces the arc, the hands stay up; 6-10 frames in total.
- Tells: ducking with the head down and the eyes closed, a straight vertical dip with the
  back bent, the hands dropping, turning away.

### Footwork

- Step-drag: the lead foot steps first, the rear foot follows, never crossing; the feet keep
  their distance. A pivot: the lead foot stays and the rear foot arcs round. Each step is a
  short slide of 20-40 cm taking 4-6 frames. A boxer keeps moving: a rhythm of shuffle and
  bounce.
- Hero frame for a shoe: the lead foot's ball-of-the-foot pivot on the canvas, the shoe's
  sole and the heel lifted, in profile at floor level.
- Tells: crossed feet, flat-footed flat steps, a hop like a dancer, the heel down.

### Skipping rope

- The rope is turned with the wrists, not the arms; the elbows at the sides, the hands at the
  hip height and about 20 cm out; a light jump of 2-3 cm on the balls of the feet, the heels
  not touching down; the body upright and relaxed. A steady beat of about 2 skips a second
  (a skip every 12-18 frames) at a basic pace; boxers do a fast shuffle and double unders.
- The rope is a blur at 30 fps, and a thin line at a high shutter; the rope should travel
  over the head and under the feet. Hero frame: the rope passing under the shoes, the shoes
  just off the ground.
- Tells: the rope turned with the arms swinging, high jumps with bent knees, the rope not
  touching the ground and passing through the feet, the rope ending in the air.

### Heavy bag

- The bag hangs from a chain at head height and swings on the hit: a jab makes it shift a few
  centimetres, a cross or a hook makes it swing 10-30 cm and sets the chains rattling; the
  bag's surface dents at the glove. Combinations: 1-2 (jab-cross) in 6-8 frames, 1-2-3 (with a
  hook) in 10-14 frames. The boxer's feet stay close to the bag, the head moves, then he steps
  out and circles.
- Hero frame: the glove on the bag with a puff of dust or sweat, the bag's surface dented.
- Tells: the bag staying still when hit, the bag swinging toward the boxer, the boxer's hands
  hanging, the glove passing through the bag.

### Hand wraps

- A hand wrap (about 4.5 m / 180 in long for adults) goes round the wrist first, then the
  thumb loop, the palm, the knuckles (several layers), between the fingers, then the wrist
  again, and ends with the velcro. A quiet product beat, 10-20 s real time, with a macro on the
  hands.
- Tells: a wrap that is a bandage tied with no pattern, the fingers uncovered at the knuckles
  in the wrong place, six fingers, the velcro missing.

## Props and setting

- The ring: a square platform 4.9-6.1 m (16-20 ft) on a side measured inside the ropes under
  US commission rules (larger rings, up to about 7.3 m, are used by agreement and in
  international bouts), raised about 0.9-1.2 m above the floor (US state rules cap it at
  54 in / 1.37 m), padded with foam, covered with a canvas that is stained and scuffed; three
  or four ropes (at least 2.5 cm thick, usually taped to about 4-5 cm) wrapped in tape and tied to the corner posts with turnbuckle pads in red and blue; a
  neutral corner in the other two corners (white). The steps and the apron, the bell and the
  stool in the corners.
- The gloves: 8 oz (227 g) for pro bouts up to 147 lb (welterweight), 10 oz (283 g) above (US commission rules); 12-16 oz for sparring; a
  thumb attached to the glove; laces or velcro; leather with a stitched seam and a wrist band.
  The red and blue corners. The gloves look larger than the fist.
- The gym: a heavy bag (30-45 kg), a speed bag, a double-end bag, a rope, mirrors, a ring,
  a timer clock with a bell (rounds of 3 min with 1 min rest in pro men's boxing), a bucket,
  a spit bucket, a towel, tape, a mouthguard, headgear for sparring.
- The boxer: shorts with a wide waistband, boots or low-top boxing shoes with a thin sole for
  pivoting, a robe in the corner, a hand-wrapped hand, sweat, vaseline on the face.
- Places: a dim old gym with a single light over the ring, a basement, a rooftop, a garage, a
  stadium ring with a spotlight and smoke, a locker room, a street at dawn for roadwork.
- Weather and light that read well: a hard top-light in smoke, sweat catching a backlight, a
  neon sign in a window, a rainy street at dawn.

## Product hero moments

For gloves: the leather and the stitching in macro, the glove hitting the bag with a puff of
dust, the laces tightened by a trainer, the glove in a locker, the packshot on black.

For wraps: the wrapping sequence in macro, the fabric tension.

For shoes: the ball-of-the-foot pivot on canvas, the lace-up in the corner, a skipping-rope
shot at the shoes, the sole in macro.

For apparel: the robe being put on in the tunnel, the waistband logo (the brand's own), the
sweat-darkened shorts, the hood.

For a rope or a bag: the rope's blur under the shoes, the bag's chain swing, the dent on the hit.

## Shots that work for boxing

| shot | for |
|---|---|
| ground-level tracking shot beside the footwork | speed, shoes at the canvas |
| low front dolly back (leading) as the boxer walks to the ring | menace, the boxer growing in frame |
| macro push-in on a glove or a wrapped hand | the product's first read |
| low side angle on the jab or the cross, ramp to slow motion | the hero moment |
| through the ropes, a foreground rope blurred | depth, the ring as a place |
| top-down drone (or overhead rig) on the ring | the ring as a square graphic, the canvas |
| walking out: tunnel walk from behind | anticipation, the lights at the end |
| low behind, walking away toward the light | the outro |
| studio packshot on black | the end card |
| speed-bag close-up in a macro | rhythm, sweat |
| slow handheld push-in on the face in profile | the stare before the bell |

## Sound

- The punch on the bag: a deep, dry thud with a slap of leather, and the chain rattle; a
  jab is a crisp "pop", a hook a heavy "whump".
- Breathing: sharp exhalations ("tss") with each punch; heavy breathing at rest.
- The bell: a bright metallic ring; a timer beep; a rope slapping on the floor in rhythm.
- Shoes on canvas: a quiet shuffle and a squeak; the ropes creak; the floor boards thump.
- The speed bag: a fast rhythmic patter; the crowd in the stadium, a corner man's call, a
  trainer's "again".
- Edit: silence before the bell, then hit on the first punch.

## Video-model risks and how to avoid them

| risk | what happens | avoid it |
|---|---|---|
| fused or extra fingers | six fingers on a wrapped hand, fingers fused in a glove | glove on in the still, the hand clenched; macro only at rest; short clips |
| gloves melting into the body | the glove blends with the chest or the face | a clear gap between the glove and the body in the start frame, a punch passing in front of a plain background |
| punches that go through the target | the glove passes through the bag or the face | start the clip at the contact frame; the bag shows a dent; "the bag swings back" |
| both arms punching | both arms extend at once, or the rear hand drops | describe "the right hand stays at the chin" and one punch per clip |
| the footwork | feet cross, the legs trade places | frame from the knees up, or a side view, real speed |
| rope behaviour | the skipping rope vanishes or stays still | a short clip, a motion blur on the rope; the rope is in the start still as an arc |
| ring ropes | the ropes bend or the number changes | a still with three or four ropes and an even tension; a locked camera |
| faces and blood | faces morph; wounds are exaggerated | frame from behind or in profile, a silhouette; do not show injuries in a product ad |
| two boxers | limbs merge in a clinch | one boxer per clip, or two boxers far apart with a sparring partner out of focus |
| text and logos | the shorts' waistband text changes | no readable text, or locked in the still |

## Ad grammar seen in boxing ads

- Dark and quiet: a single light over a heavy bag, the sound of breath and chain, no music at
  first.
- Ritual: wrapping the hands, tape, the lace-up, the walk-out, then the bell.
- A training montage: short holds (6-12 frames) cut on the punches, the rope, the speed bag,
  the road run at dawn, with a build in tempo.
- Slow motion on sweat spraying from a punch or from the hair, the glove's deformation; a hit
  (flash cut) on the impact.
- The fighter's face in close-up with a held gaze, often a direct look at the camera; a
  voice-over or a stark line of text.
- The end card: the logo, the product's name, a short line.
