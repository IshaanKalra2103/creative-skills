# Basketball

What a basketball ad has to get right, and what image and video models get wrong. Shot names
refer to `references/shots.md`; transitions to `references/transitions.md`.

## Movements

Each movement: the phases in order, how long they take at real speed (30 fps), the body cues
that make it read as real, the frame where the product shows best, and how to shoot it.
Frame counts are approximate (approx.) unless a source or measured figure is named beside them.

### Jump shot (the main movement)

| phase | frames at 30 fps | body |
|---|---|---|
| catch or gather | 4-6 (approx.) | Hands up as a target, ball arrives into the shooting pocket at chest or hip height. Knees already bending, feet landing shoulder-width, toes roughly at the rim. |
| dip and load | 6-8 (approx.) | Knees and hips flex, ball dips to the hip or chest, elbow tucked under the ball (elbow below wrist, in line with the rim). Weight over the balls of the feet. |
| rise | 6-8 (approx.) | Legs extend, ball travels up the centreline of the body to the set point (forehead or just above, shooting elbow near 90 degrees), guide hand on the side of the ball. |
| release | 2-3 | At or just before the top of the jump: elbow extends, wrist snaps, the guide hand stays off the ball. Eyes on the front of the rim. |
| follow-through | 6-10 | Shooting arm fully extended with the wrist flopped forward ("hand in the cookie jar", fingers pointing down at the rim), guide hand dropped or held up. Player lands where they took off, slightly forward. |

- From the start of the dip to release a jump shot takes about 0.6-0.7 s (17-20 frames) in
  published kinematic data (jump shots from 2.8-6.4 m); the split between phases above is
  approx. Elite three-point shooters release in under about 0.85 s from the catch.
- The ball leaves the hand with backspin, up to about 3 revolutions a second in flight
  (Tran and Silverberg 2008 simulation; a lazier shot spins less); the seams
  show as a slow rotation. It is backspin: the top of the ball moves back toward the shooter,
  the bottom forward, so the seams roll against the direction of flight.
- A three-pointer in flight is about 1.1-1.3 s from release to rim (33-39 frames, projectile
  calculation for a 7.2 m NBA three at 45-52 degree launch); a free throw about 0.85-1.0 s
  (26-30 frames). Peak of the arc is well above the rim; the ball enters steeply, about
  38-45 degrees for a free throw (field data 38-42), never flat.
- Hero frame: the release or the first frames of the follow-through, the shoe still pushed off
  the floor with the sole angled to camera, or the ball leaving the fingertips.
- Tells that it is fake: elbow flared far out from the body, the ball in the palm instead of
  the pads of the fingers and a gap of air under the palm, both hands pushing the ball (a
  two-handed chest shot in a modern jump shot), feet together and straight with no knee bend,
  the guide hand on top of the ball, the ball released while the player is falling backward
  with no reason (a fadeaway needs a visible lean and a one-foot takeoff).

### Crossover dribble

| phase | frames at 30 fps | body |
|---|---|---|
| set | 3-5 | Low stance, knees deep, ball on one side at knee height, eyes up on the defender. |
| push | 2-3 | Fingertips (not the palm) push the ball low and across, to a point in front of the opposite foot. |
| shift | 3-5 | Hips and head fake one way, the plant foot drives, the other hand receives the ball at knee or hip height. |
| burst | 4-8 | The new lead foot pushes off hard, torso low, body angled past the defender. |

- Ball travels across the body in 4-8 frames; the dribble itself is about 2.5-3.5 bounces a
  second at speed (a bounce every 9-12 frames), the ball never above the waist.
- Hero frame: the ball at its lowest point on the floor between the feet, shoes planted and
  the plant shoe's edge digging in. Low side angle, ground level.
- Tells: ball bouncing at head height, the palm slapping down, the player standing upright,
  the ball bouncing from a hand that is not in contact with it, a dribble with both hands on
  the ball (a carry).

### Dunk

| phase | frames at 30 fps | body |
|---|---|---|
| approach | 10-20 | Two or three long strides, gathering the ball on the last step. |
| plant and gather | 3-4 | A two-foot or one-foot plant, knees loaded deep, arms swinging back or the ball brought to the chest. |
| takeoff and rise | 8-12 | Explosive extension, arms swinging up, ball carried above the head. |
| flush | 2-4 | Wrist above the rim, ball pushed down through the hoop with one or two hands. |
| hang and drop | 6-10 | Brief hang on the rim (optional), then land with knees bent. |

- Rim 3.05 m, so the hand reaches about 3.3 m; a takeoff from 2.5 m with a 1 m vertical looks
  right. A flush with a clear hand-over-rim contact.
- Hero frame: the peak, the sole of the shoe at head height of the camera, or the ball above
  the rim as the net starts to react.
- Tells: the ball passing through the rim ring instead of the net opening, hands not gripping
  the ball, a jump with no knee load, a body that floats up in a straight line with no
  extension, hanging on the rim by the net instead of the iron.

### Layup and euro step

- Layup: the drive, a gather on the second to last step (the ball taken to the hip), a long
  last stride off the inside foot, ball released off the fingertips high on the backboard's
  small square (shot at about 45 degrees) with a soft roll. 14-20 frames from the gather to
  the release.
- Euro step: two quick steps in different directions around a defender. Gather with the ball
  carried wide in both hands; plant the first step wide, then push off and step across to the
  opposite side, a lateral shift of about 1 m. 6-9 frames between the two steps. Needs a low
  centre of gravity and a pivot on the first foot.
- Tells: carrying the ball overhead for the gather, three steps taken in a straight line, the
  same foot twice.

### Rebound

- Box out first (the back and elbows into the opponent, knees bent), then two-foot jump,
  arms fully extended, the ball taken with both hands and brought to the chest with the elbows
  out. 10-14 frames of rise. The rebound often shows two or three hands on a ball at once.

### No-look pass

- Eyes locked on one target, head and shoulders turn the opposite way, the ball leaves from
  the hips or the shoulder with a wrist snap, 3-5 frames for the pass itself. Chest or
  bounce passes fly at roughly 8-15 m/s (approx.); the receiver's hands are already up.

### Defence slide

- Low stance, knees bent deep, back flat, arms wide with one hand up and one down, feet never
  crossing, a push-slide step of about a shoe length. 6-9 frames a step. The hips stay level.
- Tells: feet crossing over, the player upright.

### Free throw

- Standing on the line 4.57 m from the backboard plane (NBA), one hand (the ball's weight in the
  fingertips), the same routine each time: the dribbles (two or three), a deep breath, knees
  bending, a quiet rise. The whole routine is 4-8 s; the shot itself under 1 s.
- Hero frame: the follow-through, the arm up and the ball half-way, or the ball dropping
  through the net. Good for the quiet beat.

## Props and setting

- The ball: size 7 for men (FIBA 75.0-77.0 cm round, 580-620 g; NBA about 74.9 cm / 29.5 in
  and about 624 g; about 24 cm across); size 6 for women's play (FIBA 71.5-73.0 cm,
  510-550 g). Orange pebbled leather or composite, eight panels
  with black channel seams. Keep the same ball in every shot, and the same pebbling.
- The hoop: the rim 3.05 m (10 ft) above the floor, inside diameter 45-45.9 cm (18 in in the NBA), orange steel
  ring, a net that hangs 40-45 cm (FIBA; NBA 18 in / 46 cm). Backboard 1.80 m wide by 1.05 m
  tall (FIBA; NBA 6 ft by 3.5 ft, 1.83 by 1.07 m), with a painted rectangle 59 cm by 45 cm
  behind the rim. A net on an indoor hoop is white nylon
  cord; on an outdoor park hoop it is often a steel chain net, which rings and shivers when the
  ball goes through.
- The court (international): 28 m by 15 m; (NBA) 28.65 m by 15.24 m. Free-throw line 4.57 m
  from the backboard plane (NBA, 15 ft; FIBA about 4.60 m), three-point line 6.75 m (FIBA) or 7.24 m (NBA, top of the key; 6.71 m in the corner),
  the key (lane) 4.9 m wide (16 ft in the NBA). A hardwood floor (maple) with a polished finish reflects
  the lights and the shoes; paint is solid colour. Outdoor courts are asphalt or painted
  concrete with chalky lines and cracks.
- The shoes: high-top or mid-top with a thick midsole, a herringbone or multi-directional
  outsole for grip; shoes squeak on a hardwood floor. Keep the colourway locked by reference.
- Other: the jersey and shorts, headbands, sleeves, wristbands, a towel, a water bottle,
  a ball bag, a locker room with benches.
- Places: an empty arena at night with only the court lit, a crowded one, a gym with a
  bright door, a rooftop or city park court with a chain net and chain-link fence, a street
  with a hoop on a pole, a black studio with a strip of hardwood.
- Weather and light that read well: arena spotlights from above with dark stands, low sun
  through a park fence, sodium street light, rain on an outdoor court (the ball throws
  water), steam from sweat in cold air.

## Product hero moments

For shoes:
- The sole at the peak of a jump or a dunk, the tread visible.
- The plant of a crossover, the shoe's edge bent and the midsole compressed.
- The lace-up, the heel counter and the hands pulling the laces.
- The squeak: a macro of the outsole turning on the floor, with the grain of the wood.
- The packshot: the shoe on a block of hardwood on black, rim-lit.

For a ball: the seams and pebbling in macro, the spin in flight, the ball in the net, the ball
bouncing at the floor with a ripple of dust.

For apparel: the jersey in motion, the mesh texture, the number, a sweat-soaked sleeve, the
pull-over in a locker room.

## Shots that work for basketball

| shot | for |
|---|---|
| ground-level tracking shot beside the dribble | speed, shoes and ball together |
| low front dolly back (leading) as he drives at camera | menace, the player growing in frame |
| macro push-in on a shoe on the hardwood | the product's first read |
| low side angle on the jump shot, ramp to slow motion | the release as the hero moment |
| behind the backboard, looking through the net | the payoff of a made shot |
| overhead from the rafters (top-down drone, or a crane) | the court's lines and the key as a graphic |
| through an object (the net, the chain link, a locker door) | transitions and reveals |
| tunnel walk from behind | anticipation, the arena lights at the end |
| low behind, walking away toward the light | the outro |
| studio packshot on black | the end card |
| ball-cam low on the floor, a dribble from under the ball | the rhythm of the bounce, speed ramps |

## Sound

- The bounce: a hollow, low, resonant thud on hardwood, rhythmic; on asphalt, a drier slap.
- Shoes: sharp squeaks on a stop or a cut; a skid on a hard stop; a thud on a landing.
- The net: a swish (a clean soft whoosh and a sharp snap) for a clean make; a clank for an
  iron hit with a ring that decays; a chain net rattles like a loose chain.
- The backboard: a thump and a metal ring; a dunk shakes the whole stanchion.
- Crowd: a low murmur, a roar on a make, a sudden silence before a free throw.
- A whistle, a buzzer, a shot-clock beep, a coach's call.
- Edit: drop the music out for the beat before the release, hit on the swish.

## Video-model risks and how to avoid them

| risk | what happens | avoid it |
|---|---|---|
| ball through the rim | the ball passes through the iron or the backboard | start the clip with the ball already above the rim (the net open below), or after the ball is in the net; describe the ball dropping through the open net |
| extra fingers on the ball handler | six fingers, fused fingers or hands melting into the ball | frame from the knees down, from behind or with the hand in motion blur; the ball is in the fingertips; short clips |
| extra balls | a second ball appears mid-clip | one ball in the start frame, "one basketball" in the prompt, 5 s clips |
| the ball glued to the hand | it moves with the hand instead of bouncing | start the clip with the ball at the floor, and state the ball's height in each frame |
| dribble that does not bounce | the ball floats or drifts | avoid a dribble in the hero clip, or shoot a low side angle at one bounce |
| rim and net drift | the rim bends or the net changes shape | wide shots with the hoop out of focus; a still that already shows the net clean and a locked, steady camera |
| squeaky logo and text | the jersey number or shoe logo changes | strong reference for the product, 5 s clips, qa.py checks, keep the number unseen or in silhouette |
| leg or arm swap | the arms or legs trade places in a crossover | frame from the knees down, real speed, one action per clip |
| court lines warp | the three-point arc or key wobbles | an overhead still of the court, and cut away from the lines in motion |
| the shot arc | the ball moves in a straight line or a flat arc | ask for "a high arc, backspin", and cut to the net on the make instead of following the ball |
| faces | a face morphs between frames | from behind, in silhouette or with the face blocked by the ball |

## Ad grammar seen in basketball shoe launches

- Product first, in the dark: a macro on the shoe's sole or midsole in a pool of light before
  anything moves, over a dribble sound.
- Short holds (6-20 frames) in the build, with the dribble's rhythm used as a metronome; the
  cut lands on the bounce. Longer holds (45-90 frames) on the jump shot and the packshot.
- A single hero action in extreme slow motion with a hit on the release or the dunk, often
  after a beat of silence.
- A match cut from the ball leaving the hand to the net, or from a bounce to a heartbeat or a
  door slamming.
- Anonymous or silhouetted players when the shoe is the star; named athletes in a talking
  head or a voice-over when the ad sells the player.
- Hand-held and street-level settings (a park, a rooftop) next to arena shots; courts as a
  place of origin in the story.
- The end card: the logo, the shoe's name, a short line.
