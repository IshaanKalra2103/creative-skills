# Baseball

What a baseball ad has to get right, and what image and video models get wrong. Shot names
refer to `references/shots.md`; transitions to `references/transitions.md`.

## Movements

Each movement: the phases in order, how long they take at real speed (30 fps), the body cues
that make it read as real, the frame where the product shows best, and how to shoot it.
Frame counts are approximate (approx.) unless a source or measured figure is named beside them.

### Pitching delivery (the main movement)

| phase | frames at 30 fps | body |
|---|---|---|
| set or wind-up | 15-45 | Standing on the rubber, the ball hidden in the glove with both hands at the chest, weight on the back foot. |
| leg lift | 15-25 | The lead knee rises to about waist height, the hands stay together at the chest, the body balanced on the back leg, the posture tall. |
| stride and separation | 8-12 | The hips drive toward the plate while the hands break; the glove arm reaches toward the target, the throwing arm goes back and up. |
| foot plant | 2-3 | The lead foot lands on the toe then the whole foot, a stride of about 80-87 percent of the pitcher's height (Fleisig: 83 +/- 4 percent), pointed at the plate. The throwing arm is cocked: the elbow at shoulder height, the forearm up. |
| arm acceleration and release | 4-5 | The hips rotate, then the trunk, then the shoulder, the elbow extends and the wrist snaps; the ball leaves the fingers about out in front of the lead foot. The glove tucks into the chest. |
| follow-through | 6-10 | The throwing arm finishes across the body to the opposite knee, the back leg swings forward and the pitcher lands square and ready to field. |

- Foot plant to release is about 0.15 s (4-5 frames, approx.). A 95 mph (153 km/h) fastball covers the
  60 ft 6 in (18.44 m) in about 0.4 s (12 frames at 30 fps), so the ball is a blur, and a
  30 fps clip shows it in only 12 positions.
- Hero frame: the release, the ball leaving the fingertips, or the foot plant with the dirt
  spraying from the landing spike, or the knee at the peak of the leg lift in silhouette.
- Tells that it is fake: the throwing arm moving as in a javelin throw while the body stays
  upright, the lead foot landing sideways, the pitcher's throwing hand out of the glove too
  early, the ball in the palm instead of the fingertips (four-seam grip across the seams), a
  glove hand flailing, a lack of the hip and the shoulder rotation, the pitcher standing in
  front of the rubber, or the mound flat.

### Swing

| phase | frames at 30 fps | body |
|---|---|---|
| stance | 15-30 | Feet slightly wider than the shoulders, knees soft, the weight on the balls of the feet, the bat held up behind the back shoulder, the eyes on the pitcher. |
| load | 8-12 | A small stride or a weight shift to the back foot, the hands move back a few centimetres. |
| stride and launch | 3-4 | The front foot lands, the hips begin to rotate, the hands stay back. |
| rotation | 3-4 | Hips lead, then the shoulders, then the arms, then the bat; the back heel turns up and the back knee points to the plate. |
| contact | 1 | The bat meets the ball in front of the plate, the barrel level, the arms extended, the head down looking at the contact point. |
| follow-through | 6-10 | The bat wraps around the shoulders, the back foot pivots on its toe, and the batter's chest faces the pitcher. |

- From the launch to contact is about 0.15 s (4-5 frames, approx.; derived from the 7.3 ft average swing path); the bat head travels at about 72 mph
  on average (Statcast average swing 71.5 mph; over 75 mph counts as fast, the fastest
  hitters average about 80 mph; the slowest about 62 mph), 115-130 km/h at the fast end. The ball is on the bat for under a millisecond; the
  bat flexes and the ball compresses.
- Hero frame: contact, with a splinter of wood or a ring of dust, or the bat just before
  contact with the barrel in motion blur and the batter's head down.
- Tells: the batter's eyes up and away from the ball at contact, a bat held like a golf club,
  the batter facing the pitcher with his shoulders open at the start, an upper body-only swing
  with feet planted, the bat moving up like a chop, the ball hit when the bat is behind the
  batter.

### Catching a fly ball

- Track the ball and move into its path with the glove side, the eyes on the ball, the body
  turned slightly, the glove up above the shoulder with the pocket facing the ball; catch it
  at the eyes with both hands, the throwing hand next to the glove to trap the ball. 4-6
  frames to close the glove. The running catch has the arm fully extended, the glove with the
  pocket at the end, the head turned.
- Hero frame: the ball entering the webbing, the fingers of the glove closing, or a diving
  catch with the body parallel to the ground and the glove stretched.
- Tells: a glove pointing at the ground, the ball arriving in the bare hand, the ball passing
  through the glove, two gloves.

### Sliding into base

| phase | frames at 30 fps | body |
|---|---|---|
| run | 10-20 | A full sprint to the base. |
| takeoff | 3-4 | About 3 m (10 ft) before the base the runner drops, the lead leg extended, the trail leg folded under (the "figure four"). |
| slide | 10-18 | The runner skims along the dirt on the trail-leg's thigh, buttock and one hand up; the lead foot reaches the base, the dust cloud pushes forward. |
| tag | 2-4 | The fielder's glove reaches down to the runner's leg. |

- Feet-first slide: the arms up, the head back and up. Head-first (dive): the arms extended
  ahead, the chest low, the hand reaching the bag. Both throw a cloud of dirt.
- Hero frame: the foot reaching the bag, the dust cloud, the fielder's glove arriving; a low
  side angle on the dirt line.
- Tells: sliding on grass (they slide on dirt), the legs crossed wrongly, a base moving, a
  slide without dust, the runner's head down toward the ground in a feet-first slide.

### Fielding a grounder

- Move in front of the ball, feet wide, the knees bent, the hips low, the glove open and out
  in front on the ground, the throwing hand above the glove, the eyes on the ball into the
  glove. Funnel the ball to the belly, then replant the feet and throw, with a quick transfer
  of 4-6 frames. The throw to first base takes a crow hop: a hop and a step with a pivot.
- Hero frame: the ball reaching the glove on the dirt, the dust at the contact.
- Tells: an upright body bending only at the waist, the glove up and the ball going through
  the legs, the feet together.

### Pitcher's grip

- The four-seam fastball held across the horseshoe-shaped seams with the index and middle
  fingers on the widest seams and the thumb under the ball. A curveball held along a seam with
  the middle finger. The hand is visible at the wind-up and a macro shot of the ball in the
  fingers is a hero beat. Count the fingers: four fingers and a thumb.

## Props and setting

- The ball: a cork and rubber core wound in yarn, covered in two figure-eight pieces of white
  cowhide (horsehide in the past), with 108 double-stitched raised red seams; circumference 9-9.25 in
  (22.9-23.5 cm), diameter about 7.3-7.5 cm, weight 5-5.25 oz (142-149 g). Show the red seams
  as a continuous curved figure-eight.
- The bat: max 42 in (107 cm) long, barrel max 2.61 in (6.6 cm) in diameter; wood (ash, maple
  or birch) with a visible grain and a branded barrel, a taper, a knob and a handle with pine
  tar; aluminum or composite for amateur play (a metallic ping).
- The glove: leather, a webbing between the thumb and the index finger, a pocket, stitched
  fingers, a wrist strap, laces, and a size (a fielder's glove is 11-12.75 in). A catcher's mitt
  is large and padded without fingers.
- Other: batting helmet with an ear flap, batting gloves, cleats (metal or moulded), a cap,
  the catcher's gear (mask, chest protector, shin guards), eye black, bubble gum, sunflower
  seeds, a rosin bag, a dugout bench and rail, chain-link, a scoreboard.
- The diamond: bases 90 ft (27.4 m) apart, each base 15 in (38 cm) square, white canvas bags;
  home plate a 17 in (43 cm) wide five-sided slab of white rubber; the pitcher's mound 10 in
  (25 cm) above the home plate, a circle of dirt 18 ft (5.5 m) in diameter, the rubber
  24 in by 6 in, 60 ft 6 in (18.44 m) from home; the batter's boxes 4 ft by 6 ft; the foul lines,
  the infield dirt, the infield grass (cut in a diagonal or a stripe pattern), the outfield wall.
- Dirt: red or brown clay-and-sand; raked in lines, wetted before a game; dust rises easily
  on slides. Grass has a mowed pattern.
- Places: a stadium under lights, a small-town ballpark at dusk with wooden bleachers, a sandlot
  with a chain-link backstop, a batting cage, a bullpen, a dugout, a locker room, a field after
  rain with puddles.
- Weather that reads well: golden hour with long shadows on the dirt, stadium lights with
  haze, a light rain with spray, a night game with insects in the lights.

## Product hero moments

For bats: the barrel's grain and the brand mark in macro, the ball hitting the sweet spot with
a puff of pine tar dust, the bat dropped on the plate in slow motion, a hand wrapping tape on
the handle, the packshot on black with a single rim light.

For gloves: the oil and leather texture, the ball landing in the pocket in slow motion with
dust, the laces being tightened, the breaking-in with a ball and a band, the glove on a bench.

For cleats: the spikes digging into dirt on a slide, the spray at a start, the lace-up in the
dugout, the plate being kicked clean, a macro on the sole.

For a ball: the seams in macro, the rotation in flight, the grip across the seams, the scuff
and grass stain, the ball in the glove with dust.

For apparel: the jersey stitched with the team name (use a fictional team, no real
logos), pinstripes, a cap in sun and sweat, the pull of the pants on the slide.

## Shots that work for baseball

| shot | for |
|---|---|
| ground-level tracking shot beside the runner | speed, cleats and dirt |
| low front dolly back (leading) as the pitcher delivers | the pitcher growing in frame |
| macro push-in on a glove or a bat on the dirt | the product's first read |
| low side angle on the swing or the slide, ramp to slow motion | the hero moment |
| behind the plate, over the catcher's shoulder, looking at the pitcher | tension, the strike zone |
| top-down drone on the diamond | the diamond as a graphic shape, scale |
| through an object (the chain-link backstop, the dugout rail) | transitions and reveals |
| tunnel walk from behind | anticipation, the field's light at the end |
| low behind, walking away toward the light | the outro |
| studio packshot on black | the end card |
| pitcher's point-of-view of the catcher's mitt | the target, the stillness |

## Sound

- The ball on the bat: a sharp "crack" for wood, a "ping" for aluminum; louder than people
  expect, with a stadium echo.
- The ball into the mitt: a loud "pop" or "smack" that carries; a fastball pops louder than a
  change-up.
- The slide: a hiss and scrape of dirt, a thud on the bag; a glove tag slap.
- Cleats on dirt and on concrete in the dugout; a bat tapped on the plate; a bat dropping.
- Crowd: organ music, a murmur, a roar on a hit, a vendor's call, a distant announcer.
- The umpire's call: "Strike" with a fist or a "Safe" with arms out.
- Edit: drop the music before the pitch, hit on the crack.

## Video-model risks and how to avoid them

| risk | what happens | avoid it |
|---|---|---|
| the ball vanishes in flight | a 95 mph pitch disappears or hangs | start the clip at the release and cut before the ball arrives; show the ball in a still or a macro |
| the bat passes through the ball | contact with no impact, or the ball changes direction | start the clip just before contact and describe the ball's path after it; ramp to slow motion for contact |
| bat shape | the bat bends, doubles, or becomes a stick | a bat reference, "one wooden bat", short clips, motion blur at the barrel |
| extra fingers on the glove hand | six fingers or a glove fused with the hand | frame from the knees down, the glove in profile, a macro at rest |
| the ball in the hand | the ball is held in the palm or the number of seams changes | a macro of the four-seam grip in the still, and the ball partly hidden |
| the pitcher's body | the throwing arm swaps, the body twists, the legs cross | frame from the side, behind or in silhouette, one action per clip |
| the mound | the mound flattens, the rubber vanishes | a still with a clear mound and rubber, a medium wide shot |
| the dirt and dust | the dust floats like smoke | "a cloud of dust, kicked low, settling in a second" |
| base and lines | the bases move or lines warp | locked wide shots, a shallow depth of field |
| the team and the logo | a real team logo is drawn or text changes | use a fictional team, keep the jersey text out of focus |
| faces | a face morphs between frames | from behind, in silhouette, with a cap or a helmet |

## Ad grammar seen in baseball ads

- Rituals and small details: the glove oiled, the bat taped, the dirt raked, a cap pulled down;
  quiet shots for the first third.
- Product first, in the dark or in warm light: a macro on the leather or the wood before any
  motion, with a sound of a ball hitting a mitt.
- Long holds on the stillness before the pitch (60-120 frames) and short holds on the action
  (6-20 frames); a hit at the crack or the pop.
- A single swing or pitch in extreme slow motion, with the dust or the splinters as the visual
  payoff.
- Generational stories and the sandlot: a parent and a child, a field at dusk, nostalgia.
- Narration often uses a voice-over about the game's history; the field's emptiness as a place.
- The end card: the logo, the product's name, a short line.
