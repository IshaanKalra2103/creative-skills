# American football

What an American football ad has to get right, and what image and video models get wrong.
Shot names refer to `references/shots.md`; transitions to `references/transitions.md`.

## Movements

Each movement: the phases in order, how long they take at real speed (30 fps), the body cues
that make it read as real, the frame where the product shows best, and how to shoot it.
Frame counts are approximate (approx.) unless a source or measured figure is named beside them.

### Quarterback drop-back and throw (the main movement)

| phase | frames at 30 fps | body |
|---|---|---|
| drop-back | 45-90 | Receives the snap, turns and runs backward. A three-step drop is about 1.5-2 s from the snap to the throw, a five-step about 2.5 s, a seven-step about 3 s or a little more (approx.; coaching figures, no tracking source found). Eyes downfield, ball held at the chest with two hands. |
| set | 3-5 | Last step plants the back foot, weight on the back leg, the ball brought to the ear/shoulder height in the throwing hand, non-throwing hand on the ball's front. |
| cock and stride | 4-6 | Front foot strides toward the target, hips open, the non-throwing arm points at the target, the ball behind the ear with the elbow at or above shoulder height. |
| release | 2-3 | Hips rotate, then trunk, then shoulder, then elbow, then wrist. The ball leaves off the first two fingers' tips with a spiral snap. |
| follow-through | 6-9 | Throwing arm finishes across the body, the thumb down, rear foot comes forward, hips square to the target. |

- From the start of the stride to release is about 0.25-0.35 s (8-10 frames, approx.); from
  front-foot contact to release only about 0.1-0.2 s (3-6 frames, approx.), and the arm
  acceleration from maximum shoulder external rotation to release a few hundredths of a second.
  Throw speeds: a typical NFL pass leaves the hand at about 22-27 m/s (50-60 mph); the
  NFL Combine radar record is 60 mph (26.8 m/s), and a soft touch pass is nearer 18 m/s
  (40 mph); the ball in flight is in a tight spiral, the nose pointing along the path and
  dipping slowly.
- Hero frame: the release, the ball leaving the fingertips with the laces under the fingers;
  or the cocked ball behind the ear in profile.
- Tells that it is fake: the ball held in the palm like a basketball, the ball tumbling end
  over end in a pass (it spirals), the throwing elbow below the shoulder, feet together, a
  wobbling pass with no spiral, throwing with the front foot on the same side as the arm (the
  stride is opposite).

### Catch (one-handed, toe-tap sideline)

| phase | frames at 30 fps | body |
|---|---|---|
| track | 12-30 | Eyes on the ball, run at a speed that meets it, hands moving up as the ball arrives. |
| reach | 3-5 | Arms extend to the ball's flight line; one-handed: the arm fully extended, the hand open, fingers spread. |
| secure | 2-4 | The ball cushioned by the hands and pulled in to the chest or tucked under the arm (ball high and tight, pointing forward). |
| tap | 3-6 | On a sideline catch: the toes of both feet land inbounds, one after the other, the body leaning toward the line. |

- Hands form a diamond with the thumbs and fingers, the ball caught with the fingers, not the
  palms. Eyes stay on the ball through the catch; a defender's hand is often on the arm.
- Hero frame: the ball just touching the fingers, or the toe of the cleat on the line.
- Tells: the ball appearing already in the hand, the toes landing out of bounds, the ball in
  the palm like a basketball, the receiver's head turned away.

### Juke and cut

- A planted outside foot (the cleat digging into the turf), the hips and the shoulders dip in
  one direction, then the body drives off the plant foot at about 45-90 degrees in 8-12
  frames. The cleats throw turf and rubber infill. The ball is carried tucked, high and tight, in
  the arm away from the defender.
- Hero frame: the plant, cleat bent and studs in the turf. Low, side-on.
- Tells: an upright cut with no shoulder dip, both feet planting at the same time.

### Stiff-arm

- The arm extends straight to the defender's chest, the elbow locked, the palm on the
  shoulder pad or the facemask, the ball carried in the other arm. The runner keeps stride
  while pushing the defender away. 4-6 frames from extension to contact.

### Tackle

- The tackler lowers the hips and keeps the head up (eyes on the target, "see what you hit"),
  contact with the shoulder pad in the ball carrier's midsection, arms wrap and squeeze, legs
  drive through. About 6-10 frames of impact; both bodies fall in 15-25 frames.
- Hero frame: the moment of contact, pads flexing, chin straps, a burst of turf.
- Tells: helmet-first contact, two bodies merging, wrong numbers of limbs. Keep one ball
  carrier and one tackler in the frame.

### Sprint start and 40-yard dash

- A three-point stance (one hand on the turf, fingers under the shoulder, the back flat, hips
  high), a drive out with the first three steps low and forward, a gradual rise to upright.
  The 40-yard dash (36.6 m) for elite players is about 4.3-4.5 s, 130-135 frames (the Combine
  record is 4.21 s); the first 10 yards in about 1.5 s (elite splits are under 1.5-1.6 s). Arms drive at 90 degrees, knees high.
- Hero frame: the first step out of the stance, the cleat pushing off the line.

### Kick (field goal, kickoff, punt)

| phase | frames at 30 fps | body |
|---|---|---|
| approach | 20-30 | Two or three steps back and to the side from the spot, then a curved approach of three or four steps, a slight lean. |
| plant | 3-4 | Plant foot lands beside and just behind the ball, toes at the target, knee slightly bent. |
| swing | 4-6 | The kicking leg swings from the hip, the knee leading, the toe straight, the head down. |
| contact | 1 | The instep strikes the ball's lower third (the ball on a tee at kickoff, held by a holder for a field goal). |
| follow-through | 8-12 | The leg carries high past the target line, the body rotates, the plant foot may lift. |

- A field goal from the snap to the kick takes about 1.3 s (approx.); the ball spins end over end, the
  long axis rotating, not a spiral (a field goal tumbles, a punt spirals). A punter drops the
  ball onto the foot, no tee.
- Tells: the ball spiralling like a pass, a toe poke with a flat foot, the holder's hands
  not under the ball.

## Props and setting

- The ball: an oval prolate spheroid, 27.9-28.6 cm long (11-11 1/4 in), 53.3-54 cm round the middle (21-21 1/4 in), 397-425 g
  (14-15 oz; NFL rulebook), brown leather with the pebbled grain, a row of white laces on top
  and two white stripes at each end (college and high school), no stripes in the NFL. Keep the same ball in every shot.
- The helmet: a hard shell with a facemask of four or five bars, a chin strap, and decals;
  the visor is optional. The player's face should be mostly hidden, which helps video models.
- The pads: shoulder pads and hip, thigh and knee pads under the jersey; the jersey stretches
  tight over the pads and gives the V-shaped torso.
- The cleats: moulded cleats on turf, detachable studs on grass; low-top for speed (skill
  positions), mid-top or high-top for linemen. Lock the colourway by reference.
- The field: 100 yards between the goal lines (91.4 m), two end zones of 10 yards each, 53 1/3
  yards wide (48.8 m); a yard line every 5 yards and numbers every 10, hash marks near the
  middle; goalposts at the back of the end zone, crossbar 10 ft (3.05 m) high and 18 ft 6 in
  (5.6 m) between the uprights; orange pylons, a foot-and-a-half tall, at the four corners
  of each end zone. The turf is natural grass or synthetic turf with rubber crumb infill.
- Other: gloves, mouthguard, eye black, towels, a sideline bench, a tunnel, a locker room,
  a yellow penalty flag, a line-of-scrimmage marker, a coach's whiteboard.
- Places: a stadium at night with the lights up, a high-school field with Friday lights, a
  practice field at dawn, a locker room, the tunnel, a snow game, a mud game.
- Weather that reads well: rain (spray from cleats and ball), snow, breath steam in the cold,
  stadium haze, golden hour with long shadows.

## Product hero moments

For cleats:
- Studs digging into turf on a cut, with divots and rubber infill in the air.
- The lace-up on the bench; tape on the wrist and ankle.
- A macro on the upper with grippy texture, mud and rain.
- Sprint start: the cleat pushing off the line.
- The packshot: the cleat on a slab of turf on black.

For a ball: the laces and pebbling in macro, the spiral in flight, the ball in the hands in a
catch, the hand-off.

For helmet and pads: the helmet put on in the tunnel, the facemask reflecting the lights, the
decal in macro, the strap clicking shut. For apparel: the jersey stretched tight over the pads,
sweat and mud on the fabric, the number.

## Shots that work for American football

| shot | for |
|---|---|
| ground-level tracking shot beside the runner | speed, cleats and ball together |
| low front dolly back (leading) as the runner charges camera | menace, the player growing in frame |
| macro push-in on a cleat on the turf | the product's first read |
| low side angle on the throw, catch or cut, ramp to slow motion | the hero moment |
| behind the goalposts, looking up through the uprights | scale, the score |
| top-down drone on the field | the yard lines, the formation as a pattern |
| through an object (the facemask bars, the jersey, a fence) | reveals |
| tunnel walk from behind | anticipation, the stadium light at the end |
| low behind, walking away toward the light | the outro |
| studio packshot on black | the end card |
| helmet-to-helmet close-up in the huddle | team, tension before the snap |

## Sound

- The snap: a quick slap of leather on hands; the QB's cadence ("hut hut"), a sharp call.
- Pads: a heavy crack and grunt on a tackle, plastic and foam; a collision is louder than
  people expect.
- The throw: a short air whistle and a thump into the hands; a spiral hums.
- The kick: a deep, hollow "thud" boom from the ball.
- Cleats on turf: short chop and crunch; on a wet field, a squelch.
- Crowd: a roar on a big play, a hush before the snap. A whistle, a referee's call.
- Edit: drop the music before the snap or the throw, hit it on the catch or the tackle.

## Video-model risks and how to avoid them

| risk | what happens | avoid it |
|---|---|---|
| the ball shape | the ball becomes round or pointed at both ends like a rugby ball or loses its laces | a strong ball reference, laces on top in the still, "one American football" in the prompt |
| tumbling pass | the ball flips end over end in a throw | describe "a tight spiral along its long axis"; avoid following the whole flight, cut to the catch |
| helmets and faces | the facemask melts into the face, the number of bars changes | frame from behind or in silhouette, keep the helmet reference, short clips |
| tackle pile | limbs merge, extra arms and legs in a pile | one tackler and one carrier, a low side angle, cut away before the pile |
| the ball glued to the arm | the ball sticks to a runner's body | tucked in the arm in the still, ask for "ball tucked high and tight" |
| jersey numbers and text | numbers shift on a shirt | keep the back of the jersey out of focus or unseen |
| field lines warp | yard lines and numbers wobble | wide shots with a locked camera, a motion blur on the turf |
| extra players | players appear and vanish in the background | an empty field or a shallow depth of field, a small cast |
| the catch | the ball vanishes into the hands, passes through them or the hands multiply | start with the ball already touching the fingers, short clip, hand-in-focus macro |
| the kick | the foot passes through the ball | start mid-swing, contact in the first second |

## Ad grammar seen in football launches

- Product first, in the dark: a macro on the cleat or the helmet in the tunnel before anything
  moves, with a heartbeat or a low drone.
- Short holds (6-20 frames) for the build, a slow-motion hit of 60-90 frames on the catch,
  throw or tackle.
- Ritual before action: the lace-up, the tape, the chin strap, the tunnel, then the snap.
- Collision as a punctuation: the cut to black on the impact, a hit of sound.
- Teams, crowds and the stadium as scale; many athletes in motion in a short montage, with a
  star or a silhouette at the end.
- The end card: the logo, the product's name, a short line.
