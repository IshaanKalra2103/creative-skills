# Soccer (football)

What a soccer ad has to get right, and what image and video models get wrong. Shot names
refer to `references/shots.md`; transitions to `references/transitions.md`.

## Movements

Each movement: the phases in order, how long they take at real speed (30 fps), the body cues
that make it read as real, the frame where the product shows best, and how to shoot it.

### Laces strike (shot, driven pass)

| phase | frames at 30 fps | body |
|---|---|---|
| approach | 20-30 | Two or three strides in at a slight angle (about 30 degrees to the line of the shot). The last stride is the longest. |
| plant | 3-4 | Standing foot lands beside the ball, a boot's width away, toes pointing at the target. Knee bent. |
| backswing | 4-6 | Kicking leg cocks back from the hip, knee bent to about 90 degrees, heel near the backside. Arms swing out wide for balance; the arm opposite the kicking leg comes forward. |
| contact | 1 | Ankle locked, toe pointed down, the laces meet the ball's middle. Knee over the ball, chest over the knee. Head down, eyes on the ball. |
| follow-through | 6-10 | The kicking leg carries through toward the target and the player lands on the kicking foot, one stride past the ball. |

- Hero frame: contact. The boot's whole lateral side faces a camera low and side-on; the ball
  is squashed for one frame. Ramp to 0.1-0.25x a few frames before contact, hold through it,
  ramp back out on the follow-through.
- Tells that it is fake: plant foot behind the ball instead of beside it, toe up at contact
  (a toe poke), the ball sitting still after contact, arms by the sides, the player leaning
  back (that is a skied shot).

### Dribble at speed

- A touch every 2-3 strides, the ball 30-60 cm ahead, nudged with the outside or the laces of
  the leading foot. Strides shorten near the ball.
- Body leaning forward from the ankles, low centre of gravity, arms pumping, eyes up between
  touches.
- Hero frame: mid-stride with the touching foot about to meet the ball, both boots off the
  ground or one just planting. A ground-level tracking shot at the player's speed shows both
  boots and the ball together.
- Tells: the ball glued to the foot, the ball running ahead by metres (that is a sprint, not a
  dribble), touches on every stride, an upright jogging posture.

### Cut inside (cut, chop, drag)

- Plant the outside foot wide, knee bent deep over it, hips low, then sweep the ball across
  the body with the inside of the other foot and push off the planted foot hard.
- 10-15 frames from plant to push-off at real speed. The plant throws turf and water: the
  best moment for a speed ramp and a hit.
- Hero frame: the plant, studs biting, spray in the air, the sweeping boot's inner side on the
  ball.
- Tells: the sole on top of the ball (that is a drag-back or sole roll, a different move;
  image models default to it), no knee bend, the body upright.

### Step-over

- The foot circles over the ball from inside to outside without touching it, the body dips
  the same way, then the other foot takes the ball the opposite way. 8-12 frames per step.
- Video models often lose track of which leg is which here. Keep it to one step-over per
  clip, at real speed, and frame from the knees down.

### First touch (control)

- The receiving foot meets the ball and gives with it (draws back a little) to kill its pace;
  the ball drops dead within a stride. Chest and thigh control: the body cushions backward.
- Hero frame: the boot cushioning the ball in mid-air, the ball's seams sharp.

### Volley and bicycle kick

- Volley: the ball dropping, the striking foot meets it at knee height or below, body over the
  ball. Side-on volley: the body leans away, the leg swings flat across.
- Bicycle: back to goal, the body horizontal in the air, the striking leg snapping over the
  head while the other leg drops. 15-20 frames in the air. Hard for video models; generate the
  peak as a still and push in slowly rather than asking for the whole motion.

### Header

- Eyes open, the forehead (not the top of the head) meets the ball, neck tensed, arms out.
  Jumping header: the takeoff from one foot, the body arched back, then snapping forward.

### Slide tackle

- Low on one hip, the leading leg extended along the grass, the tackling foot hooking the ball,
  a long spray of water on a wet pitch. 20-30 frames of slide.

### Keeper dive

- Push off the near-side foot, the body fully extended sideways in the air, both hands
  leading, gloves open. The top-corner save is the classic hero frame.

### Celebration

- Knee slide on wet grass (spray, both knees, arms wide), the badge kiss (no badges here),
  running toward the corner flag, arms out. Good for the payoff beat.

### Juggling (keepy-ups)

- Light, soft touches on the laces with the toe slightly raised, the ball rising to knee or
  waist height, a rhythm of about 2-3 touches a second. Good for a product macro in a studio
  set.

## Props and setting

- The ball: size 5, about 22 cm across. A match ball has thermally bonded panels and a printed
  design; for a boot ad use a plain white ball with black pentagons, or the brand's own ball
  if the ad is for it. Keep the same ball in every shot (generate later stills with an
  earlier still as the ball reference). Wet balls throw a ring of spray on contact.
- The boots: firm-ground studs (FG) on grass, artificial-grass soles on 3G pitches, flat
  rubber soles indoors. The colourway is the product: lock it with reference photos of the
  exact model on every still.
- The pitch: white lines about 12 cm wide, freshly cut stripes from the mower, wet grass
  reflecting floodlights. On 3G (artificial) pitches black rubber crumb kicks up instead of
  water and turf.
- The goal: white posts and crossbar 2.44 m high and 7.32 m wide, the net hanging behind on
  stanchions. A ball in the net makes the net bulge outward and shiver; a ball against the
  post rings.
- The corner flag, the halfway line, the centre circle, the tunnel, the dressing room bench,
  shin pads, grip socks, tape around the socks, ice bath, kit bag.
- Places: a floodlit stadium (empty or full), a cage pitch (five-a-side, chain-link fence,
  concrete, street lamps), a rooftop pitch, a beach, a muddy local park, a training ground in
  mist, a black studio with a strip of grass.
- Weather that reads well on camera: rain under floodlights (lit streaks, spray from every
  touch), mist, low sun with long shadows.

## Product hero moments

For boots:
- Macro on the upper's texture with water beading on it.
- The studs biting into wet turf on a plant (ground level, side-on, spray).
- Laces contact on a strike, frozen.
- Lacing up in the tunnel or on the bench (hands, laces, the swoosh or stripes).
- Studs-up in slow motion mid-stride, the soleplate colour showing.
- The packshot: the boot alone, rim-lit on black or on a slab of turf.

For a ball: its seams spinning in macro, the squash at contact, the net bulge, the ball
dropping out of the night sky into a first touch.

For kit: the fabric moving in slow motion with rain on it, the crest (if the ad has the
rights), the player pulling the shirt on in the tunnel.

## Shots that work for soccer

| shot | for |
|---|---|
| ground-level tracking shot beside the dribble | speed, both boots and the ball in frame |
| low front dolly back (leading) as he runs at camera | menace, the player growing in frame |
| macro push-in on a boot on the turf | the product's first read |
| low side angle on the strike, ramp to slow motion | the hero moment |
| behind the goal, through the net | the payoff of a strike |
| top-down drone on the pitch | scale, the pattern of the lines |
| tunnel walk from behind | anticipation, the floodlights at the end |
| low behind, walking away toward the light | the outro |
| studio packshot on black | the end card |

## Sound

- Ball strike: a short, low, punchy thump with a leather slap; louder and drier than people
  expect. On a wet ball, a splash under it.
- Studs on turf: short crunches, one per step. Breathing at effort.
- Net: a soft high rustle and a shiver; the post: a metallic ring.
- Crowd: a roar that swells on the strike; an empty stadium gives reverb and rain instead.
- Referee's whistle (short, trilled), the keeper's shout, a coach's call.
- Edit: drop the music out for the beat before the strike, hit the drop on contact.

## Video-model risks and how to avoid them

| risk | what happens | avoid it |
|---|---|---|
| extra balls | a second ball appears mid-clip | one ball in the start frame, "one ball" in the prompt, short clips |
| the ball glued to the foot | it moves with the boot instead of being struck | start the clip just before contact, describe the ball's path after contact |
| leg swapping | left and right legs trade places mid-stride | frame from the knees down, real speed, one action per clip |
| foot through the ball | the boot passes through it | start mid-backswing, keep the contact in the first second |
| the colourway drifts | the boot turns another colour or model over a clip | strong product reference on the still, 5 s clips, re-check with qa.py |
| the net not reacting | the ball stops at the net like a wall | generate the bulge in the still and ask for the net to shiver and settle |
| grass swimming | the turf texture crawls | a lot of motion blur or shallow focus on the background |
| faces | a face morphs between frames | frame without faces, from behind, or in silhouette |
| sole drag instead of a cut | the model's default for "drag the ball" | describe the inside of the boot on the side of the ball, and say the sole never steps on it |

## Ad grammar seen in football boot launches

- Product first, in the dark: a macro on the boot in rain or smoke before anything moves.
- Short holds (6-20 frames) through the build, longer holds on the hero strike and the
  packshot.
- One big strike with a ramp to slow motion and a hit (flash, chromatic kick) on contact,
  often on the music's drop after a beat of silence.
- A match cut from the ball leaving the boot to the ball arriving in the net or another
  place.
- An anonymous or silhouetted player when the boot is the star; a named star when the ad
  sells the player.
- The end card: the logo, the boot's name, a short line.
