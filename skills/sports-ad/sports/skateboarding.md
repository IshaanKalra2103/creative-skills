# Skateboarding

What a skateboarding ad has to get right, and what image and video models get wrong. Shot
names refer to `references/shots.md`; transitions to `references/transitions.md`.

## Movements

Each movement: the phases in order, how long they take at real speed (30 fps), the body cues
that make it read as real, the frame where the product shows best, and how to shoot it.
Frame counts are approximate (approx.) unless a source or measured figure is named beside them.

Stance: a regular skater has the left foot forward, a goofy skater the right foot forward.
The front foot sits over or just behind the front bolts, the back foot on the tail, the
shoulders roughly parallel to the board's length. State the stance in every prompt and keep it
the same across shots.

### Ollie (the main movement)

| phase | frames at 30 fps | body |
|---|---|---|
| roll and crouch | 6-12 | Knees bend and the hips drop; the arms swing low; the back foot's ball is on the tail's edge, the front foot centred behind the front bolts. |
| pop | 2-3 | The back foot snaps the tail down to the ground; the board's nose rises; the skater springs upward with both legs. |
| slide and level | 4-6 | The front foot slides up the griptape toward the nose, its side dragging the board up; the knees come up toward the chest; the back foot follows. At the peak the board is level to the ground, with the feet still on it. |
| peak | 2-3 | The board is level, the knees tucked, the arms out; the body is above the board. |
| landing | 3-5 | The legs extend, the feet land over the bolts, the knees absorb the impact by bending, the trucks and wheels hit together. |
| roll away | 3-6 | The skater rolls out, balanced, the arms settle. |

- The whole ollie is about 0.5-0.7 s (15-20 frames, approx.; no published timing found). An ordinary ollie rises 30-60 cm (approx.; lab studies use a 45.7 cm platform); an
  ollie up a stair set or over a gap rises 70-100 cm. The board stays attached to the feet
  because the front foot drags it and the back foot pushes on the tail; the board never leaves
  the soles by a gap.
- Hero frame: the peak, with the board level and under the feet, the wheels visible, or the
  pop: the tail striking the ground with the front wheels rising.
- Tells that it is fake: the board floats above the feet with no contact, the feet not on the
  board at the peak, the board nose-up at 45 degrees during the whole flight, the skater
  upright and straight-legged, no knee tuck, the feet standing in the middle of the deck.

### Kickflip

| phase | frames at 30 fps | body |
|---|---|---|
| crouch and pop | 6-8 | As in the ollie, but the front foot is angled with its toes pointing off the edge of the board. |
| flick | 2-3 | The front foot slides up and flicks off the edge of the board with the toe, in a diagonal line toward the front corner. |
| rotation | 6-9 | The board spins one full turn about its length (360 degrees) while the skater's legs tuck; the board goes round under the feet, the griptape alternates with the graphic. |
| catch | 2-3 | The feet come down onto the board as the griptape faces up again, the back foot catches the board first, then the front foot. |
| landing | 3-5 | Both feet on the bolts, knees bent, the wheels meeting the ground together. |

- The board flips once about its long axis, in roughly 0.3 s (about 9-10 frames at 30 fps, approx.), so at 30
  fps it is seen in only 9 or so positions; a 60 fps or higher capture is cleaner. A kickflip
  and a heelflip spin the board in opposite directions (the kickflip is flicked with the toe off
  the front edge, the heelflip with the heel off the back edge); do not mix them, and name one
  per clip.
- Hero frame: the board in mid-flip, edge-on to the camera, the skater above it with
  the legs tucked; or the catch.
- Tells: the board rotating about the wrong axis (a shove-it spin about the vertical axis),
  more than one flip, the board flipping with the skater's feet far above it, the grip and
  graphic both facing up, the board coming down upside down.

### Grind (50-50 and others)

- Ollie up to a ledge or rail, land with the trucks (the axles), not the wheels, on the edge
  or on the bar; the board slides along; the body weight is centred over the board, the knees
  bent, the arms out for balance, the eyes ahead. A 50-50 grind has both trucks on the
  surface; a 5-0 only the back truck; a boardslide has the middle of the deck on the rail. The
  slide lasts 15-60 frames. Wax on a ledge makes a streak; a metal rail can throw a few
  sparks if the trucks are steel and hard, but sparks in a skate ad are a stylised effect.
- Rails: a typical handrail is 0.9 m high and a round bar about 5 cm in diameter; a stair rail
  slopes down; a flat rail is level; a ledge is a concrete or granite block 0.4-0.5 m high.
- Hero frame: the moment of lock-in, the trucks on the edge with the wheels in the air, the
  shoes on the deck.
- Tells: the wheels spinning on the rail, the board floating a hand above the rail, the board
  clipping through the rail, the skater's feet detached, the rail ending at a random point.

### Manual

- A balance on the back two wheels with the nose up (a nose manual: the front two). The back
  foot presses the tail down and the front foot holds the nose a few centimetres to 20 cm up;
  the arms move to correct; the knees are slightly bent and the hips shift. A manual along a
  straight line lasts 30-90 frames. The skater is rolling, not standing.
- Tells: the nose too high (like a wheelie), the front wheels in the air with the skater
  upright and stiff, a board that doesn't move.

### Drop-in

| phase | frames at 30 fps | body |
|---|---|---|
| set | 15-30 | The tail on the coping, the back foot on the tail, the front foot on the deck near the front bolts, the front wheels over the edge. |
| commit | 3-5 | The skater leans forward and over the board, presses the front foot down, the back foot leaves the tail. |
| drop | 3-6 | The board lands on the transition, the knees bend deeply, the arms out, the weight over the front foot. |
| ride | 10-30 | The speed builds down the transition. |

- Tells: a hesitating lean backward, the wheels not touching the transition, the tail off
  the coping.

### Carve

- A wide, flowing turn: the skater leans into the turn (the toe side or the heel side), the
  board tilts about 10-25 degrees and the trucks pivot, the knees bend, the arms out to the
  sides. In a bowl or a pool the carve goes up the wall with the wheels in contact, to a lip
  where the board touches the coping, then back in a quick swoop. 15-40 frames per turn.
- Hero frame: the board tilted up the wall with the skater leaning in, and the wheels at an
  angle to the ground.

### Push

- The back foot on the ground pushes the board forward in repeated kicks, the front foot on
  the board near the bolts, the board rolling: a cadence of about 1 push every 20-30 frames
  and a glide in between; the rear foot lands beside the board after the push, then the rear
  foot returns to the tail. Mongo pushing uses the front foot on the ground instead.
- Tells: both feet on the board while moving without momentum, the pushing foot on the wrong
  side, a skater who runs beside the board.

## Props and setting

- The deck: a 7-ply maple board, 7.5-8.5 in (19-21.5 cm) wide, 28-33 in (71-84 cm) long,
  wheelbase 13.5-15 in (34-38 cm), with a concave, a kicktail at the rear and a nose at the
  front; the top is covered in griptape (a black sandpaper-like surface) and the bottom has
  the graphic. Keep the graphic the same in every shot.
- The trucks: a pair of T-shaped metal axles with a hanger and a base plate, kingpin bolt
  and bushings; a width matched to the deck; they sit on the baseplate bolted through the
  deck with four bolts each.
- The wheels: urethane, about 50-60 mm in diameter (street, commonly 52-54 mm) or larger (cruising), about 99A hardness for
  street (commonly 99A-101A); four wheels with bearings; they leave scrapes and a rumble on concrete.
- The shoes: a flat, grippy vulcanised rubber sole with a thick toe cap, stitched and glued;
  suede or canvas uppers; the shoes wear down at the toe where the ollie drags (grip tape
  burns) and the side. Lock the colourway by reference. A skate shoe is a product hero.
- Places and obstacles: a street with a curb, stairs (a 3-set, 5-set or a 10-set), a handrail,
  a flat ledge, a bank, a manual pad, a gap; a skatepark with a bowl, quarter pipe, a pool, a
  concrete plaza; a vert ramp with a metal coping and a deck; a parking lot at night; a
  warehouse; a rooftop. Surfaces: concrete (smooth or rough), asphalt, wood, ceramic tile
  (slippery), marble ledges, painted metal.
- Other: a helmet (some), knee pads, a backpack, a skate tool, a can of wax, a crew with
  a camera, a fisheye lens, a boombox.
- Weather and light that read well: low sun through a plaza with long shadows, sodium lights
  at night with wet pavement, an overcast day with soft light, a dusk with neon signs, rain
  that clears with puddles for spray.

## Product hero moments

For shoes:
- The ollie's pop, the shoe on the tail and the shoe on the griptape with the wear.
- The grind seen at the edge: the shoe's sole next to the ledge, the wax mark.
- The lace-up on the curb, the toe cap in macro, the flick on a kickflip, the sole's pattern.
- The landing: the sole flexing and the knees bending.
- The packshot: the shoe on a griptape slab on black.

For decks: the graphic in the air when the board flips, the underside facing camera in the
flip; the wood plies seen at the edge in macro; the board leaning on a wall in a studio.

For wheels and trucks: the wheels spinning in macro and the rumble of the bearings; the trucks
on a rail; the hanger's metal with scratches.

For apparel: a loose T-shirt in the wind, a beanie, the baggy pants in the carve, a jacket's
logo (brand-owned), a backpack.

## Shots that work for skateboarding

| shot | for |
|---|---|
| ground-level tracking shot beside the push or the roll | speed, wheels and shoes together |
| low front dolly back (leading) as the skater rolls at camera | energy, the skater growing in frame |
| macro push-in on a shoe or a wheel on the concrete | the product's first read |
| low side angle on the ollie or kickflip, ramp to slow motion | the hero moment |
| fisheye low-angle close shot at the feet | the classic skate video look, the board in the lens |
| through an object (a railing, a fence, a stair's gap) | reveals and transitions |
| top-down drone on a skatepark or a bowl | the bowl as a graphic shape, scale |
| follow-cam behind the skater from the waist down | the line through the street |
| low behind, walking away toward the light | the outro |
| studio packshot on black | the end card |
| handheld tracking with a moving camera (like a skate video) | authenticity, rhythm |

## Sound

- Wheels on concrete: a continuous low rumble that changes with the surface (smooth,
  crack-filled, a joint thump every few metres); on tile a high whir.
- The pop: a sharp "crack" of the tail on the ground; the board's landing: a "clack" and a
  rattle of the trucks.
- The grind: a long rasp (a scrape of metal on concrete) and a rail's ring; a ledge slide is a
  smoother scrape with wax.
- Push: a rhythmic "tk-tk" of the shoe on the ground; a bail: the board skittering away and
  rolling to a stop.
- Voices: a friend's yell ("go!") and cheering, a camera's whirr; city sounds: traffic, a siren.
- Edit: let the sound of the wheels carry the first section; cut the music at the pop, hit on
  the landing.

## Video-model risks and how to avoid them

| risk | what happens | avoid it |
|---|---|---|
| the board detaches from the feet | the board floats away from the shoes, or the shoes pass through the deck | start the clip at the peak or the pop with the shoes on the board; a short clip (3-5 s); "feet stay on the board"; avoid flips that need the feet to leave |
| the board flips wrongly | it spins about the vertical axis, flips twice, or turns into a different object | a kickflip in a still sequence: generate the mid-flip as a still with the board edge-on and push in; or avoid the flip and use the ollie; prompt "the board rotates once about its length" |
| the wheels | wheels merge, vanish, or stay still while rolling | a close-up still of the underside; motion blur on the wheels; "four wheels" |
| the trucks | the trucks float or the axle bends | frame the trucks only in a grind still; keep the camera on the shoes |
| stance swap | the skater changes from regular to goofy | state the stance and the camera side; keep one skater |
| sliding feet | the board rolls without the skater's push, or the feet slide | a tracking shot with a moving background; ground blur |
| rails and ledges | the rail passes through the board, ends in the air, or changes height | a locked camera; a still with the rail's full length visible; shoot a close macro of the contact |
| the griptape and graphic | the top and the bottom swap | the graphic shown in the start still and the griptape on top; avoid long flips |
| the landing | the skater lands on the sole of the board or the board vanishes | cut before the landing, or show the landing in a still |
| faces and hands | extra fingers, a face morph | a helmet or cap, a back view, a silhouette |
| bails | the body folds in unnatural ways | do not show a bail in a product ad |

## Ad grammar seen in skate ads

- Skate-video style: fisheye lens, handheld, a lo-fi look, a rough cut on the sound of the
  wheels; a crew and a friend filming.
- Lines rather than single tricks: a push, an ollie, a manual and a grind in a sequence on a
  street, in one long take or cut on the board's sound.
- Short holds (6-15 frames) on the tricks; slow motion for the flip; a freeze frame on the
  peak with the shoe visible.
- A product beat of the shoe's wear: scuffs, griptape burns, holes in the toe.
- A soundtrack of punk, hip hop or lo-fi, with the music hitting on the land.
- Anonymous skaters in the city at night; named pro skaters in a talking head for the
  brand's team ads.
- The end card: the logo, the product's name, a short line.
