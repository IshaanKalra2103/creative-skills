# Golf

What a golf ad has to get right, and what image and video models get wrong. Shot names refer
to `references/shots.md`; transitions to `references/transitions.md`.

## Movements

Each movement: the phases in order, how long they take at real speed (30 fps), the body cues
that make it read as real, the frame where the product shows best, and how to shoot it.
Frame counts are approximate (approx.) unless a source or measured figure is named beside them.

### Full swing with a driver or iron (the main movement)

| phase | frames at 30 fps | body |
|---|---|---|
| address | 30-90 | Feet shoulder-width (wider for the driver, narrower for a wedge), knees flexed, the hips pushed back, the spine tilted forward about 30 degrees from the hips, the arms hanging down, the club on the ground behind the ball. The ball is forward in the stance for the driver (off the lead heel) and centred for a short iron. |
| takeaway | 8-10 | The club, hands, arms and shoulders move back together in one piece, the clubhead low and outside the hands; at hip height the shaft is parallel to the ground and the toe pointing up. |
| backswing to the top | 12-18 | The wrists hinge, the lead arm stays across the chest, the shoulders turn about 90 degrees while the hips turn about 45, the lead shoulder under the chin, the weight loads on the trail leg. At the top the shaft is parallel to the ground (pros) and pointing at the target, the lead wrist flat. |
| transition | 2-3 | The hips start to move toward the target before the arms finish, the club shifts to the inside, weight to the lead foot; the club stays hinged. |
| downswing | 5-6 | The lower body leads: the hips clear (rotate open) and the arms drop; the club approaches from the inside, the wrists still hinged until the last moment, then released. |
| impact | 1 (the ball is on the face for about half a millisecond) | The hands ahead of the ball for an iron, the clubhead square to the target line, the lead leg braced and straight, the hips open about 40 degrees, the head behind the ball. |
| follow-through | 8-12 | Arms extend toward the target, then the club wraps around the lead shoulder; the chest faces the target, the trail foot comes up on its toe, the weight is on the lead foot and the belt buckle faces the target; held in a balanced finish. |

- Tour players take about 0.75 s for the backswing (22-24 frames) and about 0.25 s from the top
  to impact (about 3:1 tempo); average golfers are slower, about 0.8-1.0 s back (24-30 frames)
  and 0.25-0.3 s down (8-9 frames). The table's transition and downswing together are the
  7-9 frames from the top to impact. A PGA Tour driver clubhead speed is about 51 m/s (about
  114 mph; individual leaders about 118-119 mph), a recreational male golfer about 42-43 m/s
  (94-97 mph). The ball is gone from the face in under 1 ms,
  so at 30 fps it is never seen in contact; the frame shows either the club before or the divot
  and the ball already away.
- Hero frame: impact is not visible at 30 fps; use the transition at the top (the coiled
  body, the shaft parallel to the ground) or the finish (the balanced pose, the club behind the
  shoulder). A ramp to slow motion across impact is best shot at a high frame rate.
- Tells that it is fake: a swing with the club going back on a straight line like a pendulum
  and the body still, a right-hander with the club in the left hand in a left-hand pose, feet
  together, the head following the ball up too early, the club shaft bending, the finish with
  the weight on the back foot, an upright spine with the arms stretched out.

### Putt

| phase | frames at 30 fps | body |
|---|---|---|
| read | 150-450 | Crouching behind the ball to read the break, a plumb-bob gesture, the walk to the ball. |
| address | 30-60 | Eyes over the ball, the arms hanging, the shoulders and arms forming a triangle, the putter's face square to the line; practice strokes. |
| backstroke | 10-18 | The shoulders rock like a pendulum, the wrists quiet, the head still. |
| forward stroke | 8-14 | The putter accelerates through the ball, the stroke length about equal to or a little longer than the backstroke, the putter face square. |
| finish | 20-60 | The head stays down, still looking at the spot where the ball was, until the ball is well on its way; then the head turns to follow it. |

- A long putt rolls slowly and decelerates: a 10 m putt leaves the putter at about 3 m/s and
  takes roughly 5-6 s to stop (approx.; calculated for a fast green where a ball released at
  about 1.8 m/s rolls 3 m). Rotation (the ball
  rolls end-over-end, not a skid) shows the dimples as a slow turn.
- Hero frame: the putter's face and the ball at rest, a macro at ground level with the grass
  blades and the line; the ball dropping into the hole with a drop of the flag.
- Tells: a stroke in which the wrists flick, the head lifting before the ball is hit, a
  skidding, sliding ball, the ball curving without a slope.

### Chip and pitch

- A chip: a narrow stance with weight on the lead foot, the ball back, the hands ahead, a
  short stroke by the shoulders with little wrist hinge; the ball lands on the green's fringe
  and rolls. 10-14 frames from the start to impact, a short follow-through that stops.
- Pitch: a longer stroke with a wrist hinge to waist height and a finish at chest height.
- Tells: a wrist flick that scoops the ball, the weight on the back foot, a full finish.

### Bunker shot

| phase | frames at 30 fps | body |
|---|---|---|
| setup | 30-60 | The feet dig into the sand for stability, a wide stance, an open stance and open clubface, the ball forward, the weight on the lead side. |
| swing | 12-18 | A full-length swing with a steep path. |
| impact | 1-2 | The club enters the sand 4-5 cm behind the ball and slides under it, never touching the ball; the sand lifts a ball-sized chunk. |
| explosion | 6-12 | A fan of sand ejects from the face and carries the ball with it, the club continues to a full finish. |

- Hero frame: the explosion of sand with the ball emerging at the top, the clubhead out of
  the sand cloud, in side profile, shot in slow motion.
- Tells: the ball struck directly (no sand), a small puff of sand that goes nowhere, a
  closed face, the player standing on firm ground.

### Walk-up and reaction

- The golfer's walk down the fairway with the bag, a club resting on the shoulder; a tip
  of the cap, a fist pump after a made putt; a ball marker placed on the green with a coin.

## Props and setting

- The ball: at least 42.67 mm (1.68 in) in diameter and at most 45.93 g; a white (or yellow or
  orange) urethane or surlyn cover with 300-500 dimples (commonly about 330-400) in a regular
  symmetrical pattern; a logo and number printed on one side. Keep the same ball, with the
  brand text unreadable or locked from a reference.
- The tee: a wooden or plastic peg, commonly 2.75-3.25 in (70-83 mm) long for a driver (the rules cap it at 4 in); a
  driver ball sits so that half of it is above the top of the face.
- The clubs: a driver with a head up to 460 cc, a shaft of graphite or steel, a total length
  up to 48 in (122 cm); irons 3-9, wedges and a putter; each club has a head, a hosel, a shaft
  and a grip with a rubber texture. The driver has a large rounded head with a flat face; the
  iron has a thin blade with grooves. The shaft is a straight, thin tube: it flexes in the swing
  but does not bend like rubber.
- The course: tee box, fairway (short, striped grass with mowing patterns), rough (long grass),
  bunker (white or pale sand with a raked edge), water hazard, green (very short, smooth, with
  contour), fringe, the hole cut 108 mm (4.25 in) in diameter and about 10 cm deep, a metal
  cup, and a flagstick (typically 2.1-2.4 m, with a pennant flag); the pin position is marked.
  Yardage markers, sprinklers, a cart path.
- Other: the golf bag, the cart, a towel, a glove (one hand only), cleats (spikes or spikeless
  soles), a visor or hat, a range finder, a divot of turf, a ball marker, the scorecard.
- Places: a links course by the sea with dunes, a parkland course with trees and mist, a
  driving range at night under lights, a rooftop simulator bay, a mini-golf, a desert course, a
  studio with a patch of green.
- Weather that reads well: low sun with long shadows on the fairway, morning mist, dew on the
  grass, wind-swept grass, an overcast day with soft light, light rain.

## Product hero moments

For clubs:
- The clubface in macro with the grooves and the logo, a golf ball resting at the face.
- The swing through the ball in slow motion from a low angle, the divot flying.
- The head cover removed; the shaft in a rack; a club dropped on the green.
- The clubhead on the grass, dew on the face.
- The packshot: the club on black or a patch of turf, rim-lit.

For balls: the dimples in macro, the ball dropping into the cup, the ball on the tee, the ball
against the sky in flight with a contrail effect, the ball compressing at impact (high-speed
reference), a ball being marked.

For shoes: the cleats on the green, wet grass on the toe, the lace-up, the spikes biting
through the follow-through, the stable posture.

For apparel and gloves: the glove being pulled on, the fabric in the wind during the finish,
the hat brim with sun, the quarter-zip, the sweat-wicking texture.

## Shots that work for golf

| shot | for |
|---|---|
| ground-level tracking shot beside the walk to the ball | rhythm, shoes and grass in frame |
| low front dolly back (leading) as the golfer approaches the camera | calm confidence |
| macro push-in on the clubface or the ball on the tee | the product's first read |
| low side angle on the swing, ramp to slow motion at the downswing | the hero moment |
| behind the tee, following the ball into the sky | the payoff of a strike |
| top-down drone over the hole | the green as a graphic shape, scale |
| through an object (the flag, a tree's leaves, the bunker's grass edge) | reveals |
| tunnel walk: walking out from the clubhouse | anticipation |
| low behind, walking away toward the light | the outro, the sun low on the fairway |
| studio packshot on black | the end card |
| ball-cam: a camera at ground level looking up at the club | the downswing and the divot |

## Sound

- The strike: a crisp "tock" for a driver, a sharp "click" for an iron, a dull "thud" for a
  mis-hit; the club whooshes through the air before it.
- The putt: a soft "tick"; the ball dropping in the cup is a hollow "plunk".
- The bunker: a dull muffled "whump" and a spray of sand grains.
- Quiet: birds, wind, a distant mower, footsteps on grass, the clink of clubs in a bag, a
  zip, a Velcro glove strap.
- Crowd: restrained applause and a hush; "quiet please", a whisper.
- Edit: drop the music out for the address, let the swish and the strike carry the hit.

## Video-model risks and how to avoid them

| risk | what happens | avoid it |
|---|---|---|
| a bending club | the shaft bends like rubber, or the head detaches | keep the club in motion blur at the downswing; a clear still of the club; frame a close-up of the face and stop before the swing; "a rigid straight shaft" |
| the club passes through the ball | no contact | start with the ball already in flight, or cut at the strike and show the ball in the sky |
| extra balls and clubs | two balls on the tee, two clubs in the hands | "one golf ball on one tee", one club in the start still, 5 s clips |
| the ball vanishes | a drive disappears | follow with a locked camera, cut to the ball's landing on the fairway |
| hands and grip | extra fingers, the grip wrong, a left hand and a right hand swapped | frame from behind and below, the golfer in silhouette, a macro on the grip at rest |
| the swing direction | the golfer swings the wrong way, or the club goes through the body | a still that shows the stance and the target line; "right-handed golfer, the target to the left of the frame" |
| the swing phases | the backswing looks like a baseball swing or a tennis stroke | prompt phase by phase; use the top of the backswing as a still and push in |
| grass and flags | the grass crawls and the flag is a random shape | shallow focus and a locked camera |
| the cup | the cup is drawn as a hole in the green without a rim | a macro still of the cup with a cup liner |
| faces | a face morphs between frames | frame from behind or in silhouette, a cap or visor |

## Ad grammar seen in golf ads

- Slow, quiet, premium: wide landscapes at the golden hour, the sound of wind, a hush.
- Product first: a macro on the ball or the clubface before a frame of the course, then the
  golfer.
- Long holds (45-120 frames) on the setting and the address; the swing in slow motion at the
  top with a ramp to the strike, a hit on contact, then the ball in the sky.
- A match cut from the ball in the sky to the ball in the cup, or from the ball to a moon or a
  sun.
- Humour and a casual tone appear in ads aimed at younger players; reverence in heritage ads.
- Voice-over or a line of text about precision, patience, a single moment.
- The end card: the logo, the product's name, a short line.
