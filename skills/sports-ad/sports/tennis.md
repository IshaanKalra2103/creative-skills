# Tennis

What a tennis ad has to get right, and what image and video models get wrong. Shot names
refer to `references/shots.md`; transitions to `references/transitions.md`.

## Movements

Each movement: the phases in order, how long they take at real speed (30 fps), the body cues
that make it read as real, the frame where the product shows best, and how to shoot it.
Frame counts are approximate (approx.) unless a source or measured figure is named beside them.

### Serve (the main movement)

| phase | frames at 30 fps | body |
|---|---|---|
| stance and bounces | 30-90 | Feet shoulder-width behind the baseline, the ball bounced several times, a breath. |
| toss and wind-up | 15-20 | Both arms go down together, then the tossing arm rises straight up and releases the ball at its full reach; the racquet arm lifts back. |
| trophy | 3-5 | Knees bent, the tossing arm still pointing up at the ball, the racquet behind the head, the elbow high, the body coiled sideways. |
| racquet drop | 4-6 | The racquet falls behind the back ("back scratch") as the legs drive up. |
| acceleration to contact | 3-5 | Legs extend, the hips and shoulders rotate, the arm extends up to the ball at full reach. |
| contact | 1 | Racquet face meets the ball at the highest point, the arm fully extended, the body in the air, the eyes up on the ball. |
| follow-through | 6-10 | Racquet carries down across the body to the opposite hip, the back leg swings through into court, the player lands on the front foot. |

- A pro's toss peaks a little above the contact point (the ball is already dropping when it
  is struck), roughly 3.2-3.6 m up; contact is at about 2.8-3 m for a tall man (both approx.).
  From the trophy to contact is about 0.3-0.4 s (9-12 frames, approx.). A men's first serve
  averages about 185-195 km/h (115-120 mph) on the ATP tour and strong servers reach
  210-230 km/h (130-143 mph); the tour record is 253 km/h (157 mph, Isner) and the fastest
  recorded 263 km/h (Groth, Challenger). The ball travels from the server to the returner
  in roughly 0.4-0.6 s and is a streak at 30 fps.
- Hero frame: contact, the ball squashed against the strings with the strings deflecting;
  or the trophy pose from below with the shoe's sole and the sky behind.
- Tells that it is fake: a toss that is thrown rather than lifted (a high arc to the side),
  both feet planted and the knees straight at contact, the racquet in front of the face
  throughout (no racquet drop), a contact point below the head, the ball hit while the eyes
  are down, the toss arm dropping early.

### Forehand

| phase | frames at 30 fps | body |
|---|---|---|
| unit turn | 4-6 | The shoulders and hips turn sideways, the racquet back with the non-hitting hand on the throat. |
| backswing and load | 4-6 | The racquet loops back and drops, the knees bend, the weight goes to the back foot. |
| forward swing | 3-4 | Weight transfers forward, the hips lead, then the shoulders, the arm, the racquet; the racquet drops below the ball and accelerates. |
| contact | 1 | In front of the front hip, the arm slightly bent, the racquet face vertical, the wrist laid back, the head still and looking at the contact point. |
| follow-through | 6-10 | The racquet finishes over the opposite shoulder (modern) or extends toward the target, the body rotates through. |

- From the start of the forward swing to contact is about 0.1-0.2 s (approx.; pelvis and trunk
  rotation peak about 0.06-0.09 s before impact in pros). Topspin: the racquet
  moves low to high, and the ball rotates forward on its top. Pro racquet-head speed at
  impact is about 32-34 m/s (115-125 km/h).
- Hero frame: contact or just after, the strings loaded; or the finish with the wrist and the
  racquet over the shoulder.
- Tells: the racquet moving flat with no low-to-high, the ball struck beside the body at hip
  level with no turn, the ball struck behind the body, a high backswing with the elbow
  pointing to the sky, the non-hitting arm hanging.

### Backhand (two-handed and one-handed)

- Two-handed: the dominant hand on the grip, the second hand above it, the shoulders turned so
  the back faces the net, the weight loaded on the back foot; contact in front of the body,
  both arms extended and the racquet face vertical; the finish high over the shoulder. 4-6
  frames of the forward swing.
- One-handed: the non-hitting hand holds the racquet's throat to the end of the backswing,
  then lets go as the hitting arm extends. At contact the arm is straight, the chest sideways,
  the free arm extends back for balance. The finish is up, the racquet pointing to the sky.
- Tells: a two-handed grip with the hands apart on the handle, a one-handed backhand with the
  second hand hanging, the chest facing the net.

### Volley

- Short and compact: a quick split step, a short forward punch with the racquet in front of
  the body, the wrist firm, the elbow in front, no backswing. Contact 3-5 frames after the
  start. The ball is a block with a small follow-through, the knees bent.
- Hero frame: contact at the net, the racquet face angled, the net tape behind.

### Split step

- A small hop at the moment the opponent hits the ball, both feet landing together shoulder
  width, then a push off one foot toward the ball. The hop is 5-8 frames long, a few
  centimetres high; the first step follows in 2-3 frames.

### Sliding on clay

- Run toward the ball with long strides, plant the outside foot and let it slide 0.5-1.5 m on
  the surface, knee bent, the hips low, the other leg out for balance, the racquet already
  back; the clay builds up in front of the shoe in a small wave and a cloud of dust. A slide
  takes 10-20 frames, ending as the stroke hits.
- Hero frame: the slide with the shoe's inside edge dragging a line in the clay and the dust.
- Tells: sliding on a hard court, the sole flat and not edge-on, no dust, a straight leg.

## Props and setting

- The ball: 6.54-6.86 cm across, 56.0-59.4 g, yellow-green felt over rubber, a curved white
  seam making a figure-eight on the surface. The felt has fuzz that sheds and flattens with
  play; new balls are fluffy, used balls are flat and, on clay, dusty and orange. Use one
  ball per shot and keep it the same colour.
- The racquet: head typically about 645 cm^2 (100 square inches; a common size, not a rule), a total
  length of 68.6-73.7 cm (27 in standard; the ITF maximum is 29 in / 73.7 cm, 31.7 cm wide), a graphite frame with a head shape of oval/rounded-rectangle,
  the strings in a grid of typically 16 mains by 19 crosses (18 by 20 is also common), a grip wrapped with an overgrip, a
  butt-cap at the end. Strings are a single flat plane with an even grid; a racquet's logo
  and string pattern are the first thing a model gets wrong.
- The court: 23.77 m long, 8.23 m wide for singles (10.97 m for doubles); the net 0.914 m high
  at the centre, 1.07 m at the posts; the service line 6.40 m from the net; the baseline is
  the back line; the white lines 5 cm wide (baseline up to 10 cm). Surfaces: red clay (crushed
  brick dust, which marks where the ball lands), grass (short green, wearing to a brown patch
  at the baseline), hard court (acrylic on asphalt or concrete in blue, green or other
  colours with a lighter surround). Indoor hard courts have a covered ceiling and a flat light.
- Shoes: court shoes with a herringbone sole for clay, a flatter sole for grass (with small
  nubs) and a durable sole for hard courts. Lock the colourway by reference.
- Other: a ball kid, a chair umpire's high chair, the net cord, the towel, a wristband,
  a headband, a bag with racquets, a ball machine, a water bottle, sunglasses, a visor.
- Places: a stadium with a closed roof, an outdoor club court with a hedge, a clay court
  with a coloured sunset, a rooftop court, a public park court with a cracked surface and a
  chain-link fence, a courtyard at night under floodlights.
- Weather that reads well: low sun with long shadows over the clay, rain pausing the match,
  heat shimmer, floodlights with insects and mist.

## Product hero moments

For racquets:
- The strings loaded at contact, the ball deforming against them (a macro, high speed).
- The frame in macro with the carbon weave, the grommets and the logo.
- The racquet spinning on the clay line, dust motes in light.
- The grip being wrapped, a hand re-gripping at the baseline.
- The packshot: the racquet on black, the strings lit from behind.

For shoes: the slide on clay, the sole's herringbone, the lace-up, the ball kid's feet on a
court line, a sprint to a drop shot.

For a ball: the fuzz in macro, the seam spinning, a new can opened with a puff of air, the
ball hitting the line and raising a puff of chalk or clay.

For apparel: a skirt or shirt in the wind, sweat on a headband, the toss from below with the
shirt riding up, a hat in the sun.

## Shots that work for tennis

| shot | for |
|---|---|
| ground-level tracking shot beside a player running to a ball | speed, shoes and ball in frame |
| low front dolly back (leading) as he runs at camera | menace, the player growing in frame |
| macro push-in on the strings or the frame | the product's first read |
| low side angle on the serve or forehand, ramp to slow motion | the hero moment |
| behind the net, through the net's mesh | the ball arriving and the player's reaction |
| top-down drone on the court | the lines as a graphic pattern, scale |
| through an object (the strings, the net, the fence) | a transition from the ball to the next scene |
| tunnel walk from behind | anticipation, the court light at the end |
| low behind, walking away toward the light | the outro |
| studio packshot on black | the end card |
| ball-cam: a camera low at the baseline looking up at the toss | the trophy pose from below |

## Sound

- The ball on strings: a sharp, tight "pock" with a ring on a hard hit; softer on a drop shot.
  A serve cracks.
- The bounce: a dry thud on clay with a puff of dust; a hard court gives a sharper bounce;
  grass gives a soft, dull thump.
- Shoes: a long scrape on a clay slide; squeaks on hard courts; a soft pad on grass.
- Grunt or exhale with the stroke, a deep breath before the serve, the ball bounce count.
- The net cord: a quick "tick" and a rattle; the post: a metallic tap.
- Crowd: a hush before the serve, applause after the point, an umpire's call ("out", "let").
- Edit: the quiet is important. Drop the music for the toss and hit the contact.

## Video-model risks and how to avoid them

| risk | what happens | avoid it |
|---|---|---|
| strings melting | the strings blur into a smear or a fabric, the grid changes between frames | a strong racquet reference, the strings seen at an angle and in motion blur, a short clip; use a macro still with a clear string bed and keep the camera still |
| racquet shape change | the head becomes round, doubled or its handle bends | the racquet in the start still with a clear head and handle; "one racquet" in the prompt; 5 s clips |
| extra balls | two balls in the air, or a ball that never lands | one ball in the still and the prompt; avoid the toss and contact in one clip |
| the ball vanishes | at serve speed the ball disappears or hangs | start the clip at the trophy and cut at contact; the ball in flight shown as a blur or cut to the net |
| two-handed backhand | the hands merge or swap | frame from the legs and the racquet, behind or in silhouette |
| arm swap or extra arms | a toss arm duplicates | one action per clip; from a low angle with the arm out of frame |
| lines and net | court lines swim, the net mesh crawls | a locked camera, the net out of focus, a shallow depth of field |
| clay and dust | the slide lacks dust, or the dust floats like fog | describe "a short cloud of orange dust, settling in a second" and put dust in the still |
| faces | a face morphs between frames | frame from behind or in silhouette, a hat or visor |
| left and right | a right-hander becomes left-handed mid-clip | state the dominant hand in the prompt and keep the same side |

## Ad grammar seen in tennis ads

- Product first, quiet: a macro on the strings or the shoe on the line, a bounce sound over
  silence.
- Long holds on the serve ritual (bounce, breath), short holds in the rally build, an impact
  at contact.
- Slow motion at the toss and contact; the ball and the strings as the visual centre.
- A rally cut to the rhythm of "pock, pock"; the ball as a metronome.
- Contrasts: the stadium and the park court, the star and the child, the clay and the grass.
- Silhouettes and low sun; athlete close-ups with a held gaze are common in apparel ads.
- The end card: the logo, the product's name, a short line.
