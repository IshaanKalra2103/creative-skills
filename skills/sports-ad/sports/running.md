# Running (track and road)

What a running ad has to get right, and what image and video models get wrong. Shot names
refer to `references/shots.md`; transitions to `references/transitions.md`.

## Movements

Each movement: the phases in order, how long they take at real speed (30 fps), the body cues
that make it read as real, the frame where the product shows best, and how to shoot it.
Frame counts are approximate (approx.) unless a source or measured figure is named beside them.

### Sprint start from blocks (the main movement)

| phase | frames at 30 fps | body |
|---|---|---|
| on your marks | 30-60 | Hands behind the line, fingers and thumb in an arch, feet in the pedals of the blocks, the front knee over the line, the rear knee on the track, head down, the neck relaxed. |
| set | 30-60 | Hips rise above the shoulders, the front knee about 90 degrees, the rear knee about 120-135 degrees, weight forward on the hands, shoulders slightly ahead of the line. Stillness. |
| gun and reaction | 3 | A legal reaction time is at least 0.100 s (3 frames); elite reactions are 0.12-0.18 s (4-5 frames). Hips push up and forward, hands leave the track. |
| drive phase | 20-40 | The first step roughly 60-90 cm (approx.), a very low forward lean at about 45 degrees, the rear leg swinging through low and forward, the arms driving hard (elbows 90 degrees), the foot landing behind the hips. The head stays down for the first 3-4 steps. |
| transition | 40-60 | Strides lengthen, the torso rises gradually; the body is close to upright by roughly 30 m (approx.). |

- Elite 100 m: about 9.6-10.0 s men, 10.6-11.0 s women. About 41-47 steps; 4.3-5.0 steps a
  second at top speed (a step every 6-7 frames). Each foot is on the ground for about 0.09-0.10
  s (about 3 frames).
- Hero frame: the set position from the side (the arch of the hips, the hands, the spikes in
  the blocks), or the first drive step, the rear foot leaving the pedal.
- Tells that it is fake: the hips lower than the shoulders in the set, hands in front of the
  line, upright posture on the first step, the feet not in the blocks, the head up looking at
  the finish at the start, blocks facing the wrong direction (pedals angled toward the line).

### Max-velocity sprinting form

- Tall posture, a slight forward lean from the ankles, the hips high, the chest open. The knee
  drives up to about hip height, the foot of the swing leg dorsiflexed (toe pulled up) and under
  the hip; the foot lands under the centre of mass on the ball of the foot, the heel never
  touching down. The arm drives at about 90 degrees: the hand goes from hip to cheek height, the
  fingers relaxed, never a fist across the chest.
- Stride: about 2.2-2.6 m for an elite sprinter at top speed. Flight phase: both feet are off
  the ground for about 3-4 frames out of each step cycle (about 6-7 frames) at 10-12 m/s. Contact is 2-3 frames.
- Hero frame: mid-flight, the knee up, the spike's plate and pins visible, or the contact with
  a puff of rubber dust. A ground-level tracking shot at the sprinter's speed, side-on.
- Tells: heel-striking, a bouncing head, arms crossing the midline, knees low (a jog), the
  hands high above the shoulders, a back-leaning torso, shoulders hunched, a neck forward.

### Distance running form

- Upright but with a slight forward lean from the ankles; a relaxed face and shoulders; the
  arms bent at about 90 degrees swinging forward and back (the hands to the hip, the elbow
  back), not across the body. Cadence: 170-190 steps a minute for most runners (a step every
  about 10 frames at 180 steps a minute); a foot contact is about 0.2-0.25 s (6-8 frames) at distance pace.
- The flight phase is short (2-4 frames), the knee lift is lower than in sprinting, the stride
  is shorter.
- Hero frame: the foot just before landing (the toe up, the sole to camera) or at midstance
  with the shoe flexing; for a road shoe, the midsole compressing.
- Tells: bouncing too high, arms across the chest, overstriding with a straight leg far in
  front, a stiff upright body, a shuffle with no knee lift in a "fast" run.

### Foot strike

- Heel strike: the heel hits first, ahead of the centre of mass, common in recreational
  runners at slow paces. Midfoot: the foot lands flat under the hip. Forefoot: the ball of the
  foot, the heel drops down lightly (sprinters stay on the ball). Most road runners land on the
  heel or midfoot, most track runners on the midfoot or forefoot.
- Hero frame for a shoe: the strike frame from a ground-level side angle, the midsole
  compressed and the upper flexing. About 3-6 frames at slow motion (ramp to 0.1-0.25x) fills
  a whole beat.
- Tells: a toe-landing in a jog, a heel that stays high at a slow pace, a landing foot
  rotated outward or inward.

### Hurdles

| phase | frames at 30 fps | body |
|---|---|---|
| approach | 6-9 | The final stride before takeoff is shortened; the torso comes forward. |
| takeoff | 2-3 | The lead leg drives up and straight, the knee leading, the foot toe up; the trail leg stays behind. The takeoff is 2 m from the hurdle (110 m). |
| clearance | 4-6 | The torso lunges forward, the opposite arm reaches toward the lead foot, the trail leg is pulled through sideways (knee up, ankle flexed) and the knee goes over at a right angle. |
| landing | 2-3 | The lead leg comes down fast on the ball of the foot, the trail leg lands in the next stride. |

- Men's 110 m hurdles are 1.067 m (42 in) high; women's 100 m 0.838 m (33 in); men's 400 m
  0.914 m and women's 400 m 0.762 m. Ten hurdles in a race, 9.14 m apart in the 110 m (13.72
  m to the first), three steps between hurdles. The hurdle's L-shaped base weights the
  barrier so it tips if hit, and the top bar is white with black or coloured stripes.
- Hero frame: the clearance, the lead foot over the bar and the trail leg's knee at a right
  angle to the torso.
- Tells: jumping over (a vertical hop) instead of a flat lunge, the trail leg dangling, a
  hurdle hit that does not move, an extra hurdle, the hurdle without the base.

### Finish dip

- In the last stride the runner leans the torso forward, the arms swing back, the chest dips
  toward the line; the torso (not the head, arms, legs or hands) must cross the finish line
  plane first. 4-6 frames of lean. The face is open mouthed, in effort.
- Hero frame: the chest on the line with the finish tape or the line plane, the numbers on
  the bib. At a distance race the arms rise in a gesture.
- Tells: a fall instead of a lean, the finish line passed by the head, a tape pulled the
  wrong way.

### Starting out and warm-up

- Tying a shoe (the lace pulled through the loop), a stretch, a deep exhale at the line,
  bounces on the toes. These are quiet product beats with no motion risk.

## Props and setting

- The track: 400 m for one lap in lane 1 (two straights of 84.39 m and two curves of 115.61 m), lanes 1.22 m wide
  (1.25 m maximum), 8 or 9 lanes, white lines 5 cm wide, a polyurethane or rubber surface
  in red/terracotta, blue or other colours with a slight grain and a spring. The finish line
  is at the end of the home straight, a 5 cm white line with the photo-finish camera.
- Blocks: two metal footplates on a rail with adjustable pedals, angled so that the feet push
  back at about 45-70 degrees.
- Spikes: a lightweight shoe with a plate of 6-8 pins (no longer than 9 mm on a synthetic track, 12 mm for high jump and
  javelin, per the World Athletics rule as summarised by SATRA; indoor venues often limit pins to
  6 mm) in the forefoot; track spikes may have a sole no thicker than 20 mm since 1 November 2024
  (World Athletics); the sprinter's spikes are stiff and flat, the
  distance spikes have a foam midsole. Road shoes have a thicker foam midsole (a stack height
  up to the 40 mm road-race limit, commonly 30-40 mm), a rubber outsole, and often a rocker shape; trail shoes have deep lugs.
  Never put spikes on a road scene or road shoes on a track start.
- Kit: a singlet, split or brief shorts, a bib (number pinned at four corners) on road races,
  a hip number on the track, a watch, a headband, a cap, a visor, sunglasses, compression
  socks, a race belt, and gels.
- The road: asphalt with painted marks, tape across the finish, a marathon (42.195 km), the
  distance markers, a barrier with banners, a water station with cups on the ground, a pacer.
- The trail: a dirt single track in a forest, mud, rocks and roots, a mountain ridge, a
  desert, a coastal path.
- Places: an empty stadium at dawn, a city at 5 am with a streetlight, a bridge, a beach at
  sunrise, a cold morning with breath steam, a treadmill in a gym.
- Weather that reads well: low sun with long shadows and flare, rain on asphalt with
  reflections, fog, snow, heat haze, a night run under street lights with a headlamp.

## Product hero moments

For shoes:
- The foot strike, the midsole compressing, in slow motion at ground level.
- The spike plate: a macro of the pins biting the track, rubber dust rising.
- A macro on the upper's mesh in rain, with water beads running off it.
- The lace-up on the line, the hands pulling the laces.
- The sole's pattern left in mud or wet sand; footprints in the first snow.
- The packshot: the shoe on black, rim-lit, or on a slab of asphalt with a painted line.

For apparel: the singlet's fabric rippling in wind, the sweat map on a shirt, the reflective
trim catching a headlamp or car light, the zip pocket, the cap's brim with rain on it.

For a watch or a tracker: the wrist swinging at the stride, the screen readout (hold it
unblurred at a rest frame; do not trust a model to render digits).

## Shots that work for running

| shot | for |
|---|---|
| ground-level tracking shot beside the stride | speed, both shoes in frame |
| low front dolly back (leading) as the runner charges camera | menace, the runner growing in frame |
| macro push-in on a shoe on the track or the road | the product's first read |
| low side angle on the foot strike or the start, ramp to slow motion | the hero moment |
| behind the finish line, the tape breaking | the payoff of the race |
| top-down drone on the track | the lanes as a graphic, scale, a runner in lane 4 |
| walking away toward the light | the outro, a dawn run |
| through an object (the starter's blocks, a chain-link fence, a bridge's railings) | reveals |
| tunnel walk from behind | anticipation of a stadium |
| studio packshot on black | the end card |
| car-mounted tracking shot at the runner's pace on a road | distance, rhythm |

## Sound

- Breathing is the music: in, out, in time with the stride. Rhythmic heartbeat for the set
  position.
- Footfalls: a light slap on a track, a heavy pad on asphalt, a crunch of gravel on a trail,
  a squelch in mud. Spikes on a track make a sharp rhythmic "tick" with a slight scrape.
- The starter: "on your marks... set..." and the gun (a sharp crack, an electronic beep in
  modern starts); the lap bell.
- Wind, a crowd's roar in the stand, a distant cheer on the road, a pacer's call.
- Shoelace pull: a quick zip; velcro; a zip of a jacket.
- Edit: drop the music for the set position (silence is the tension), hit on the gun.

## Video-model risks and how to avoid them

| risk | what happens | avoid it |
|---|---|---|
| leg swapping | left and right legs trade places mid-stride, a third leg appears | frame from the knees down or from behind, real speed, 5 s clips, one runner |
| the feet slide | the feet skate across the ground with no contact | a side-on tracking camera moving at the runner's speed, with the ground blurred |
| a moonwalk or treadmill | the runner steps without moving forward | a tracking shot that follows the runner, not a static camera, a background that moves |
| lane lines warp | lane lines bend, merge or end | a locked track angle; a shallow depth of field and a blurred track |
| spike confusion | the shoe changes between a spike and a road shoe | the shoe in the start still, a close-up reference, "track spikes with a pinned plate" |
| the colourway drifts | the shoe turns another colour or model | strong product reference on the still, 5 s clips, qa.py |
| arm swing | the arms swing with the same leg, or merge into the torso | ask for "opposite arm and leg", frame so the arms are partly out of frame |
| blocks | the blocks melt into the feet or face the wrong way | start the clip from the set position, with the blocks defined in the still |
| the crowd | repeated faces, melting people | out of focus or a distant crowd, an empty stadium |
| the bib | digits and text change | a bib seen from behind and out of focus, or blank |
| rain and breath | the breath steam looks like smoke | "faint breath mist, quickly dispersing" |
| faces | a face morphs between frames | from behind, in silhouette, a cap and sunglasses |

## Ad grammar seen in running ads

- A single runner in a long take: a dawn run through a quiet city, the world waking up, with
  the cadence as the rhythm.
- Product first: a macro on the shoe's foam or the spike plate before any motion, on the
  breath sound.
- Short holds (6-20 frames) in the build, with cuts on the foot strike; long holds (60-120
  frames) on the start in the set position and on the final stride.
- A voice-over or text overlay of a number: a pace, a distance, a time.
- The solitary runner as a lonely figure in a landscape (the wide shot), contrasted with
  crowds in the stadium or a mass road race (the top-down drone).
- Slow motion at the foot strike; speed ramps from slow to fast at the start gun.
- The end card: the logo, the shoe's name, a short line.
