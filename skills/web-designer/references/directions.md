# Directions

A direction is the set of decisions that makes every page and screen of a
product feel like the same object. Without one, each section is designed
from defaults and the result averages into slop.

**The hex values and fonts below illustrate a register. They are not a
palette to copy.** Two sites that both lift a value from this file are the
same site. Derive every value from your own concept.

## How to choose

1. **Write three concept sentences**: "<Product> is a <thing>." Fill each
   with something from the real world, never an adjective, and take each from
   a **different source family**:

   | Family | Examples |
   |---|---|
   | Printed matter | a race card, a seed packet, a field guide, a zine, a museum label, a vinyl sleeve's back, a pharmacy leaflet |
   | Objects and materials | a ceramic glaze, an enamel sign, a Polaroid, a deck of cards, a terrazzo floor, a sticker sheet, a mechanical watch, a tool catalogue |
   | Places | a public pool, a night market, a planetarium, a corner bakery at 6am, a ski lift station, a radio studio, a hardware store |
   | Media | a weather broadcast, a Saturday cartoon, a film title card, an arcade cabinet, a teletext page, a museum audio guide |
   | Nature | a tide chart, moss, a sunrise over water, a beehive, a herbarium sheet, a coral reef, a weather front |
   | Play | a board game, a pinball table, a toy kitchen, a puzzle box, a gachapon machine, a paper fortune teller |

   At most **one** of the three may be paper-and-ink (a printed form on an
   off-white ground with mono labels). That is the current default and this
   skill's known rut (slop.md §8).

   **Discard your first idea.** For each family, write down the first thing
   that comes to mind, then cross it out and use the second or third. The
   first association is the average association: tested runs given the same
   invoicing brief independently reached "a station departure board" twice.
   Boards, timetables, tickets and receipts are where every model goes first.
2. **Map each concept to a direction below**, or invent one with the same
   fields. The three must land on different **grounds** (light, dark, colour
   field or image), different **type**, different **richness sources** and,
   for sites, different **grammars** (layout.md).
3. **Render all three** on the real first screen (SKILL.md Step 2), then pick.
4. **Write the tokens** for the winner. Every page reads from them.

The category never picks the direction. Invoicing can be Soft tactile; an
architecture studio can be Colour field; a dev tool can be Object world.
Fighting the category's default look is often where the memorable site is.

## A. Product as hero

*Linear, Stripe, Raycast, Apple product pages.* The product itself, rendered
large, real and in motion, is the image. Everything else gets out of its way.

- **Type:** one strong sans with real weight contrast, or a sans with a
  distinctive width axis; tabular numerals wherever the product shows data.
- **Colour:** taken from the product's own UI. Often dark, but not by default.
- **Layout:** the product at 70 to 100% of the viewport width on the first
  screen, cropped boldly; sections that zoom into one part of it.
- **Motion:** the product doing its job (a list filling, an invoice going
  paid), scrubbed or looping, never decoration.
- **Its own slop:** a fake "product card" that is a pretty rectangle with
  three lines in it. The product shot must be the real product, at a size
  where you can read it.

## B. Editorial

*The New York Times Magazine, Stripe Press, The Browser Company essays,
Kinfolk.* The site as a publication: a typeface with a voice, a strong grid,
and the confidence of space.

- **Type:** a serif with character at display sizes (Newsreader, Source Serif
  4 Display, Gloock, Young Serif, Zodiak, Gambetta); a plain sans or the
  serif's text cut for reading. Big headlines, real long-form measure.
- **Colour:** a page ground that is *not* warm off-white by default: cool
  newsprint, white with black ink and one spot colour, a deep ink-blue page,
  a single saturated cover colour per section.
- **Layout:** columns, pull quotes, captions, images with credits, drop caps
  if earned.
- **Motion:** almost none. Page-like.
- **Its own slop:** The Paper Edition (slop.md face 1). Rotate the ground,
  drop the italic accent word, and earn the serif.

## C. Instrument

*Bloomberg Terminal at its calmest, Flighty, aircraft displays, Teenage
Engineering manuals.* Dense, exact, legible, built for people who use it all
day.

- **Type:** a sans designed for small sizes (IBM Plex Sans, Public Sans,
  Atkinson Hyperlegible) or a mono with character (Martian Mono, Commit Mono,
  Fragment Mono) for figures; tabular figures always.
- **Colour:** a deep ground or a bright one, neutrals stepped in four levels
  of one hue, one signal colour that means "act now".
- **Layout:** tight grid, aligned columns, labels above values, dividers
  instead of cards, nothing decorative.
- **Motion:** numbers that tick, lines that draw, rows that slide into place.
- **Its own slop:** sci-fi HUD cosplay (glows, scan lines, cyan). Real
  instruments are calm.

## D. Swiss poster

*Railway signage, Josef Müller-Brockmann, Vignelli's subway map, Experimental
Jetset.* Grid, type and one or two flat colours, at poster scale.

- **Type:** a neo-grotesque or a grotesque with a wide family (Archivo,
  Schibsted Grotesk, Familjen Grotesk, Switzer). Two weights. Flush left.
- **Colour:** white or one bold ground, black, one primary.
- **Layout:** a strict 12-column grid used visibly, huge numerals, extreme
  scale contrast, no rounded boxes.
- **Motion:** snaps along the grid axes.
- **Its own slop:** sterile. One element of play or warmth in the content.

## E. Soft tactile

*Partiful, Poolsuite, (Not Boring) Software, Arc's welcome pages.* Chunky,
physical, joyful, made by one hand.

- **Type:** a rounded or soft display face (Fredoka, Chillax, Baloo 2, M PLUS
  Rounded) or a heavy characterful grotesque, big and tight.
- **Colour:** two or three saturated flats from one family on a tinted ground,
  hard offset shadows, bevels, pressed states.
- **Layout:** big targets, stickers, objects you poke.
- **Motion:** springs, squash on press, confetti only at a real milestone.
- **Its own slop:** kindergarten Memphis. Restraint in the number of colours
  is what makes it crafted.

## F. Colour field

*Apple's colourful product launches, Spotify Wrapped, Pentagram's poster
sites.* One saturated ground fills the screen; the type sits on it like a
poster.

- **Type:** big. 96 to 200px display moments, tight tracking, black or white
  only.
- **Colour:** one bold ground per section or state (tomato, cobalt,
  chartreuse, signal orange), ink picked by contrast.
- **Layout:** poster compositions, type as the image, few elements.
- **Motion:** the ground changing between sections as you scroll.
- **Its own slop:** colour for no reason. Each ground must mean something
  (a step, a category, a time of day).

## G. Gallery

*Museum sites, Aesop, Leica, photographers.* Images do all the talking.

- **Type:** small and quiet; one display size used rarely.
- **Colour:** bone, black or a material colour; no accent, or a tiny one.
- **Layout:** full-bleed and generous, captions like wall labels.
- **Motion:** slow cross-fades and zooms.
- **Its own slop:** empty and cold. The imagery must be extraordinary.

## H. Field manual

*Field Notes, park maps, seed catalogues, technical manuals, blueprints.*
Utility printed on a material, with a hand-made trace.

- **Type:** a condensed sans for headings, a typewriter or mono for data.
- **Colour:** a material ground (kraft, blueprint blue, graph green, slate)
  with one stamp colour.
- **Layout:** rules, grids, stamps, labels, coordinates, filled-in fields.
- **Its own slop:** skeuomorphic costume, and the studio portfolio
  (slop.md §8). Suggest the material through colour and rule.

## I. Object world

*Apple Vision Pro pages, Spline-heavy launches, Bruno Simon.* One hero
object, rendered in 3D or as a lit illustration, reacts to the scroll and the
pointer.

- **Type:** heavy and characterful, set against the object.
- **Colour:** the object's materials decide it (enamel, glass, chrome, felt).
- **Layout:** the object owns the first screen; text in few, large blocks.
- **Motion:** the object turns, opens, assembles with the scroll.
- **Its own slop:** generic glossy blobs. The object must be *this* product
  or a specific thing from the concept. Needs real assets or a carefully
  built CSS/SVG/three.js object.

## J. Cinematic

*Apple TV+, A24, Airbnb Experiences, Criterion.* Full-bleed photography or
film is the ground; type sits on it like a title card.

- **Type:** a display face with presence, white on image, large.
- **Colour:** sampled from the images; each section tinted by its picture.
- **Layout:** image behind everything, text on scrims, few words.
- **Motion:** slow parallax, video that scrubs with the scroll.
- **Its own slop:** stock footage with a headline on it. The images must be
  the user's own or art-directed as one shoot.

## K. Interface nostalgia

*Poolsuite FM, Teenage Engineering, early Mac OS, teletext, Winamp skins.*
The site is a piece of software from another era, played straight.

- **Type:** a pixel or bitmap-era face for display (Silkscreen, Pixelify
  Sans, VT323, Redaction), a plain UI face for reading.
- **Colour:** the palette of the era (platinum grey, teletext primaries,
  beige plastic), used exactly.
- **Layout:** windows, title bars, menus, desktop icons, with real modern
  accessibility underneath.
- **Motion:** instant, stepped, no easing.
- **Its own slop:** nostalgia as a costume over a template. The metaphor
  must organise the content (each window is a product, the menu is the nav).

## L. Retail counter

*Muji, Apple Store, Dieter Rams catalogues, a good hardware store.* The
product range, laid out like a well-run shop: clear, priced, touchable.

- **Type:** a precise sans with excellent numerals; prices as big as names.
- **Colour:** the products' own colours on a quiet ground.
- **Layout:** a catalogue grid with real filters, generous product images,
  specs in tables.
- **Motion:** quick, responsive, add-to-basket feedback.
- **Its own slop:** the Shopify theme. The shop needs one idea of its own
  (how it sorts, how it shows a product, how it talks).

## Web-app directions

Apps take a direction too, but it lives in the material, the density and the
shell more than in a hero:

- **Instrument** (C) for operators and analysts: dense, dark or bright,
  one signal colour.
- **Calm document**: Things, iA Writer, Notion at its best. White or
  paper, one superb text face, the content is the interface, chrome
  appears on demand.
- **Workshop**: Figma, Linear, Raycast. Neutral surfaces, the user's
  content provides colour, keyboard-first, panels that dock.
- **Tactile tool**: a consumer app with personality (E): chunky controls,
  colour for state, sound-like motion.
- **Map-led / canvas-led**: the map, board, timeline or canvas is the
  ground; everything else floats on it.

## Inventing a direction

Fill the same fields: **source (and its family), type, colour, richness
source, layout or grammar, motion, its own slop.** Then check it is not just
one of the above with a new name.
