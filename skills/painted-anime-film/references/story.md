# Story progression

These films are short (45–75 s), have no dialogue, and are about one change: a world or a being goes from one state to another. The grammar below is about function, not order. Build each new film's beat sheet from its own premise.

## From premise to beat sheet

1. **Name the change in one line:** *before* → *after*. For example, "a sleeping guardian wakes and flies" or "a grey valley gets its colour back".
2. **Find the signature moment:** the single image that *is* the premise (the thing the title promises). It goes at 60–75% of the runtime and gets the biggest shot. Show the event itself, not the room it happens in.
3. **Choose a witness:** something small and tender that gives scale and lets the audience feel the change, such as a bird, a flower, a lamp or a leaf. It appears early and is changed by the end.
4. **Write each beat as one picture plus one change.** "A grey lantern, then a drop of dew falls past it" is a beat. "The ghost feels hopeful" is not.
5. **Assign the functions below,** then order them in whatever way serves this story. Merge or drop functions freely.
6. **Time it:** list shot lengths, check the total, and shorten toward the climax.
7. **Run the no-copy check** (below).

## Functions a beat can serve

| Function | What it does | Example forms |
|---|---|---|
| Stillness | sets the world before the change; lets the eye rest | establishing wide, a held insert, ambient life only |
| The sign | the first small thing that is different | a flicker, a ripple, eyes opening, a sound |
| The witness | a small life that notices | a bird lands, a flower turns, an animal looks |
| Stir | the change begins in the body | a tremor, a breath, a first movement |
| Emergence | the subject comes into the world | rising, unfolding, stepping out, waking |
| Regard | the subject looks at something and wants it | a look up, a look down at the world, a look at its own hand |
| Contact | the subject touches the world and the world answers | a hand on a flower, a foot on grass |
| Gathering | anticipation before the release | light builds, wind pulls in, a crouch |
| Release | the signature moment | ignition, a wave of colour, a leap, a song |
| Transformation | the world is visibly different | colour spreads, lamps light, flight |
| Coda | the changed world, quiet again; the witness changed | a held wide, a small detail, then fade |

## Shape

- **The first quarter is quiet.** Let the world exist before anything happens. Sound and motion are minimal.
- **Build in steps, not in a ramp.** Each step is a new beat that answers the previous one. Insert a hold between steps; a pause before a big beat makes it big.
- **The climax is short and loud.** Use quick cuts, camera motion and the brightest value of the film.
- **The coda is long and quiet,** and echoes something from the start, changed, without restaging the opening shot.

## The no-copy rule

Learn the aesthetic and the motion grammar from a reference, never its beat order. Before building, write the new film's beats and the reference's beats side by side. Any run of three or more beats with the same functions in the same order, or any shot that is a re-skin of a reference shot (same staging, same camera, same effect), must be changed. Reorder it, change the function, or stage it differently (a different angle, a different effect, a different witness).

The reference film's sheet (*The Guardian of the Morning Forest*), for that check:

| 2 | bird | witness | a songbird lands on its shoulder |

| 8 | lookup | regard | head lifts toward the sky |
| 9 | gap | regard | POV up through the canopy gap |
| 10 | ignite | gathering | jets cough, then ignite |
| 11 | windring | release | top-down ring of wind across the forest |
| 12 | launch | release | launches on ones, smears, roll |

Re-skins to avoid in particular: a bird landing as the witness, a light on the face as the sign, a top-down ring as the release, a launch into the sky as the transformation, and a leaf settling in the empty place as the coda.
