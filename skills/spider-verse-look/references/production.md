# Production: how the film was made, and who said what

From the art book (PDF page numbers) and the making-of video (timestamps; full notes in `video-making-of.md`). Use it for context, captions and credits, not for the look itself.

## Origin

- Amy Pascal (then Sony Pictures chief) and Avi Arad wanted the first big-screen animated superhero film, "a four-quadrant animated movie that was made for everyone" (PDF 012). Arad: "Everyone was going from animation to live-action… I think we should do it the other way" (PDF 012).
- The 2014 Sony hack leaked emails about an animated comedy from Phil Lord and Chris Miller. After the Feb 2015 Marvel deal moved live-action Peter Parker to the MCU, the animated film was Sony's "leftover project" (video 01:00).
- Lord and Miller's conditions: the lead is Miles, and the story builds on the 2014 *Spider-Verse* comics event (video 03:10). Lord: "We decided we were in if the movie didn't center on Peter Parker" (PDF 013). After seeing the Jeff Koons retrospective he pitched "a post-modern Spider-Man" (PDF 013).
- Brian Michael Bendis (foreword, PDF 010–011) co-created Miles with Sara Pichelli in 2011. His idea: if Peter dies heroically enough, he becomes the new Spider-Man's "Uncle Ben".

## The look, and the people who made it

- **Alberto Mielgo** (production designer from 2015, later credited as visual consultant) painted alone for months on an empty floor, reading Spider-Man comics; Sony flew him to off-limits New York rooftops "so he could stand where Spider-Man would stand" (video 04:30–05:00). "His vision put us on the path" (Belson, PDF 014). He left mid-production (video 12:30); his spray-painted Miles logo is still on the merchandise.
- **Justin K. Thompson** (production designer) pushed Mielgo's start further "by leaning into the comic book language" (PDF 015). **Dean Gordon** (art director) on value banding, darkness and light bleeds (PDF 016). **Danny Dimian** (VFX supervisor, Imageworks) on misregistration for focus and breaking the computer's perfection (PDF 017). **Josh Beveridge** (animation supervisor) on frame modulation (PDF 017).
- Directors: **Bob Persichetti** (animation; ex-Disney *Tarzan*, sculptable rigs), **Peter Ramsey** (boarded act three), **Rodney Rothman** (the writer who became a third director, working in the edit) (video 09:10, 15:00).
- Character design: **Shiyoon Kim** (2D), **Omar Smith** (3D), paint by **Wendell Dalit**, **Yashar Kassai**, **Robh Ruppel**, **Naveen Selvanathan**. Visual development: Patrick O'Keefe, Yuhki Demers, Peter Chan, Zac Retz, Neil Ross, Craig Mullins, Jesús Alonso Iglesias, Dean Gordon, Paul Lasaine. Colour script: Dave Bleich (PDF 030). Head of story: Paul Watling.

## How hard it was (video)

- Two animators spent about a year on 10 seconds to nail the look; animators averaged one foot of film (16 frames) per person per week; about four times longer than normal (10:40).
- Twos broke CG pipelines; Imageworks rendered a hidden in-between frame just to keep cloth simulation stable (07:20). Face lines were drawn per frame, then predicted by an ML model trained on artists' drawings and corrected by artists (17:10).
- ~2,800 shots, 2–3× a typical feature; three panels on screen for three seconds = nine seconds of animation (18:20). The crew grew from ~60 to ~177 animators, Imageworks' largest (17:55).
- The collider finale needed six styles at once, "making five movies at once" (18:40). The whole third act was redone less than a year before release (15:40).
- The 2017 teaser's building drop was the only finished animation in existence at the time (14:30).
- Result: $35M opening, A+ CinemaScore, ~$400M worldwide on a $90M budget; Best Animated Feature over *Incredibles 2*, the first non-Disney/Pixar win since *Rango*; Peter Ramsey the first Black director to win the category (20:30). *Puss in Boots: The Last Wish* and *TMNT: Mutant Mayhem* followed its lead (22:10).

## Story beats (book chapters)

Foreword → Introduction → Setting the Scene (Manhattan, Brooklyn, RIPeter) → Who Is Miles Morales? (family, Aaron, graffiti, subway) → Blurred Vision (Visions Academy, sticky powers, the comic) → Miles Meets the Spider-Men (depot, Collider, Goblin, funeral, MJ, Peter B.) → Out of the City (Alchemax, Doc Ock, the forest, Gwen) → Spider Team Suit Up (the hideout, Ham, Peni, Noir) → On the Prowl (Prowler) → The Battle (Aunt May's, Queens, Scorpion, Tombstone, Aaron's death) → Fisk's Fiasco (Kingpin, Vanessa, glitches, quakes, the portal) → Scene Spotlight: Swinging Through the City (the graveyard, the Venom Strike, the train/taxi/police chase) → Conclusion.

## Quotes worth keeping

- "Ideally, we want to be able to stop every frame of the film and have it look like an illustration." — Justin K. Thompson (PDF 017)
- "What if the camera didn't de-focus like a lens? So, we splintered and offset the image in a way that is similar to a misprint." — Danny Dimian (PDF 017)
- "In our artwork we broke down colors and values into defined shapes with short or no transitions." — Dean Gordon (PDF 016)
- "We used dark shapes, with just glimpses of light at times. We always think about what part of the shot we're exposing for." — Dean Gordon (PDF 016)
- "You want crisp pop with aggressive clarity." — Josh Beveridge (PDF 017)
- "We are trying to bring the human hand back into visibility on top of all the amazing technological achievements." — Phil Lord (PDF 039)
- "He sort of resembles a baby deer—he's got skinny legs with wobbly knees and he's got these big hands and feet." — Justin K. Thompson (PDF 037)
- "It was always about seeing how far we could reduce the world around us and how much we could get away with." — Patrick O'Keefe (PDF 118)
- "Nothing is really parallel or truly lines up." — Patrick O'Keefe (PDF 168)
- "One of design's greatest pleasures is stark contrast." — Yashar Kassai (PDF 134)
- "We used all the tricks, light over dark, dark over light, featuring the most saturated colors to track our main characters." — Dean Gordon (PDF 196)
- "He is basically this pure black figure and the most abstracted CG character I've ever seen." — Phil Lord (PDF 176)
- "What the mask says is that when you put it on, you have the heart and soul of a hero." — Avi Arad (PDF 012–013)
- "If it's not broken, break it." — the Imageworks VFX team's motto (video 07:05)
