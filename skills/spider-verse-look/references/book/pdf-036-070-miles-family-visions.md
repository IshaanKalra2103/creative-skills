## Text insights

### Visual philosophy / the "human hand"
- **Every frame should read as an illustration, not a render.** The CG tools are only the substrate; artists must keep the hand-made mark visible on top. (Phil Lord, writer / executive producer, PDF 039.) Quote: *"We are using these contemporary 3D, CG animation tools, but we want every artist to remember that every frame is an illustration of something. We are trying to bring the human hand back into visibility on top of all the amazing technological achievements."*
- **Ground the spectacle in one point of view.** However wild the visual ideas get, each board goes back to Miles' perspective: a 13-year-old who can't believe his destiny. (Miguel Jiron, storyboard artist, PDF 038.) Quote: *"it was helpful to remind myself to always bring it back to Miles and ground all the craziness with his unique perspective."*
- **Design against the power-fantasy body.** The film's message is that strength doesn't need muscle or height, and the designs carry that: a skinny, gangly hero among big adults. (Avi Arad, producer, PDF 038.) Quote: *"you don't have to be muscular and tall to be strong. Everyone is beautiful in their own way, and it always looks better in animation!"*
- **Environments tell the parents' story through set dressing.** Heritage and backstory go into décor details, not dialogue (Justin K. Thompson, production designer, PDF 042; see Environment design).
- **Contrast of worlds is the core environment principle.** Brooklyn is "warm, raw, and lived-in". The Academy is its opposite: "clean, perfect geometric plans and shapes… very cold and sterile" (Peter Chan, development artist, PDF 064).

### Comic-book language seen in this chunk (from the art; the text in this range does not discuss technique directly)
- **Halftone dots used as surface texture, not only as shading.** Examples: the red suit covered in a darker-red dot screen (PDF 037); lighter dots over a saturated yellow chair (PDF 040); dots only in the terminator/shadow band of Jefferson's coat (PDF 051); a fine square dot grid over night shadows (PDF 052).
- **Colour fill drawn off-register from the ink line** (Jesús Alonso Iglesias suit sketches, PDF 038). The grey/red fill shape is shifted a few pixels from the black contour, so white slivers show between line and fill.
- **Magenta/yellow chromatic fringes on hard edges** (Peter Chan kitchen, PDF 042; purple fringe on Aaron's jacket, PDF 054).
- **Classic cartoon/comic symbols in character sketches** (PDF 038): stars circling a dazed head, cloud "poof" puffs for impact, sweat drops, and "!!" stress ticks beside the head.
- **Panel-strip push-in** (Miguel Jiron story art, PDF 039). Five tall, narrow panels with white gutters each crop the same face progressively tighter. The last image breaks out of the frame into open white space, and wavy squiggles above the head show the spider-sense.
- **Duotone / monochrome portrait posters** inside environments: banners on the school façade (PDF 064); halftone manga-face and planet posters in Miles' room (PDF 043).
- **Graffiti as a design system** (Patrick O'Keefe, PDF 059): spray drips, bubble letters, halftone dot patches, speech balloons, triangles and X marks, and the hero shown as a hollow silhouette sprayed with a white outline.
- **Villain POV as a masked scan view** (Zac Retz lighting keys, PDF 063). A binocular/visor-shaped mask frames a monochrome dot-matrix, wireframe view of the tunnel, in one version blue and in another red/magenta with a target arrow.

### Colour & light (from captions and art)
- **Lighting keys are painted per shot with a very limited palette** (Zac Retz, PDF 063). The Prowler chase is almost entirely black + violet + magenta + white flashlight; the only neutral daylight frame is the final one, back in the station crowd.
- **Warm vs. cold is the story palette.** The Morales home is warm yellows, ochres and wood with violet shadows. The Academy is cold white, lime-green and purple ("cold and sterile", Peter Chan, PDF 064). Night scenes with Aaron are deep blue/purple with neon.

### Character design

#### Miles Morales
- **Personality contrast with Peter Parker.** Miles is the child of two present, loving parents and is confident, but has a very different set of problems. (Kristine Belson, president, Sony Pictures Animation, PDF 037.) Quote: *"It's Miles that makes me love this movie… Miles is the child of two present and loving parents. He is confident, and has a very different set of problems to deal with."*
- **Not a genius, a kid.** Peter was a boy genius; Miles "is a kid that wants to be a kid", so his journey is more challenging. (Josh Beveridge, animation supervisor, PDF 037.)
- **Proportion rule: "baby deer".** He should feel unfinished at the start: skinny legs, wobbly knees, big hands and feet. That silhouette makes him stand out in fights next to bulkier characters. (Justin K. Thompson, production designer, PDF 037.) Quote: *"We wanted Miles to feel like he's still developing at the beginning. He sort of resembles a baby deer—he's got skinny legs with wobbly knees and he's got these big hands and feet."*
- **Age: 13** (Miguel Jiron, PDF 038).
- **Reluctant hero framing.** Quote: *"This is a super hero story that is not about fitting the mold or being a genius. This is a story about being a reluctant young hero who wants to be a kid first."* (Avi Arad, producer, PDF 069.)
- **Suit sketches** by Jesús Alonso Iglesias (PDF 038): black/grey suit with red on the shoulders, hands, soles and chest spider. Peter + Miles sketches are also by Iglesias (PDF 039).

#### Rio Morales (mother)
- **Role:** loving, modern Brooklyn mom and hard-working Puerto Rican nurse. She understands Miles' reluctance about Brooklyn Visions Academy but wants him to have the best education (book text, PDF 046).
- **Design brief:** an "authentic everywoman" who clearly resembles her son (PDF 046).
- **Observed, not caricatured.** Rio was built from real people the artists ran into in New York. Her "sensitive and expressive appeal" is echoed in Miles' features, which sells their bond. (Justin K. Thompson, PDF 047.) Quote: *"We tried to create a more observed character with Rio… She has a really sensitive and expressive appeal that we can see echoed in Miles' features."*
- **Credits:** artwork by Dean Gordon and character concept by Alberto Mielgo (PDF 044–045). Final 2D design by Shiyoon Kim, paint by Wendell Dalit (PDF 046). Facial-expression sheet by Shiyoon Kim (PDF 047).

#### Jefferson Davis (father)
- **Backstory:** a tough veteran police officer who struggles to make Miles understand his expectations. He and his brother Aaron used to get into trouble together; when he met Rio and had a child, he went straight and joined the police (PDF 048).
- **Two-sided design brief.** He had to show strength and physicality ("tough-guy persona") and also "great warmth". He is strict because he wants Miles to avoid his old mistakes, but his warm smile "can melt just about any heart". (Justin K. Thompson, PDF 050.) Quote: *"Physically, Jefferson is very imposing, but he is still someone we are supposed to empathize with. His design is a great reflection of these two sides of his personality."*
- **Real-life resemblance:** he looks a lot like director Peter Ramsey's father ("He actually looks a lot like Peter's dad!", Thompson, PDF 050).
- **Body type:** "a wide offensive lineman football player body", contrasted with Aaron's lean basketball-player shape (Thompson, PDF 057).
- **Voice:** Brian Tyree Henry (*Atlanta*) (PDF 054).
- **Credits:** concept art by Alberto Mielgo (PDF 048–049). Young Jefferson and Aaron by Wendell Dalit (PDF 050). Expression sheet by Shiyoon Kim (PDF 051). 2D design by Shiyoon Kim, paint by Yashar Kassai (PDF 051).

#### Aaron Davis (uncle / the Prowler)
- **Role:** "one of the most complex and charismatic characters" in both the original Miles comic and the film. He is the cool uncle and a fun influence until his secret is revealed (PDF 054).
- **Two paths.** He moves between positive role model and "a darker, more disturbing presence"; Aaron and Jefferson are the two possible paths for Miles. (Chris Miller, writer/producer, PDF 054.) Quote: *"He is the guy who teaches Miles that he doesn't have to take everything so seriously all the time… Of course, we find out that he is one of the bad guys, the Prowler."*
- **Designed from the actor.** Most characters were inspired by people on the street, but Aaron is "one of the few characters that resembles the actor that voices him" (Justin K. Thompson, PDF 057).
- **Proportion rule.** Shiyoon Kim (*Tangled, Frozen, Wreck-It Ralph*) drew on Mahershala Ali's "long, thin physique and his tall, thin head". Aaron is a "lean basketball player shape", against Jefferson's lineman bulk. (Thompson, PDF 057.)
- **Voice:** Mahershala Ali (*Moonlight, Alita: Battle Angel*) (PDF 054).
- **Credits:** expression sheet by Shiyoon Kim. Final: 2D design by Shiyoon Kim, 3D design by Omar Smith, paint by Yashar Kassai (PDF 054). Concept by Alberto Mielgo (PDF 055 left). Expressions by Shiyoon Kim and sketch by Shiyoon Kim and Tony Siruno (PDF 055). Early sketches "developing the look and style of Aaron Davis" and concept development by Jesús Alonso Iglesias (PDF 056–057).

### Environment design

#### Morales apartment (Brooklyn)
- **Heritage in the details.** Everything reflects Miles' "warm, multicultural upbringing". Designers put elements of both parents' heritage into the décor (PDF 042).
- **An old classic Brooklyn building.** Rio's Puerto Rican heritage shows in the décor and Jefferson's backstory is "laid out in the designs". Miles' bedroom combines both parents with "a reflection of his artistic style" (Justin K. Thompson, PDF 042).
- **Story beat:** on his first day, Miles is driven away from the old neighbourhood waving at friends, then gets culture shock at how "regimented" the Academy is (Thompson, PDF 042).
- **Credit:** all apartment and bedroom illustrations are by Peter Chan (PDF 040–043).

#### Aaron's apartment
- **A refuge full of forbidden cool.** It's full of things a teenage boy would love and "any sane parent would say no to": throwing knives, katanas, strange electronic gadgets and "an absurdly loud sound system". It is where Miles escapes responsibility, "and in the end, the only place he can learn it!" (Yuhki Demers, vis dev artist, PDF 052.)
- **Credits:** final artwork by Yuhki Demers (PDF 052). Concept collection by Yuhki Demers, Yun Ling, Vaughan Ling and Neil Ross (PDF 053).

#### Elevated subway, graffiti, tunnels
- Captions only: concept of Miles and Aaron by Zac Retz and story art by John Puglisi (PDF 058). Graffiti concept by Patrick O'Keefe (PDF 059). Elevated-station artwork by Neil Ross (PDF 060–061). "Miles explores the subway" concepts by Zac Retz and Yun Ling (PDF 062). Lighting keys by Zac Retz (PDF 063).

#### Brooklyn Visions Academy ("Blurred Vision")
- **Three parts:** school exterior, interior and dorm-room exterior (PDF 064).
- **The goal is the inverse of Brooklyn.** Modern design and materials "cover up the historic stonework from the past, with only a tiny bit showing through the entrance". "Clean, perfect geometric plans and shapes. The overall scheme is also very cold and sterile." (Peter Chan, development artist, PDF 064.)
- **Research:** the team visited big charter schools in Los Angeles and New York and took many photo references (PDF 067).
- **The school's backstory as a design generator.** It is an old Brooklyn building bought by a charter school, which hired an LA architecture firm to modernise it while keeping some Brooklyn charm. "The school is literally a bridge between two worlds… Past, present, and future all coming together in one place. An aspirational place that offers unlimited opportunities that Miles eventually learns to love." The audience should also empathise with parents who "simply want a better life for their kid". (Justin K. Thompson, PDF 067.)
- **Credits:** entrance by Peter Chan and exterior by Neil Ross (PDF 064–065). Uniforms designed by Alberto Mielgo, painted by Dean Gordon; atrium by Chin Ko (PDF 066). Interiors by Peter Chan and Zac Retz (PDF 067). Early building concepts by Tony Ianiro (PDF 068). Classroom and hallway concepts by Robh Ruppel, Bastien Grivet and Jessica Rossier (PDF 069).

## Recipes

1. **"Baby deer" hero rig/proportions.** Build Miles at about 6–6.5 heads tall. Legs and forearms should be thin (limb width ≈ 0.35 × head width). Make hands ≈ 0.9 × head height and feet ≈ 1.1 × head height, with knees angled slightly inward. Put him next to adults at 8–9 heads (Aaron, Jefferson) so the silhouette contrast carries the story.
2. **Two-adult contrast by shape language.** Jefferson: a wide rectangle, shoulders ≈ 3.5 head-widths, square head (h:w ≈ 1.05). Aaron: a tall thin vertical, a long narrow head (h:w ≈ 1.6), arms hanging to mid-thigh. Rio: organic curves and a big black hair mass. Differentiate characters by primitive shape before detail.
3. **Halftone as fabric/surface texture.** For a costume or a big flat plane, fill with the base colour, then overlay a 45° dot grid (pitch ≈ 1/150 of the frame width). In lit areas use dots of a *lighter* tint of the same hue; in shadow, a darker shade. For hero costumes, cover the whole red area with dark-red dots (≈ #771923 on ≈ #c8323f). For heavy clothing (a coat), confine the dots to the terminator band between lit and shadow.
4. **Fill/line misregistration.** Draw the colour fill as one layer and the ink contour as another, then offset the fill by (+3, +2) px per 1000 px of figure height. White slivers should appear where fill and line don't meet. Keep the contour broken, since lines don't need to close.
5. **Chromatic edge fringe.** On hard silhouette edges (cabinets, jacket edges), draw a 2–4 px magenta (≈ #e0409a) line offset to one side and a yellow (≈ #f0e040) or violet (≈ #8050c0) line to the other. Use it sparingly, on a few key edges per frame, not every edge.
6. **Faceted "painted CG" interiors (Peter Chan look).** Shade every object with flat polygon facets, including curved ones: posterise to 3–4 bands and snap them to polygons. No outlines. Cast shadows are hard-edged violet (≈ #5a3c5a) on warm floors (≈ #b8862a). Add sun shafts as translucent pale parallelograms (white at 20–30% alpha). Break some edges into stepped rectangles (a pixel-mosaic fringe, step ≈ 4–8 px).
7. **Comb-edged tone transitions (Neil Ross look).** Between two flat tones, skip the gradient. Draw a row of short parallel ticks (length 6–14 px, spacing 3–5 px) perpendicular to the boundary, in the darker tone, reaching into the lighter one. Use the same trick for hair edges (Mielgo's Jefferson): a black mass with a comb-toothed fringe.
8. **Pixel-mosaic weathering on buildings.** On stone or brick facades, scatter axis-aligned rectangles (sizes snapped to a 4–8 px grid) in 3–4 tones within ±8% lightness of the base. Cluster them around windows and corners. Under raking sun, lit face ≈ #c8703c, shadow face ≈ #1e1414, sky flat ≈ #6f7d88.
9. **Atmospheric city depth without detail.** Background skyscrapers are flat rectangles with no outline. Each depth layer moves toward the haze colour (mauve-pink ≈ #c1a9a0 for a hazy Brooklyn day; blue-grey ≈ #99a2a8 for overcast), and window lights are sparse 1–2 px rectangles. Keep full saturation and detail only in the foreground.
10. **Warm/cool split light for emotional beats.** Set a warm amber key (≈ #d4a04c) from a doorway or practical light on one side and a deep blue fill (≈ #1c2250) everywhere else. Faces split warm/blue along a hard terminator, with an orange rim on hair edges. Leave a third of the frame near-black (≈ #110d14) as negative space.
11. **Night interior with shaped light pools.** Render the room at 80% near-black and blue-violet. Add a fine square dot grid over shadowed walls and 1–3 hard-edged parallelogram light pools on the floor from windows. Pick one saturated accent: neon line art (purple/cyan), a lava lamp (≈ #f06020) or an oxblood sofa (≈ #6a1a2a).
12. **Chase/lighting-key palette.** Limit the frame to 4 values of a single hue family: black ≈ #0d0615, indigo ≈ #271150, violet ≈ #6b488e, magenta ≈ #9a5681, plus pure white for the flashlight and glare. Out-of-focus lights become flat hard-edged white discs at 60–90% alpha. For speed, use white horizontal streaks converging to the vanishing point. End the sequence on a neutral daylit frame for release.
13. **Villain visor POV.** Mask the view with a peanut/binocular shape (two overlapping rounded rectangles). Inside, render the scene as a monochrome dot-matrix (a point grid whose dot size follows luminance) with thin wireframe edges, in blue-violet (≈ #3a2a9a) or red-magenta (≈ #b02050) plus a white target arrow.
14. **Panel-strip push-in for spider-sense.** Split the frame into 5 tall panels (aspect ≈ 1:3.5) with white gutters. Each shows the same face scaled up ~15% more than the last. Draw wavy vertical squiggles above the head in every panel, and let the last image break the panel border into open space.
15. **Graffiti layer kit.** Build a wall from lime tile (≈ #a8b870). Add pink cloud blobs (≈ #e8408a) as a base, then script lettering in yellow (≈ #f0e030) with a violet outline (≈ #4a3aa8) plus vertical drips (random lengths 10–60 px, 2–4 px wide, with round drops at the ends). Scatter orange triangles, X marks and halftone dot patches that shrink toward their edges, plus a spray-mist fuzz (a noise-alpha falloff). The hero can appear as a hollow dark silhouette with a dripping white outline.
16. **Two worlds through palette.** Home/Brooklyn uses warm ochre, honey wood, lemon walls, violet shadows and clutter. School/modern uses white, acid lime ≈ #c0d820, purple ≈ #8a5ac8, giant numerals, symmetric one-point compositions and orthographic elevations with repeated modules. Old parts of the new building keep orange brick ≈ #c0582a and mustard ≈ #d89a30.
