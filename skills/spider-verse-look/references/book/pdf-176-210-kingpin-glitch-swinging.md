## Text insights

### Visual philosophy / comic-book language

- **No motion blur anywhere in the film.** Every image had to stay "crispy" at high speed. To stop fast motion from strobing, they used two things instead of blur:
  - **multiple imagery as in-betweens**: multiple-exposure copies of a limb or head on the fast frames;
  - **backgrounds abstracted into "movement tunnels of color and snowflakes."**

  They wanted "a bolder statement than softening everything." — Bob Persichetti, director, PDF 196/199. Quote: *"The filmmakers also eliminated the idea of motion blurs for the film. That meant all the images needed to stay crispy at high speed… We allowed for multiple imagery as in-betweens and making the backgrounds become more abstract and become these movement tunnels of color and snowflakes. Those elements helped us fight the idea of strobing and all those things that most CG films use motion blur to solve."*
- **Animated on twos.** "The majority of our animation is done in twos instead of ones [images are held for two frames, rather than one] or twelve images per second, instead of twenty-four images. That's rare for CG." — Persichetti, PDF 195.
- **The swinging sequence was a licence to go further:** "abstract the backgrounds, really push the color palettes and lean in to our animation." It was "some of our most expressive, visual statements so far in the film." — Persichetti, PDF 195.
- **Screen printing is the base look, and every effect has to survive it.** Powers FX were judged on whether they worked "with the screen print technique that is one of the defining visuals of the movie", and invisibility had to keep "the screen-printing and the line-work on top of it." — Christian Hejnal, VFX producer, PDF 182. *Takeaway: the halftone/print pass and the ink-line pass sit on top of the FX layer, not underneath it.*
- **Cubism is the visual model for parallel worlds intruding.** Space is fractured, and each fracture shows the same place at a different angle, scale and rendering. — Dean Gordon, art director, PDF 187. Quote: *"Cubism seemed a natural idea since it represents multiple views of a scene within a single picture frame. So in our version, space is fractured, and within each fracture we see a different angle, scale, and rendering, of the same place. Each fracture shows a parallel but slightly different version of our world existing in another universe and now trying to resolve itself in ours."*
- **The portal is perspective unfolded flat.** "That's why visually it looks like abstract art. We have taken perspective, unfolded it and laid it out on the big screen." — Persichetti, PDF 188.
- **Studio mandate:** "subvert expectations and take big chances… surprise people with something that they haven't seen before by incorporating the graphic language of comics." — Kristine Belson, president of Sony Pictures Animation, PDF 203.
- **Homage:** the world weaves in "visual cues that pay homage to the gritty origins of Spider-Man and the Golden Age of comic books." — author (Zahed), PDF 203.
- **Aim at the audience's taste:** "Our goal became about expanding the taste of the audience and getting them to accept and love something that might initially seem different than the other CG-animated movies." Because the property already has a huge invested audience, "we could be riskier with our choices." — Persichetti, PDF 199.
- **Storyboard shorthand (from the art, PDF 198):**
  - radiating "action lines" around a panel;
  - comic speech balloons ("SPIDER-MAN!") used as a staging device, multiplying from panel to panel as a crowd recognises him;
  - spot colour (taxi yellow, suit red/blue) over pencil.

### Colour & light

- **12–14 lighting keys for a 10-block ride.** "Miles and Pete are being towed for ten blocks, so we wanted each block to look differently from the last one… We used twelve to fourteen lighting keys to differentiate each block. The earlier blocks, as they come from Manhattan, are darker and the scenes get lighter as they progress." — Dean Gordon, PDF 195–196. *Rule: step the light through a travelling sequence, with each segment its own colour key and brightness rising over time.*
- **Practical city light as the palette:** "We have stores, neon lights, and street signs casting their light on the streets." — Gordon, PDF 196.
- **Characters must read against busy backgrounds.** "We used all the tricks, light over dark, dark over light, featuring the most saturated colors to track our main characters." — Gordon, PDF 196. *Rule: keep the heroes as the most saturated element, or flip value (dark figure on a bright field, or bright figure on a dark field).*
- **Wet and icy street reflections, shot from a low camera.** "If you're down on the ground with a low-camera angle, you can have this great reflectivity of the lights and cityscapes to heighten the visuals." — Gordon, PDF 196.
- **New York got "this special color palette and visual representation of movement."** — Persichetti, PDF 199.

### Character design

#### Kingpin (Wilson Fisk)

- **Scale:** "at least eight feet tall, seven feet wide", so he is almost as wide as he is tall. — Justin K. Thompson, production designer (the sentence starts on PDF 175 and continues on PDF 176).
- **Concept:** "like this giant black hole that moves through space and time and swallows up everything in the room" (because he is the one who opens the portal). — Thompson, PDF 176.
- **"An infinite source of dark energy."** Monstrous alter ego of the real-estate magnate Wilson Fisk. — author, PDF 176.
- **Silhouette-only design:** "limiting ourselves to just a silhouette but still getting really dynamic performances out of him… This guy's head and his hands sort of float on this void." — Thompson, PDF 176.
- **Custom rig:** "We even created this special rig for him that allowed us to almost pose him independently from his anatomy and get these amazing silhouettes out of our shots." — Thompson, PDF 176. *Takeaway: pose the silhouette as its own shape, not as a consequence of the skeleton.*
- **He dominates every frame and even the camera:** "His physical presence doesn't leave room for anything else. He can just stand there, and everything bends to his will, even the camera. He is basically this pure black figure and the most abstracted CG character I've ever seen." — Phil Lord, producer, PDF 176 (it is also why Kingpin is one of his favourite characters: "he seems to eclipse every one of the frames that he happens to be in").
- Art credits: character sketches by Mark Ackland; facial expression development and character concepts by Shiyoon Kim (PDF 176–177).

#### Vanessa Fisk

- She is Kingpin's wife. Her death is what motivates him to open the portal, and the plan is doomed: "anyone from other universes will be different when they are pulled through into another one." "Kingpin has always been incredibly infatuated with his wife. He is willing to give up everything for her." — Thompson, PDF 178.
- Credits: 3D design by Omar Smith and paint by Yashar Kassai (turnarounds); colour concepts by Paul Lasaine (the car and family-portal panels), PDF 178.

#### Older Peter B. Parker

- **Built as the damaged version of the first Peter.** "Peter's nose is broken. His eyes are swollen and his ears are asymmetrical—even in the CG model that we built. The first Peter Parker is perfect, but then we aged him about fifteen to twenty years and added two decades of Spider-Man wear and tear on him." — Persichetti, PDF 199. *Rule: build wear and asymmetry into the model itself, not just the texture.*
- **Introduction:** Miles is at his lowest point at the gravesite when "a figure emerges from the darkness. At first, we think it's Prowler, but it's the older version of Peter Parker. It's not the version that Miles wanted or needed, but it's the one he is stuck with." — Persichetti, PDF 194.
- **Comic engine:** "the fun of a kid Spider-Man and a grown man Spider-Man… born out of the spirit of the 1989 movie *Weekend at Bernie's*. Peter is unconscious, and Miles is puppeting him as they get dragged through Manhattan." — Persichetti, PDF 194–195.
- **Arc:** "He is forced into this three-legged sack race with Miles, and that's when he remembers why he is a super hero. He finds purpose and meaning in being Spider-Man again." — Persichetti, PDF 199.
- **Showing physical damage:** "You don't see the actual damage illustrated this vividly in animated movies. But the physical toll is something that we were keen on showing." — Persichetti, PDF 199.

#### Miles Morales

- Half-Black, half-Puerto Rican, 13 years old, smart, good-hearted, loving parents, "initially reluctant to follow in Peter Parker's shoes." — author, Conclusion, PDF 203.
- Venom Strike triggers "every time he's nervous." — Hejnal, PDF 182.
- In this sequence he wears the store-bought Spider-Man suit (red/blue with big, cartoonish white eye lenses in heavy black rims), per the art on PDF 193–201.

### Environment design

- **Kingpin's hallway and office** (Patrick O'Keefe, PDF 179–181). The text says nothing about them. See the visual notes: gilded cream/gold palace interior, marble, a colonnade, and a suprematist/constructivist abstract art collection.
- **Brooklyn street for the dimensional quakes** (Robh Ruppel environments, PDF 184–185): overcast, ordinary, low-rise, which makes a quiet stage for the chaos.
- **Graveyard** (PDF 192–195): a snowy Manhattan churchyard at night beside a gothic church. Craig Mullins did the early concept of Peter's grave.
- **Manhattan chase** (PDF 196–201): an above-ground train chase, then police cars and taxis; neon, stores and street signs; snow; wet and icy streets.

### FX

- **Glitch = the same shot rendered through several cameras and styles at once.** "The glitching was a modern invention by our team, using the multiple cameras that mirror the multiple universes. They are all shot on the same character and same animation, but are treated differently, creating this cubist, fragmented look. The glitch happens on characters, buildings, cars because of these dimensional earthquakes as different worlds intersect." — Christian Hejnal, VFX producer, PDF 182.
- **Portal = a 7-camera array, one render style per camera.** "the technical team at Sony Imageworks developed a camera array that allowed them to project seven different angles on to the screen at the same time while enabling them to render each one of those cameras in a different style." Also: "you're looking at seven different locations (the same locations in different universes) from subtly different angles with different render scripts on them. Then we had to develop animation to make all these things smash into each other and resolve into one spot." — Persichetti, PDF 188.
- **Portal design brief:** "It couldn't be a tunnel or a derivative of anything we'd seen in *Doctor Strange*. We really wanted it to represent what would happen when you smash together these subatomic particles and create a tear in the space-time continuum… These multiple universes are trying to resolve themselves through the portal." Other dimensions are drawn to Miles' Brooklyn because the Collider is there. — Persichetti, PDF 188.
- **Dimensional quakes** come from the particle collider tearing the space-time continuum. They "posed many opportunities and challenges for the visual artists." — author, PDF 184.
- **Venom Strike:** "inspired by a 2D anime style, which were brought to a 3D environment by our team… look development and compositing teams worked very hard to get the final look just right, especially to make it work with the screen print technique… it looks like a sharp lightening [sic] effect." — Hejnal, PDF 182. The art shows jagged white-core, cyan-glow bolts that trace the body's contours (PDF 183, 194).
- **Invisibility is modelled on cuttlefish camouflage, made view-independent.** "Our look dev team looked at how cuttlefish are able to appear invisible when they position themselves in front of an object. It's view dependent, so the fish only looks invisible if you are looking at it straight ahead. So, our team had to modify that and come up with a solution so Miles would appear invisible in all directions in a 3D environment, while still maintaining the overall look of the movie with the screen-printing and the line-work on top of it." — Hejnal, PDF 182.
- Dean Gordon "worked on matching the design of Miles' powers to the aesthetic of the film." — caption, PDF 182.

### Pipeline & tech

- A multi-camera render array with a different render script per camera, composited into one frame. It serves both the glitch and the portal (Hejnal PDF 182; Persichetti PDF 188).
- A custom Kingpin rig that poses the silhouette independently of the anatomy (Thompson, PDF 176).
- Look development and compositing teams own the final look of the powers FX (Hejnal, PDF 182).
- The asymmetry of the older Peter is sculpted into the CG model (Persichetti, PDF 199).
- 12–14 lighting keys drive one travelling sequence (Gordon, PDF 196).
- Most animation is on twos; there is no motion blur (Persichetti, PDF 195–196).

### Story & production

- **Nickname:** the graveyard scene, where the older Peter appears, was lovingly called "the arrival of the Fairy Godmother." — Persichetti, PDF 192.
- **Placement:** it is a key first-act sequence just after the collider fight. Miles is "shaken by the shocking death of the classic Spider-Man", becomes aware of the Spider-Verse, and the sequence works as the transition from act 1 to act 2, "where Miles first experiences what it's like to be Spider-Man." — author/Persichetti, PDF 192/199.
- **Plot beats:** Miles accidentally Venom-Strikes Peter, then involuntarily fires a web and they stick together. Police arrive and chase them, the web catches a passing train, and they're dragged "into this wild ride through Manhattan." — PDF 194.
- **Escalation in story:** "whatever we threw at them, they wanted more"; Chris Miller and Phil Lord "really wanted to up the stakes, so we added more of Miles and Peter getting chased by police cars." Watling: "I am a real sucker for slapstick humor." — Paul Watling, head of story (*Hotel Transylvania 2*, *Smurfs: The Lost Village*), PDF 196.
- **Persichetti on the sequence:** "the wildest apex of what we had created so far in the movie. Of course, we ended up blowing the doors off the whole thing in the third act!" — PDF 199.
- **Bios:**
  - Bob Persichetti: *The Little Prince*, *Puss in Boots*, *Shrek 2* (PDF 192).
  - Christian Hejnal: long-time studio veteran, worked on Sony's first three live-action *Spider-Man* films (PDF 182).
- **Leadership (PDF 203):** executive producers Chris Miller and Phil Lord; directors Peter Ramsey, Bob Persichetti and Rodney Rothman; SPA president Kristine Belson.
- **Origins:** Spider-Man was created by Stan Lee and Steve Ditko in *Amazing Fantasy* (1962). Brian Michael Bendis wrote the book's foreword and co-created Miles (PDF 203–204).
- **Acknowledged crew (PDF 204):** Justin K. Thompson, Kristine Belson, Amy Pascal, Avi Arad, Phil Lord, Chris Miller, Peter Ramsey, Bob Persichetti, Christina Steinberg, Dean Gordon, Paul Watling, Danny Dimian, Josh Beveridge, Shiyoon Kim, Patrick O'Keefe, Miguel Jiron, Marcelo Vignali, Peter Chan, Yashar Kassai, "Zachary Rets" (credited "Zac Retz" on PDF 196), Yuhki Demers, Christian Hejnal, Jeffrey Thompson. ## Visual insights (from looking at the art)

### PDF 176 (book 164): Kingpin sketches (Mark Ackland) and facial expressions (Shiyoon Kim)

- **Ackland body sheet (8 figures).**
  - The whole body is one enormous rounded mass (egg, bean, boulder or "refrigerator" rounded-rectangle).
  - The head is a tiny bump on top, about 1/8–1/10 of the mass height, often sunk into the shoulders.
  - Legs are short and tapering, with small feet. Props: a walking cane.
  - Lines are thick-and-thin brush-pen black with spot-black wedge accents at hems, lapels and buttons.
  - Fills are flat marker greys (#d6d5d4 light, #a5a2a2 mid). One variant is dark grey with a fine speckle/dot texture; one wears a window-pane plaid suit with a white vest.
  - The ground shadow is a small grey ellipse filled with **radial hatch stripes** under the feet.
- **Kim expression panels (4, bottom).**
  - A pale face sits inside a **solid black mass (#010005) that fills most of the panel**. The black's top edge is a big arc, so the body looks like a hood or a mountain.
  - The face is a flat-topped, wide-jowled shape with loose graphite/ink hatching around the eyes, brow and mouth. A white collar V and thin black tie sit at the bottom.
  - Composition: the black shape bleeds off three panel edges, so the character literally eclipses the frame.

### PDF 177 (book 165): 3×3 Kingpin head concepts (Shiyoon Kim)

- **Shape:** the face is an inverted egg or teardrop (narrow bald dome, widest at the jowls/jaw), about 1/4 of the visible body width. It sits in a **pure black silhouette** with a white shirt V and black tie at the base.
- **Line:** thin clean contour, about 2px at 1400px page width. Interior lines are minimal: brows, nose, nasolabial folds, a C-shaped ear, a jowl crease. The scream panel shows a stylised tooth row, as a comb shape.
- **Colour:** mostly flat white faces. Some panels add a **cool coloured light gradient** on the face: lavender-grey #abacb4 at the top shifting to violet #6a5a9a, or teal under-light #90a5a1 to mint #b8d8c8. So there is colour lighting on an otherwise B/W design, with no warm skin tone.
- **Composition:** varied square crops. The black mass often cuts diagonally across a corner, or the white background is only a sliver (panels 1, 7, 8). The head tilts and leans in from the frame edge, which is the "eclipse" idea.

### PDF 178 (book 166): Vanessa (Omar Smith 3D design, Yashar Kassai paint) and colour concepts (Paul Lasaine)

  - Square-neck cap-sleeve pencil dress: purple brocade (#4a2a4a / #7a3a6a), plain black, or dark with red/green/gold floral pattern. Gold cuffs.
  - Soft painted lighting with a light halftone/print grain. Soft grey contact-shadow ellipse.
- **Lasaine, bottom left.**
  - A dark SUV side-on at night, with horizontal speed treatment.
  - A **horizontal band of halftone dots** in the background.
  - Thin whipping light lines in red #d04030, orange and yellow #f0c040 across the car.
  - Smeared light blocks, dry-brush grunge, splatter.
- **Lasaine, bottom right.**
  - Kingpin's giant dark hand and sleeve loom in from the top right as a near-black mass (#1a0f0f).
  - A glowing doorway of warm cream-gold light (#f5d08a → #fff4d0) holds tiny silhouettes of Vanessa and their son (in red).
  - Spray/splatter texture at the vignette edges. Scale contrast: huge dark foreground mass, tiny bright figures.

### PDF 179 (book 167): Kingpin walking down his colonnade (Patrick O'Keefe)

- **Palette:**
  - gold/ochre marble columns, mid #8d703d, shadow #6c553f;
  - highlights and floor light pools #d4b679;
  - floor #b09a64;
  - ceiling lights near-white #fefef9;
  - Kingpin #000 with a pinkish face.
- **Composition:** a letterboxed frame (about 2.4:1) on a black page. One-point perspective down a symmetrical colonnade, eye-level camera. Kingpin is small in the centre, and his black blob is the darkest, simplest shape in a busy warm frame (value focus).
- **Kingpin proportions here:** about 5 heads tall, body width about 0.85× height, tiny pale hands at his sides, a white shirt V and a lapel pin.
- **Lights and reflections:** ceiling downlights are drawn as **concentric flat ellipses** (white core, cream ring, tan ring) with no gradient. Floor light pools repeat the same stepped ellipses. Kingpin's reflection is a long mirrored dark shape in the polished floor.
- **Texture:** each column carries a **vertical strip of fine dot grid (halftone screen) along its shadow side / terminator**. Marble veining is done with dry-brush strokes.
- Light direction: overhead, warm.

### PDF 180–181 (book 168–169): Kingpin's office (Patrick O'Keefe)

- **Palette:**
  - cream-white walls and columns #fcfaee / #f0e6d1;
  - gold mouldings #c8a040 → #e8c860 (a bright yellow edge on the top faces of mouldings);
  - desk and chairs #c8b388 / #e0d0a8;
  - checker floor tiles #d3c09d / #b8a27b;
  - **plum-magenta foliage** #7a2050 / #9a3060;
  - zebra rug in black and white;
  - gold globe with brown continents #6f5839;
  - black desk blotter.
- **Rendering:**
  - Everything is flat cut shapes with hard edges.
  - Marble is scattered semi-transparent flat patches plus little clusters of short parallel hatch strokes.
  - **Columns are shaded with a single vertical row of dark dots running down the terminator line, plus a band of dot grid.** A halftone "strip" replaces a soft gradient.
  - Foliage is drawn as flat dry-brush silhouettes with no outline.
  - Gloss on gold and leather is rendered as crisp flat white or pale-yellow shapes.
- **Art collection (9 thumbnails, PDF 180 top left):** suprematist/constructivist compositions (Malevich/Kandinsky-like). Ochre/mustard quadrilaterals (#b08a20), red squares (#e04a3a), yellow arcs (#e8d040), black bars, thin lines and small circles on white, in gold frames. The bottom row has three darker abstracts: green-black with yellow strokes, mauve with pink blobs, and a brown/cream blocky one. Two of them hang in the office on PDF 181.
- **Story prop:** an ornate gold-framed family portrait over the fireplace (Kingpin, Vanessa in a cream dress with a red rose, son in shirt and striped tie). Red roses, clock and candles on the mantel.
- **Camera:** eye level, three-quarter view across the desk, wide lens.

### PDF 182 (book 170): Miles' invisibility triptych (Dean Gordon)

- **Three versions of the same pose over different backgrounds**, showing the camouflage adapting:
  1. a yellow-orange brick wall with maroon graffiti loops (#f1c166 / #e0a040, loops #7a2a3a);
  2. a dark violet bedroom (#120810) with a record shelf;
  3. a teal-grey room lit cyan (#8aa0b0 / #c0e0f0) with a tube light.
- **The figure is transparent: the background shows through, but the line-work and a fine dot screen stay visible** inside the silhouette.
  - Contours and interior lines, including skeleton-like shin and hand lines, render as bright lines (white or pale cyan #c0e0ff) or as darker lines in the warm version.
  - The interior is a fine halftone grid sampling the background colour.
  - The edges have a light fringe.
- In code: invisibility is **background + (lines + dot screen of the figure)**, and the figure's own colour fill is removed.

### PDF 186–187 (book 174–175): Cubist multiverse (Dean Gordon, "Beautifully hectic art")

- **Structure:** the frame is cut into large **straight-edged triangular and quadrilateral shards**. Each holds NYC from a different camera (worm's-eye up a tower, bird's-eye down a street, straight elevation, a rotated view) at a different scale. The shards overlap at about 40–60% opacity, so you see through one into the next, and the seams are hard-edged with no feathering.
- **Each shard gets a different render style:**
  - posterised photo (3–4 value levels, windows reduced to blocky pixel marks);
  - flat vector colour;
  - threshold black and white;
  - **thin white outline "wireframe" lines** tracing building edges and crossing shard borders.
- **Palette:**
  - periwinkle/lavender #8a88c0 / #58557e;
  - deep navy/black #04030c / #212556;
  - warm greys #6c6769 / #c0bdbe and cream #e8e0d0;
  - brick brown #6a3a30;
  - sky-blue window accents #80b0e0;
  - a few **acid green** (#48a020) and magenta (#c040a0) shards as punctuation.

## Recipes

1. **No motion blur: crisp speed.** Never blur a moving character. On frames where a limb moves more than about 1/20 of the screen, draw 2–3 **crisp ghost copies** of that limb or head along the path (opacity 100% → 60% → 35%) plus 3–6 **straight white speed-line strokes** trailing behind it. Keep the character on twos (update pose every 2nd frame at 24fps) while the camera and background can move on ones.

2. **Movement tunnel background.** When the camera travels fast, replace the city with:
   - a **pixel mosaic**: quantise the background to square cells about 1.5–2% of frame width, each a flat colour from the local palette (violet #371d66, magenta #c02070, orange #d07040, teal, lime #b0e060);
   - 20–60 **parallel streak bars**, all at the travel angle. Each is a bright white/cyan core line 2–4px wide with a 10–20px soft glow, length 5–25% of frame width.
   - For a dolly toward a vanishing point, make the streaks radial wedges from that point instead (Peter Chan style).

   Snowflakes become short crisp dashes aligned to the motion.

3. **Travelling light keys.** For a chase through N blocks, give each block its own colour key (store and neon light colours cast on the street) and raise overall brightness monotonically from the first block to the last (Gordon: 12–14 keys over 10 blocks, dark → light). Always keep the heroes as the most saturated element, or value-flip them (dark silhouette with magenta-left / cyan-right rims on a bright centre).

4. **Night-snow graveyard palette.**
   - Sky and buildings #14162d → #2a2a60, with grids of small pale-blue lit windows.
   - Ground snow lit **warm pink-magenta #975680 → #c080a0** under streetlamps, with shadows in #29233e.
   - Tombstones as pure black shapes with a 2–4px white snow-cap stroke along the top edge.
   - Trees as black branch silhouettes.
   - Snow as sparse white or cyan-blue (#8ad0e8) circles in 3 sizes, some large and bokeh-soft.
   - Flashlight beams as additive white wedges at about 40% alpha with a linear fade to 0 at the far end.

5. **Kingpin silhouette character.**
   - Body: one pure-black (#000) rounded mass, about as wide as it is tall (8 ft × 7 ft), about 5 heads tall.
   - Put a small pale inverted-egg face (narrow dome, wide jowls) near the top, a white shirt V with a black tie under it, and two small pale hands at the sides. No interior detail in the black.
   - Pose and animate the **silhouette outline** directly (a free-form shape rig), not an anatomical skeleton.
   - Frame him so the black mass bleeds off the frame edges and eclipses at least 50% of the shot.
   - Optionally light the face with a cool gradient (lavender #abacb4 → violet, or teal #90a5a1) instead of skin tones.

6. **Halftone strip shading on cylinders (O'Keefe interiors).** Shade columns and curved surfaces with a flat base colour plus **one vertical band of a small dot grid (dot pitch about 6–8px at 1400px) running along the terminator**, plus a single row of dark dots on the terminator line itself. No gradient. Draw light pools and ceiling fixtures as **3 stepped concentric flat ellipses** (white core, cream, tan).

7. **Glitch = multi-camera shards.** Render the same scene from 3–7 cameras (different angle, FOV and scale). Give each a different "render script" (posterise to 3 levels, threshold B/W, flat vector, white wireframe edges, thermal-map remap, duotone). Composite them into **hard-edged triangular or quadrilateral shards** at 40–60% opacity overlapping each other. To resolve the glitch, animate the shard polygons and camera transforms converging to one view. Palette: periwinkle #8a88c0, navy #212556, greys, with acid-green #48a020 and magenta #c040a0 accent shards.

8. **Dimensional-quake plume.** On a calm, neutral plate (overcast #e7e9de sky, tan brick):
   - emit a vertical column of **square pixel blocks** (indigo #3a2a8a / violet #5a3aa0 with red flecks), random sizes 0.5–2% of frame width, density falling off with height;
   - add a pale cyan-white bloom (#d0e8ff) at ground level;
   - **row-shift** horizontal bands of the neighbouring buildings by random offsets (horizontal smear strips);
   - for "worlds colliding", clone the same prop (hydrant, cone, sign, traffic light) 20–50 times with random colour variants and rotations into one cluster, with its edges dissolving into blue-violet pixel blocks.

9. **Portal burst.** Put a vanishing point at bottom centre. Fill 30–60 wedge rays, alternating flat colour and **dot-halftone rays** (dot rows aligned to the ray, dot size scaling with distance), in coral #f68abd, peach #fdb178, yellow #e7b630, violet #76479e, sage #80a975 and sky blue. Overlay thin white looping scribble lines and a few spray-paint splatter patches. Keep it high-key, never a dark tunnel.

10. **Portal interior / misregistration collage.**
    - Mint/celadon base #d8eee0.
    - Repeat the tumbling figure at 3–5 scales and rotations inside overlapping translucent triangle shards.
    - Draw soft blob shapes three times: magenta copy offset up-left, violet/cyan copy offset down-right by about 0.5% of width.
    - Add 45° halftone dot grids in some shards and a ceiling band of black ink blobs slashed by white scratch lines.
    - Ink the figures with scratchy black brush over flat pink/red.

11. **Venom Strike.** Generate branching jagged polylines that **follow the character's silhouette and limb axes** (sample points along the contour and jitter them). Stroke each bolt 3 times: a wide cyan glow (#8ad0ff, blurred, additive), a mid blue, and a 1–2px white core. On impact, add a radial starburst of 10–20 long white spikes with a blue halo. Keep the halftone/print pass over it.

12. **Invisibility (cuttlefish camouflage).** Drop the character's colour fill. Show the background through the silhouette, then draw on top:
    - the character's ink lines in a light colour (white or pale cyan #c0e0ff on dark backgrounds, darker on light ones);
    - a fine dot screen inside the silhouette, sampling the background colour;
    - a 1–2px light fringe on the silhouette edge.

    Make it view-independent: composite in screen space, so it works from any angle.

13. **Spider-sense marks.** Draw 2–3 short stacked horizontal zigzag strokes (5–7 peaks, orange-red #ff5040, 3–4px) floating next to the head and on the shoulder. Split the background behind the hero into two flat fields, hot pink #ee3c79 and deep blue #211c71, with a spray-texture seam.

14. **Faceted night character lighting (Chan / Gordon).** Shade characters in flat facets:
    - key hot pink #ef519f from the front-left, shadow indigo #111130;
    - **a thin crisp cyan rim (#a0d8f0)** on the back edges, 2–3px, no blur;
    - for daytime or neutral scenes, a warm copper-brown key on the black suit (#a0704a) with white slivers for the lenses.

    Webs are thin wobbly white 2D lines that can loop into spiral lassos around the wrist. Hand pose: the "thwip" (middle fingers folded).

15. **Sketch-style off-register fill (Iglesias).** Draw the scratchy contour in black, then fill the silhouette with flat colour **inset or offset by 3–6px from the line** so white slivers show between line and fill. Add one flat light-grey cast shadow (#c5bcbc) offset diagonally onto the wall. Apply keylines on city views the same way: thin red (#e04848) or black outlines offset from building edges (PDF 210).
