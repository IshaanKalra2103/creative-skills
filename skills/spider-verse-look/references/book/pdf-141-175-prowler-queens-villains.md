## Text insights

### Visual philosophy / comic-book language

- **Light and darkness are pushed further than in other CG films.** Deep, graphic darks and comic-book-style lighting are named as a range extension that the style made possible. (Dean Gordon, Art Director, PDF 144) "We have been able to play with light and darkness in ways that haven't been done in CG-animated movies before… especially when it comes to creating dark and graphic scenes with comic book-style lighting."
- **Take out CG detail and render it flat.** The Prowler, in particular, was built by removing CG elements and rendering them flat, which makes him "much more graphic". The rule: fewer rendered gradients and more flat graphic planes, especially on villains. (Bob Persichetti, Director, PDF 150) "We have taken out a lot of CG elements and rendered them in a flat way, so we can get a much more graphic representation."
- **A radio voice gets a comic device.** In the Noir gag strip, the old-time-radio broadcast uses a spiky, electric speech balloon with zig-zag "sound" tails coming from a drawn radio set. Noir's other balloons are hand-lettered "quotes from the times": "A day that will live in infamy!" or "Nothing to fear but fear itself!", and "It was the best of times, it was the…". His challenge to Miles is "C'mon! Put up your dukes!" (Vi-Dieu Nguyen sketches, PDF 141) The margin note reads "Spider Noir's voice could sound like old-timey radio."
- **Selective colour in action thumbnails.** In the fight sketches the heroes are the only coloured elements, in red, blue and pink for Gwen. Villains and sets stay in grey wash. SFX lettering such as "POW" is also red, with outline star-bursts. (Iglesias, PDF 160–162, observed from captions and art)
- **Multiple exposure shows a motion path.** One drawing shows Miles four times, numbered 1–2–3–4 in red, flipping over a heavyset villain. This is the comic convention of a single panel holding a whole action. (Iglesias, PDF 162)
- **Storyboards split a wide frame into simultaneous slices.** Rob Porter's boards cut a single 2.4:1 frame into 3–4 vertical sub-panels, so that each Spider-person's reaction appears side by side (Peter B., Ham, Noir, Gwen, Peni). Spider-sense is drawn as short wavy tick-strokes radiating around the heads. A whip-pan is drawn as a panel of pure horizontal streaks. (Rob Porter, PDF 163)

### Colour & light

- **Deep darks read as graphic shapes, not murk.** (Dean Gordon, PDF 144; see above.)
- **Lighting keys give villains a colour code.** The Prowler's palette is purple, navy and black with a single magenta accent. Early colour concepts tried acid lime-green with a hot-pink cape (Iglesias, PDF 149). The storyboards still show him green and purple (Rob Porter, PDF 163).
- **Tombstone is desaturated to match Noir.** Thompson frames him as Noir's 1930s underworld counterpart (PDF 166). The final paint is almost entirely grey, lavender-grey and black, so the character already reads as a black-and-white Noir foil.
- **Kingpin is drawn as a void.** On PDF 172–175 his body is flat black, with only a halftone rim to separate it from the background (visual evidence, and the chapter text continuing on PDF 176: "like this giant black hole… swallows up everything").

### Character design

#### Spider-Noir (continued from the previous chapter)
- His voice should sound like old-time radio, and his dialogue should be period quotes: Dickens, FDR's "infamy" speech and "nothing to fear but fear itself". (Vi-Dieu Nguyen, PDF 141)
- In the sketches he is a fedora and trench coat drawn in loose diagonal hatching. His goggles are two white circles on a black mask, and he is noticeably taller than Miles.

#### Miles
- **Theme.** "Miles, like every incarnation of Spider-Man, is someone whose nose is pressed against the window, wanting to be part of something that they think they're not part of. That's a universal feeling for all of us." (Amy Pascal, Producer, PDF 143)
- A lighting key recreates "an intimidating moment for Miles": standing in front of Peter Parker's glass suit case in the secret lair. (Yuhki Demers, PDF 142)

#### Alternate Spidey suits (PDF 143, Yuhki Demers, "A variety of spidey suits")
Twelve orthographic front views, all on one lean Spider-Man body (about 8 heads tall, narrow waist, long legs), so that only the costume varies:
1. All-black stealth suit.
2. Navy suit with bronze ribbed arm and thigh bands.
3. Blue/red suit with a large white spider emblem, in the style of the "Advanced" game suit.
4. Red/gold Iron-Spider style.
5. Dark navy with thin blue piping.
6. Plain red suit with a small black spider.
7. Classic blue/red.
8. Black with neon-green circuit lines.
9. Black with yellow circuit lines and a yellow spider.
10. Silver/chrome.
11. Blue top with black legs.
12. Classic suit with a red high-collar cape.

**Design rule:** keep one silhouette and change only colour blocking and line motifs.

#### Prowler / Uncle Aaron
- **Role.** "A tall, lanky, ultra-fit, trained assassin who is one of the Kingpin's main henchmen." The mid-film reveal is that he is Uncle Aaron. The designers mixed the original comics Prowler costume with the Miles Morales-comics version, then "put their own interpretation on it." (Book text, PDF 148)
- **Gear.** "We updated it and personalized the design to fit who we thought Uncle Aaron was… He is kind of the low-fi Iron Man." (Bob Persichetti, Director, PDF 150) The kit, in Persichetti's words:
  - "steel claws that are like gloves, which have power punches in them";
  - "pneumatic boots that give him the ability to jump really far, almost fly";
  - a "giant towel-like glider cape that almost seems comedic, but on him it looks quite gritty";
  - "one of my favorite designs in the film."
- **Motorcycle.** His bike is a "highly-modified café racer… slick, light-weight, and minimalistic". He built it himself because he is a "clever gear-head". (Book text, PDF 153) Justin K. Thompson, Production Designer, adds: "Prowler is a brilliant cat burglar who is very good at putting things together… Just like his car, boots, and other gadgets, he put this racer together using found parts. It's functional and super cool." The bike was 3D-designed by Vaughan Ling and painted by Wendell Dalit.
- **Credits for the final look (PDF 148):** 3D design by Omar Smith, paint by Yashar Kassai and Justin K. Thompson. Tony Siruno's turnarounds "get closer to the final look" (PDF 149). Iglesias did the colour concepts and development. Bob Persichetti did the story art. The Iglesias sketch sheet is annotated "A little bit of chaos" (PDF 150).

#### Aunt May
- **Personality.** "This is certainly not your father's Aunt May." She echoes "popular mature actresses like Lily Tomlin and Jane Fonda", and the team calls her "bad-ass" and "feisty". (Book text, PDF 158)
- **References and balance.** "Just as our Peter Parker is different from the live-action versions of Spider-Man, our Aunt May is also quite an original in this universe. She can easily handle herself as well as the other characters from the other Spider-Verse worlds. We looked at a lot of feisty older actresses, especially Lily Tomlin as we see her in the Netflix sitcom *Grace and Frankie*. But along with the toughness, she also has this natural warmth and support for Miles… She can't be too tough without showing her caring side as well." (Shiyoon Kim, Character Designer, PDF 158)
- **Credits.** 2D design by Shiyoon Kim, 3D by Omar Smith, paint by Wendell Dalit. Facial-expression development by Shiyoon Kim. Full-colour costume concepts by Jesús Alonso Iglesias (PDF 158–159).
- **Expression sheet (PDF 158).** Nine heads. Two carry speech balloons, "...Peter..." and a second, probably "...Miles?". The range runs from grief and suspicion to a wry, closed-eye smile and a laugh. The note is the "balance" Kim describes.

#### Scorpion
- "Two of the original villains of the Spider-Man universe (Scorpion and Tombstone) have small roles in the movie as they assist Kingpin." The designers "kept the basic physical characteristics of the duo, but added a little bit of spin to match the rest of the colorful heroes and villains." (Book text, PDF 164)
- "For Scorpion, we meched him a little more. We gave him an incredible tail and mechanical arms, and his legs go from bipedal to quadruped when he's fighting." (Bob Persichetti, Director, PDF 164)
- **Credits.** Final 2D design by Shiyoon Kim, 3D design by Taehyun (Terry) Park, paint by Wendell Dalit (PDF 165). Iglesias did the concepts (PDF 164).

#### Tombstone
- A "big Harlem-born thug who serves as Fisk's right-hand man." (Book text, PDF 166)
- "Tombstone has always been a dangerous hit man who has fought Spider-Man many times… It was fun to add Tombstone to the movie since we knew fans would recognize him from the comics. He looks like this giant zombie, and he gets into a hand-to-hand fight with Spider Noir. He winds up being the perfect counterpart to Noir—a 1930s Spider-Man going against an underworld gangster inspired by the same era." (Justin K. Thompson, Production Designer, PDF 166)
- **Credits.** 2D design by Shiyoon Kim, paint by Wendell Dalit. Action concepts by Iglesias. "Stylized concept sketches" by Jim Mahfood and Shiyoon Kim (PDF 166–167).

#### Kingpin (Wilson Fisk)
- "First introduced by Stan Lee and John Romita Sr. in 1967, Kingpin is one of the most powerful and ferocious crime lords in the Marvel Universe." (Book text, PDF 172)
- "He is a smart crime-lord and a savvy businessman. He puts a lot of money into the city, and that's how he throws people off, and gets all the city officials on his side." (Justin K. Thompson, PDF 172)
- **Scale.** "In the comics, Kingpin was always portrayed as this gargantuan character, so for our movie, I wanted him to be at least eight feet tall, seven feet wide." (Justin K. Thompson, PDF 172) So his bounding box is roughly square, with an aspect of about 1.14 : 1.
- The quote continues on PDF 176, in the next chunk: "Since he is responsible for opening this portal to another dimension, I thought it would be cool to portray him like this giant black hole that moves through space and time and swallows up everything."
- **Credits.** 2D design by Shiyoon Kim, 3D by Omar Smith, paint by Wendell Dalit. Silhouette sketches by Iglesias. The following spread is by Craig Mullins (PDF 172–175).

### Environment design

#### Peter's secret lair and suit vault (PDF 142)
- Patrick O'Keefe's visual-development paintings show a lab platform hanging in a black void. It is held up by giant pale-grey, organic, branching struts that look like a web or tree roots, with thin web cables strung everywhere.
- The floor is red with a large black spider emblem. A curved line of about 14 lit glass suit cases runs along it, and there is clutter: computer monitors, a chemistry set, a mug, an Eames-style lounge chair.

#### New York rooftops and alleys (Prowler chase; PDF 144–147)
- Alberto Mielgo's art for the "On the Prowl" chapter shows Chinatown and Lower-Manhattan-style rooftops (signs with Chinese characters), water towers, verdigris domes, fire escapes, AC units, TV antennae and satellite dishes.
- There are large painted billboards, one with the word "BACK".
- The alley spread carries believable signage: "Hotel THE CHEAP" as a vertical blade sign, "Office Space for Lease", "Gran & Sons 212 841…", "CLINMAN ARCHFIELD HOUSEHOFF" and a big "212 841…" phone-number banner.

#### Aunt May's house (PDF 154–157)
- "Aunt May's home had to be as warm and inviting as the character herself." (Book text, PDF 154)
- "We wanted it to feel like anyone's grandma or aunt could live there. I like to imagine it smells like homemade chocolate-chip cookies with a hint of mothballs!" (Yuhki Demers, artist, PDF 154)
- **Storytelling props (observed):**
  - Bedroom: a crucifix over the bed, a wedding photo and a couple's portrait, angel figurines on the dresser, and an old portrait of a man in military uniform (Uncle Ben).
  - Living room: a folded U.S. flag in a triangular case on the mantel.
  - Kitchen: yellow floral wallpaper over a tile grid, a clock, a retro fridge covered in photos.
  - Throughout: a CRT TV and a box fan. (Yuhki Demers paintings, PDF 156–157)
- Peter Chan painted the neighbourhood (PDF 154–155; credited as "PREVIOUS SPREAD" on PDF 157). His pieces include:
  - a backyard in autumn with a shed, a birdbath, a watering can and a fallen-leaf carpet;
  - a pastel row of detached Queens houses in lime, blue-grey and mustard with a pink neighbour, under a tangle of overhead power lines;
  - a sidewalk memorial ("RIP" signs, flowers and candles) under a telephone pole, next to a red hydrant and a USPS mailbox;
  - Manhattan towers faintly visible in the haze behind.

#### Queens (PDF 168–171)
- **Purpose.** Queens, "the key location for Aunt May's house, allowed the artists to create a comfortable contrast to the madness of New York City and the high-octane drama of the film's action sequences." (Book text, PDF 168)
- **Reference habit.** Patrick O'Keefe photographs mailboxes, traffic lights and fire hydrants for reference: "I can't get enough of stickers on business doors letting me know about the hours of operation and which credit cards are accepted." (Patrick O'Keefe, artist, PDF 168)
- **Imperfection rule.** "Designing the main strip of Queens was a chance to create something iconic, nostalgic, and utterly ordinary. The trick is to recognize and celebrate the inconsistencies: Nothing is really parallel or truly lines up. Some buildings have poor foundations and sink a little to one side. Some façades have paint peeling or doors that don't match. It's the variety of choices that make this world so beautiful." (Patrick O'Keefe, PDF 168)
- **Artist in-jokes.** "The artists enjoyed adding their own personal touches to the artwork—note the name of the shop in the top left": the shop is "JUSTIN K. THOMPSON P.O.S." (Caption, PDF 170)
- **Shop list (PDF 170–171):**
  - JUSTIN K. THOMPSON P.O.S.; JON'S; SICK SUP LIES (letter missing); OK AUTO PARTS; ICE CREAM SHOP;
  - WINE & LIQOUR (sic); FISH N CHIPS; FOOT + SKIN CARE; KING'S DISCOUNT AUTO PARTS;
  - FLOWERS; COLLECTIBLES; HEINRICH AIR CONDITIONERS 24 hrs; CLEANERS; THARDWARE SUPPLIES (Tools + Paint + Electrical + Plumbing);
  - POD'S BARBER SHOP; LAUNDROMAT (Dry Cleaning, Sewing, Hems); WOOD FLOOR INC.; UNITED STATES MAIL SERVICE (Westside Station, Queens, New York); AGENCY.
  - The captions call these shop fronts "detail and authenticity" (PDF 171).
- **The alley where Aaron dies (PDF 168–169; painting by Patrick O'Keefe).** A dark brick alley holds a fire escape, a dumpster marked "GBE", stencilled "please do not block" on the wall, a box numbered "2", and a roll-down shutter with graffiti. The Prowler lies in a puddle with his mask off. Miles leans over him while a police light blooms at the alley mouth.

#### Kingpin's world (PDF 174–175; Craig Mullins)
- A baroque, gilded palace interior: moulded ceilings, a crystal chandelier, gold picture frames, a mantel with urns, rococo chairs, a statue, a patterned red carpet. It is the wealthy "real-estate magnate" backdrop.

### FX
- **Prowler's pneumatic boots leave a trail.** A thin, glowing pink-magenta arc runs behind the boot, with a hot dot at the heel emitter. It traces his leap trajectory across the rooftops. (Mielgo art, PDF 145; Persichetti describes the boots on PDF 150)
- **Glass bursts in the Prowler window-entry storyboards** are drawn as sharp black triangles radiating outward (Rob Porter, PDF 163).
- **Doc Ock's tentacles** in the sketches are pale tubes marked with a dotted segment pattern, swinging in wide arcs. Loose grey smoke shapes mark impacts. (Iglesias, PDF 161)

### Pipeline & tech
- **The standard design pipeline** runs from a 2D design (Shiyoon Kim) to 3D design/sculpt (Omar Smith, Terry Park, Vaughan Ling) to a paintover on the 3D render (Wendell Dalit, Yashar Kassai, Justin K. Thompson). This is repeated for the Prowler, Aunt May, Scorpion, Tombstone, Kingpin and the motorcycle. The "paint" stage is where halftone, flat planes and ink accents get added over the 3D. (Captions, PDF 148, 153, 158, 165, 166, 172)
- **Lighting keys** are separate paintings made per moment: Yuhki Demers for the lair (PDF 142) and Zac Retz for Miles and the police officer in the alley (PDF 169).

### Story & production
- The Prowler is revealed as Uncle Aaron in the first half of the film (PDF 148). He dies in a Queens-adjacent alley. The police arrive and Miles raises his hands (Zac Retz lighting keys, PDF 169).
- The fight at Aunt May's house brings Doc Ock (female), Scorpion, Tombstone and the Prowler against Miles, Peter B., Gwen, Noir, Ham and Peni (Iglesias, Rob Porter, PDF 160–163).
- Tombstone fights Spider-Noir hand to hand (PDF 166).
- Kingpin is responsible for opening the portal (continues on PDF 176).

## Visual insights (from looking at the art)

**PDF 141 (Vi-Dieu Nguyen, comic-style sketches).**
- **Palette:** black felt-tip on white. The only colour is red, on Miles's web-pattern hoodie mask.
- **Technique:** diagonal parallel hatching for Noir's coat shadows (about 45°, strokes 3–6 px apart at page scale). Hand-lettered all-caps captions. A spiky "broadcast" balloon with zig-zag emanation ticks.
- **Proportions:** Noir is about 1.3× Miles's height in gag scale. His goggles are two plain circles with white fills.

**PDF 142 (Yuhki Demers lighting keys; Patrick O'Keefe paintings).**
- **Lighting keys:**
  - Cold navy/indigo environment (#1c2250 to #3a4a9a), with the red suit (#b02838) glowing inside a white-blue lit case (#c8d0f0).
  - Fluorescent strip lights appear as flat white parallelograms.
  - The side cases are defocused with red/yellow ghost doubling, like a smeared chromatic echo rather than a clean Gaussian blur.
  - The mask lenses are pale yellow-white with dark rims.
  - In the second key, Miles is a pure black back-silhouette placed between lit cases, for contrast and scale.
- **O'Keefe lair:**
  - Pure-black void (#050508 to #21222b).
  - Huge pale lavender-grey struts (#c4c1d3) with soft gradients.
  - Glowing white cases (#e8e8f2) holding small coloured suit silhouettes.
  - A glossy red floor (#a01e2c to #895762) with a black spider emblem and mirror reflections of the cases.
  - Thin white web lines at random angles across the whole frame.
  - The camera looks slightly down, wide-angle, from a high vantage point.

**PDF 143 (Yuhki Demers suits).**
- Flat, even front lighting with a slight top-left key. Muscles are defined by 2–3 value steps of the suit colour, with no outlines.
- Suit reds run #d8202e to #e63c46, blues #2a3a9a to #3050c0, navies #141830, golds #d8a030, neon green #70e030, yellow #e8c820 and silver #c8c8d0.
- All figures are about 8.5 heads tall, a heroic elongated canon.

**PDF 144 (Alberto Mielgo rooftops, left page).**
- **Palette:**
  - Hazy pale green-grey sky and distant towers (#c6cdbd, #d5daca).
  - Mid-distance brick projects (#8c6959) with a faint greenish haze.
  - Mid-ground ochre/khaki walls (#bbb070).
  - Foreground maroon brick (#7a3448).
  - Slate-navy rooftops (#3a4470 to #444857).
  - Accent billboards in pink and lilac (#cc859c, #d9abad).
  - Tiny lime-yellow squares (#c8e04a) as glitchy highlights.
- **Light:** flat, overcast, near-shadowless daylight. Form comes from colour temperature and planes, not cast shadows.
- **Shape:** everything is stacked rectangles with ruler-straight edges, but window grids are irregular. Brick is rendered as clusters of slightly darker rectangles, not a regular pattern.
- **Glitch detail:** small rectangles are misregistered, offset by a few pixels (blue and purple rectangles overlapping a building edge), plus scattered isolated magenta and lime pixels.
- **Atmosphere:** strong perspective haze. Contrast drops to near zero by the third depth layer, and value rises toward #e0e6d0.

**PDF 145 (Alberto Mielgo; the Prowler leaping over Chinatown rooftops).**
- The camera is high, with a street canyon receding to the left, full of traffic and pedestrians.
- The Prowler is placed in the upper-left third, in profile, mid-leap. His cape is a jagged, angular flat shape in violet (#5a3aa0 lit, #3a2870 shadow).
- **Details on the Prowler:** a yellow chest patch (#c8b030); halftone and stripe detail on his legs; pink-magenta boot soles (#e060a0) that each emit a thin glowing magenta trail (#ff6f9f at about 60% opacity) arcing across the entire frame.
- **Background:** the high-rise housing block is a grid of pale balconies. Its colour-dot windows are pink, lime and lavender. The same haze logic as PDF 144 applies.

**PDF 146–147 (Alberto Mielgo; Prowler alley spread).**
- **Composition:** a vertical street canyon. The Prowler, very small, stands at the gutter (the centre seam), back to the camera, and looks toward a pale skyline with a Chrysler-like spire. Strong converging verticals.
- **Palette:**
  - Left wall in near-black purple shadow (#0d0c11 to #2a2340).
  - Sunlit fire escapes as flat yellow-green shapes (#b2b27b to #c8d070).
  - Distant skyline in pale blue-grey (#c1cbd5).
  - Right page: warm orange-lit brick (#cc795f to #e08a5a) against an elevated structure in deep navy and black (#0a1030).
  - Rust-red signs (#b8332a) and a traffic light (red #b62e43, yellow #f5e373).
- **Prowler close-up:** the hood is one flat violet shape (#4a2a8c) with a single white highlight slit. The lit side of the shoulder armour (yellow-green, #b9a21c) carries a fine halftone dot texture and 3–4 diagonal ink hatch strokes. Everything else is flat.
- **Stepped silhouettes:** building edges are rendered as stepped, pixel-staircase silhouettes (Mielgo's "low-res" look). Signage text is set in clean sans-serif (Helvetica-like), in perspective.

**PDF 148 (Prowler final; 3D by Omar Smith, paint by Yashar Kassai and Justin K. Thompson).**
- **Proportions:** about 9–10 heads tall including the collar spikes, with a very small hooded head, long legs and narrow shoulders.
- **Silhouette:**
  - Three sharp collar spikes around the hood, like a crown or bat ears.
  - A cape with a serrated zig-zag hem that reaches the ankles.
  - Chunky mechanical gauntlets: segmented black cylinders with magenta vent stripes (#c040a0) and gold studs on the knuckles.
  - Heavy platform boots with a magenta ring.
- **Colours:** body navy (#141336) with purple panels (#4a2e69 to #5a3a9a). The chest emblem is a dark stylised "ghost/bat" shape. The mask has black eye patches and small white eye slits.
- **Shading:** a faint woven grid texture on the suit, and flat value planes.
- **Iglesias sketches (same page):** grey-marker action poses with speed lines drawn as straight ruled lines and impact ticks.

**PDF 149 (Prowler colour concepts, story art and turnarounds).**
- **Iglesias colour concepts:** acid lime (#a0d030) body, yellow chest, hot-pink/magenta cape (#d050c0), pale blue bands.
- **Persichetti story art:**
  - Two panels of the Prowler hanging in a window frame as a pure black silhouette.
  - In the second panel, the side panels are filled with dense diagonal white-on-black line hatching (about 30° diagonal), like a speed or texture fill, and the background gains horizontal venetian-blind slats.
  - A strong two-value (black/white) graphic language.
- **Tony Siruno turnarounds:** confident black ink contour with varied weight (thick on the shadow side, thin inside). Solid black cape masses with sawtooth hems. A single flat lavender spot colour (#8a70b0) for gloves, boots and hood shading. No gradients.

**PDF 150 (Iglesias concept art).**
- Brown-sepia line sketches (#6a4030) of Aaron unmasked (bald, goatee) and in costume.
- Big brush-ink drawings mass the shadows into solid black. The cape is one flat black shape, and light areas are left as bare paper.
- Hand lettering reads "A little bit of chaos".

**PDF 151 (Yashar Kassai paint; Prowler leaping).**
- **Cape:** 2 flat values, a shadow indigo (#100b29 to #1f1245) and a lit violet (#4a2a8a to #5a30a0). The top edge is scalloped and stepped.
- **Cape texture:** very fine horizontal hatch lines (about 2 px spacing) in the shadow part. A few thin black ink lines are drawn over the cape to suggest folds; they don't follow the shape edges exactly.
- **Accents:** a hairline magenta rim light on the boot and leg edges (#d040a0). The hood has a pale lavender highlight plane (#b8b8e0) on the key side.
- **Motion lines:** 3–4 thin, slightly tapering grey parallel lines trail the lower foot at about 60°.
- **Colour count:** white background, about 6 colours in total.

**PDF 152 (Iglesias character development).**
- Marker style: loose black brush contour with flat marker fills in olive green (#7a9030), slate grey (#6a7480), lilac cape (#c0b0e0), purple hood (#5a3a90) and a yellow chest (#e8d040).
- Rough white paper gaps are left inside the fills, and there are yellow eye glints.
- A third figure is line-only with cross-hatch shading on the cape.

**PDF 153 (Prowler motorcycle; 3D by Vaughan Ling, paint by Wendell Dalit).**
- **Palette:** violet bodywork (#6a3ab0), lime-green (#83af02) wheel hubs and tank roundel, magenta/pink frame (#c34490), silver-white discs (#d8d8e0), black tyres (#050508), and a lavender-grey cast shadow (#a293bc).
- **Halftone use:**
  - The fairing's shading transition is a visible halftone dot field, with dots getting larger into the shadow.
  - The cast shadow on the ground is a hard-edged shape filled with a regular dot grid (lavender dots on white).
  - The shadowed hub interior is also dotted.
- **Speculars:** flat white shapes and elongated lozenges, not blurred highlights.
- **Details:** race number "77" in a white disc; decals "FRAP", "GONZO GARAGE", "ROYCE", "CE". A drippy, brush-textured edge on the tyre bottoms.

**PDF 154 (Peter Chan; Queens backyard and street).**

## Recipes

1. **Void villain with a halftone rim (Kingpin).**
   - Fill the silhouette pure black (#020205). Draw the head and hands as the only lit forms (skin #dab79c), and place the head inside the silhouette, below the shoulder line.
   - For the rim:
     - Stroke a 1–1.5 px pale-blue edge (#9fb5d6) along the key-light side only.
     - Inside it, stamp a band of navy dots (#23305e) on a 45°-rotated grid (cell about 6 px at 1400 px width). Set dot radius to `r = 0.5 * cell * max(0, 1 - dist/bandWidth)`, with bandWidth about 6% of the figure's width.
   - Use no gradients anywhere else.
   - Proportions: bounding box about 8 : 7 (h : w); head about 1/9 of the height; tiny feet; legs an inverted V under a flat coat hem.

2. **Halftone shadows instead of soft shadows (motorcycle, dresser).**
   - Render the cast shadow as a hard-edged polygon, not a blur, and fill it with a dot screen: lavender-grey dots (#a293bc) on the ground colour, 45° grid, constant dot size.
   - For form shading on glossy parts, blend lit to shadow with a dot screen whose radius grows with the shadow term: `r = cell * 0.5 * smoothstep(0.3, 0.8, 1 - NdotL)`.
   - Make speculars flat white lozenges.

3. **Mielgo-style hazy city layers.**
   - Build 4–6 depth layers of flat rectangles (buildings), with window grids as small darker rectangles at irregular spacing.
   - Blend each layer toward a haze colour (#dfe5cf) by depth: `mix(base, haze, depth^0.7)`.
   - Near layers use maroon, ochre and slate (#7a3448, #bbb070, #3a4470).
   - Scatter 0.5% of tiles as single lime (#c8e04a) or magenta (#d070b8) "glitch" pixels.
   - Offset a few rectangles by 2–4 px to fake misregistration.
   - No cast shadows: overcast, flat light.

4. **Prowler shape kit.**
   - Very tall body (9–10 heads) with a tiny hooded head, three sharp collar spikes framing it, and chunky cylinder gauntlets and boots.
   - The cape is one polygon with a sawtooth hem (5–7 teeth, alternating long and short). Fill it with two flat values, indigo #1a1040 and violet #4a2a8a, split by a hard terminator.
   - Draw 2–4 free black ink strokes across the cape for folds, deliberately not on the edges.
   - Add a hairline magenta rim (#d040a0) on the back-lit side.
   - Palette: navy #141336, purple #4a2e69, black, one magenta accent, optional yellow-green chest light with dot texture.

5. **Pneumatic-boot jump trail.**
   - On each leap, emit a thin (2–3 px) additive magenta line (#ff6f9f, alpha 0.6) along the boot's past positions for about 0.5 s, with a bright 6 px dot at the heel.
   - Let it fade by length, not blur, so the trajectory arc reads across the whole frame.

6. **Motion lines for fast limbs.**
   - Behind a moving foot or fist, draw 3–4 thin parallel grey strokes (#555, 1–2 px) that taper at the far end, spaced about 12 px apart and aligned to the velocity.
   - For a hero charge, use red dry-brush streaks instead: rectangles with ragged alpha edges.

7. **Loose, misregistered keylines (Yuhki Demers interiors).**
   - Draw the object as flat fills first.
   - Then draw black contour strokes on only 30–60% of its edges. Offset each stroke by a random 2–6 px and rotate it about 1–3°. Let strokes stop short or overshoot.
   - Add an optional 3 px cream/white halo offset behind one silhouette, like a misprinted underlayer.
   - Floors: vertical smear streaks of the reflected furniture colours.

8. **Queens shop-front generator (O'Keefe's "nothing lines up").**
   - Orthographic elevation, flat light, no shadows.
   - Each building is 2 storeys. Choose a brick colour from #8d6c5f / #a4a090 / #c8b890 and draw mottled rectangles in 3 tones.
   - Rotate the whole building 0.2–0.8° (it "sinks to one side"). Jitter window positions by ±3 px and sizes by ±5%.
   - Draw window glass as 2–3 flat grey reflection blocks.
   - Add AC units, a chain-link roof fence, antennas and dishes.
   - The shop sign band uses a random font (condensed sans, serif or script) with a 10% chance of a missing letter. Use honest local names (laundromat, barber, auto parts, cleaners, hardware).

9. **Peter Chan foliage and neighbourhood.**
   - Tree canopies are 200–600 small squares (4–10 px) in autumn tones (#e8b830, #e07a2a, #a39571, #6a7a30) clustered on a blob mask, with thin black branch lines drawn through them.
   - Houses are flat clapboard planes (lime #cfd070, grey-blue #9aacb0, mustard #c8a840, pink #e0c2b6) with white trim.
   - The distant skyline is pale and covered in a coarse halftone screen that fades into the sky (#e1dbe7).
   - Power lines are black 2 px curves across the top third.

10. **Backlit magenta rim lighting key (Zac Retz).**
    - Put the subject in dark (#241f24 / #2c0b12) against a blown-out pastel background (#dcdce4).
    - Trace the silhouette's outer edge with a 3–6 px hot magenta-red rim (#ff3a6e), computed as `rim = pow(1 - dot(N, V), 3)`. Add it only on the back-light side, so the rim alone describes the figure.
    - Draw police or car lights as concentric ring bokeh (2–3 transparent rings, #f0bcdb to #e04070), not soft glows.

11. **Glowing tech joints (Scorpion).**
    - Fill a lime disc (#9cd23a) with a lighter core.
    - Surround it with a halftone ring of lime dots on a dark base, dot radius decreasing outward over about 1 disc radius.
    - Don't add a Gaussian bloom.

12. **Deliberate aliasing on silhouettes (Mullins, Mielgo).**
    - On one side of a big dark silhouette, quantise the edge to a step grid (8–12 px blocks), so the edge becomes a pixel staircase while the other edges stay smooth.
    - Also use stair-stepped shapes for light shafts on walls (Demers bedroom).

13. **Selective-colour action beat (thumbnail look).**
    - Render the villains and the set in 2–3 greys (#8a8a8a, #b0b0b0, #444).
    - Keep full saturation only on the heroes (#e02030 red, #2040c0 blue, Gwen pink #f0a0c0) and on SFX.
    - Write SFX ("POW") in red block caps with a black outline, 3–5 outline star-bursts and radiating zig-zag strokes.

14. **Multiple-exposure motion path.**
    - For an acrobatic move, draw the character 3–5 times along a curved path in one frame.
    - Optionally number each pose, or fade earlier poses to 40–70% opacity.

15. **Split-frame reaction beat and spider-sense.**
    - Divide a 2.4:1 frame into 3–4 vertical slices with 4 px black gutters, each holding a different character's close-up, to show simultaneous reactions.
    - Spider-sense is 6–10 short wavy strokes (sine squiggles, 1.5 px, black) radiating from around the head.

## Best quotes

1. "We have been able to play with light and darkness in ways that haven't been done in CG-animated movies before. It has really extended the range of what we've been able to put on the screen, especially when it comes to creating dark and graphic scenes with comic book-style lighting." (Dean Gordon, Art Director, PDF 144)
2. "Miles, like every incarnation of Spider-Man, is someone whose nose is pressed against the window, wanting to be part of something that they think they're not part of. That's a universal feeling for all of us." (Amy Pascal, Producer, PDF 143)
3. "He is kind of the low-fi Iron Man… We have taken out a lot of CG elements and rendered them in a flat way, so we can get a much more graphic representation, and he's one of my favorite designs in the film." (Bob Persichetti, Director, on the Prowler, PDF 150)

Some façades have paint peeling or doors that don't match. It's the variety of choices that make this world so beautiful." (Patrick O'Keefe, artist, PDF 168)

8. "He winds up being the perfect counterpart to Noir—a 1930s Spider-Man going against an underworld gangster inspired by the same era." (Justin K. Thompson, on Tombstone, PDF 166)
