# Video insights — "Making Spider-Verse Was Absolute Chaos" (PM. Films, 24:22, uploaded 2026-08-30)

Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5gmMKUekX3Q · transcript: `transcript.txt` (Whisper large-v3-turbo, names corrected). Timestamps are mm:ss.

## Technique (the part that matters for building the look)

- **Smooth animation looked wrong.** Early Imageworks tests (Spider-Man leaping off a building and swinging; an emotional close-up of Gwen) showed 24 unique drawings a second "looked way too perfect and too clean". (06:03)
- **The emotion lives in the ink lines, not the anatomy.** CG faces weren't emotional enough; in the comics the feeling sits in ink lines "that weren't necessarily attached to any underlying anatomy at all". So lines were drawn directly onto faces and handed to animators as part of the rig. (06:15)
- **No motion blur. Animate on twos.** Motion blur was removed entirely; most animation is on twos (12 drawings/s). Smears and stretched geometry do the job motion blur normally does. (06:40)
- **No smooth gradients.** Gradients were replaced with dot patterns (halftone) and cross-hatching. (06:50)
- **Focus = misregistration.** Anything out of focus is faked by misaligning the colour layers, "like a comic that came off the press slightly wrong". (06:55)
- **"If it's not broken, break it."** Lord & Miller asked VFX supervisor Danny Dimian for "something confusing or brand new, or ideally both"; his team made that their motto. (07:05)
- **Twos break CG pipelines.** Cloth, hair, lighting and FX all assume a new position every frame. Imageworks generated a hidden in-between frame the audience never sees, just to keep cloth simulation stable. (07:20)
- **Hand-drawn explosions.** Simulated explosions "looked dead", so 2D artists hand-drew explosion cycles and the FX were rebuilt from those drawings. (07:35)
- **Broken models.** Buildings were faked: window panes float slightly in front of the walls they belong to — "an old illustrator's trick done on purpose". (07:45)
- **Paint yourself into a corner.** Bob Persichetti summed up the philosophy as "deliberately painting yourself into a corner, and then finding a way out." (07:55)
- **Sculptable rigs.** Persichetti (who worked on Disney's Tarzan) had every character rig made sculptable so an animator could push a pose off-model for a frame or two, exactly like comics do. (08:45)
- **Frame rate as character arc.** Most of the film is on twos, but not evenly: in the forest sequence Peter B. Parker is on ones (smooth, in control) while Miles is on twos (choppier, less controlled). The gap closes as Miles improves; by the Leap of Faith he's on ones too. (16:30)
- **ML-predicted ink lines.** Face lines drawn over CG had to move correctly every frame. Imageworks trained a machine-learning system on artists' drawings to predict where lines go on the next frame; artists corrected the output. Became part of Sony's patents. (17:10)
- **Panels multiply the work.** Three panels on screen for three seconds = nine seconds of animation. The film has ~2,800 shots, 2–3× a typical animated feature. (18:20)
- **One sequence, many rulebooks.** In the collider finale six spider-people enter with different designs and visual rules; an animation co-director compared keeping the styles apart to "making five movies at once". (18:40)
- **Music from Miles' headphones.** Daniel Pemberton's score; the soundtrack was built from music that could plausibly come out of Miles' headphones ("Sunflower", Post Malone & Swae Lee, released Oct 2018). (19:15)

## Visual development

- **Alberto Mielgo** (Spanish painter/art director, Emmy for *Tron: Uprising*) was hired as production designer in 2015 before there was a director. He spent months alone on an empty floor in Culver City reading Spider-Man comics and painting; that footage is the earliest of the film. (04:30)
- Sony gave him a Spider-Man costume for reference and flew him to New York with a location expert who got him onto off-limits rooftops "so he could stand where Spider-Man would stand" and shoot reference photos. (05:00)
- Out of this came an animation test; only four scenes were completed. (05:45)
- The Miles logo Mielgo spray-painted on cardboard is still on the merchandise. He left mid-production (reportedly wanting to push the graphic style further than the team would go) and is credited as visual consultant. (12:30)
- Two animators spent about a year on **10 seconds** of footage just to nail the target look. Animators averaged one foot of film (16 frames, ~⅔ s) per person per week; animation took ~4× longer than normal. (10:40)
- The 2017 CCXP teaser's building-drop shot (Miles falls off a skyscraper, the camera rotates, the city flips) was the only finished animation that existed at the time. (14:30)

## Production facts

- Origin: 2014 Sony hack leaked emails between Amy Pascal and the studio president about an animated comedy from Phil Lord & Chris Miller. After the Marvel deal (Feb 2015) live-action Peter Parker went to the MCU, so the animated film was the "leftover project". (01:00)
- Lord & Miller's two conditions: the lead is Miles, and the story builds on the 2014 *Spider-Verse* comics event. (03:10)
- Announced CinemaCon 2015 for 20 July 2018; moved to Dec 2018, then pulled forward to 14 Dec 2018 despite animating 4× slower. (03:40, 13:35)
- Directors: Bob Persichetti (joined Dec 2015; owned animation), Peter Ramsey (*Rise of the Guardians*; boarded act three), Rodney Rothman (writer who became third director; lived in the edit generating alt dialogue). (09:10, 15:00)
- First animatic ran over two hours. Cut material: a Miles/Gwen romance, a subplot where Miles learns to be Spider-Man from a DVD commentary by James Cameron and Tom Cruise; Tobey Maguire was considered for Peter B. The whole third act was redone less than a year before release. (15:40)
- Ganke's role was cut back to a silent roommate so he wouldn't read as a copy of *Homecoming*'s Ned Leeds. A post-credit cameo of Maguire/Garfield/Holland was scrapped. (13:55)
- Crew grew from ~60 animators to 142 (Aug 2018) to ~172–177 — Imageworks' largest ever. Persichetti reviewed animation ~7 hours a day. (17:55)
- Imageworks supervisor Danny Dimian appears as a nervous guest at Kingpin's dinner. (19:00)
- Opened 14 Dec 2018: $35M weekend (biggest December animated opening), A+ CinemaScore, third weekend bigger than second, ~$190M domestic / ~$400M worldwide on a $90M budget. Won Best Animated Feature over *Incredibles 2* — first non-Disney/Pixar winner since *Rango* (2011); Peter Ramsey first Black director to win the category. (20:30)
- Legacy: *Puss in Boots: The Last Wish* and *TMNT: Mutant Mayhem* dropped physically-smooth CG; Mutant Mayhem's director credited Spider-Verse with proving a finished film could keep the roughness of its concept art. (22:10)
